Trump Backed a United Ireland Yesterday. Today He's Due to Hand Over the Irish Open Trophy.

By Golf Raw Editorial Staff Published 13 Sep 2026 Updated 13 Sep 2026, 14:00 IST 4 min read

Donald Trump and Taoiseach Micheál Martin are due to meet again this afternoon at Trump International Golf Links in Doonbeg, County Clare, a day after Trump told reporters in Dublin that Irish reunification was inevitable and “may as well happen now”, drawing pushback from British political leaders including Prime Minister Andy Burnham. RTÉ reports Martin is attending the Irish Open's final day with Minister Patrick O'Donovan and Minister of State Charlie McConalogue, and that the three are expected to feature when Trump awards the trophy around teatime. Shane Lowry began the round four shots clear on 16-under.

The DP World Tour confirmed this venue in September 2025. Twelve months of notice, which is the part almost nobody is mentioning today.

Golf Raw was not in Dublin or at Doonbeg. Quotes and sequence come from RTÉ, The Journal's live blog, The Irish Times, CNN, MS NOW, AFP, the Clare Echo and Golf Monthly, all published on 12 or 13 September 2026.

What Trump actually said

Two sentences and a gesture.

Per MS NOW, Trump said: “The way I look at it, it's going to happen eventually. It may as well happen now. You got the right man over here to get it moving,” motioning toward Martin as he said it. CNN reports he also said: “But I will tell you, I'd love to see it unified.”

The remarks came at a roughly 20-minute press conference at Farmleigh House, the last item on a Dublin schedule that The Journal's live blog records as President Catherine Connolly at Áras an Uachtaráin, a bilateral with Martin at Farmleigh, and a speech at the US Ambassador's Deerfield residence.

The Irish government did not move. Martin's spokesperson told The Irish Times: “No, our Good Friday Agreement and Programme for Government commitments remain.”

Nothing Martin himself said at that press conference has reached us in a form we can verify, so we're not putting words in his mouth.

Who objected, and who didn't

DUP leader Gavin Robinson, per MS NOW: “Northern Ireland's constitutional future will be decided by the people of Northern Ireland, not by commentary in London, Dublin or Washington.” He added: “As unionists we are not interested in the destruction of our country by joining with the Irish Republic.”

The same report notes Burnham had already put a referendum on Northern Ireland leaving the UK “off the table” last month, before Trump said anything.

Not every Irish reaction was discomfort, which most coverage skips. The Irish Times reports Aontú leader Peadar Tóibín urging the government to use the comment as leverage on Britain over the criteria for a border poll.

Doonbeg was booked a year ago

Here's the payoff. The DP World Tour confirmed Trump International Doonbeg as the 2026 Irish Open venue in September 2025, per BBC Sport, alongside the rest of the schedule.

This is the 71st edition of a tournament first staged in 1927, and Doonbeg's first time hosting. AFP reports Trump has owned the course since 2014.

The Hill reported last month that this is a pattern: in his second term Trump has attended multiple tournaments at courses his company owns, including the Cadillac Championship at Doral in May.

Golf Raw's position, and it's about the institution rather than any individual: the tour signed this contract knowing exactly who owned the golf course and what he does. Anyone at the DP World Tour expressing surprise at today's split screen had a year to see it coming.

Meanwhile, there's a golf tournament

Lowry shot a course-record 62 on Friday, nine birdies and a bogey, then added a 65 on Saturday to reach 16-under, per Golf Monthly. He's four clear of Jacob Skov Olesen and seven clear of Joaquín Niemann. The leaders went off at 2.05pm local in pairs.

Saturday's play stopped for about five minutes when Marine One landed, per the Clare Echo, which also reports Trump watched Lowry on the 18th. AFP has him watching from over that green flanked by his sons Eric and Don Jr, and applauding Rory McIlroy.

Lowry afterward: “I'm going to be leading my home tournament and it would mean the world to knock this one off. So I'm just going to have to put my head down, get back to work whenever my tee time is tomorrow, and give it a run.”

McIlroy shot 74 for 2-over and T50, per Golfmagic, and still said: “I think there's a great buzz around the tournament because Shane's up there and leading. So, yeah, hopefully he keeps it going, that would be great for obviously Irish golf.” Tom McKibbin is next-best Irish on 7-under. Jon Rahm slipped outside the top 30.

What we don't know yet

Whether Trump physically hands over the trophy. Whether Martin, O'Donovan and McConalogue stand beside him. Whether Trump takes more questions at Doonbeg, which RTÉ flags as the source of Government nerves. Saturday's protests at Doonbeg and Shannon drew 20 to 30 people each, per a CNN photographer; today's numbers aren't in. And nobody has won anything yet.

FAQ

Is Trump presenting the Irish Open trophy?

He's expected to, per Golfmagic and RTÉ, with Martin and two government ministers expected to feature. It hadn't happened at the time of writing. Trump has owned the Doonbeg course since 2014, per AFP.

What did Trump say about a united Ireland?

That reunification was inevitable and “may as well happen now”, gesturing at Martin as he said “You got the right man over here to get it moving”, per MS NOW. CNN also quotes him saying he'd love to see it unified.

Who is leading the Irish Open?

Shane Lowry, at 16-under after rounds of 67, a course-record 62 and 65, four clear of Jacob Skov Olesen and seven clear of Joaquín Niemann as of the end of round three.

The Raw Verdict

The tour picked this venue a year ago. Everyone involved has had twelve months to anticipate exactly this afternoon, and the institutions that made the booking don't get to look surprised now.

Watch the 18th green, and watch what the crowd is loudest for. A 39-year-old from Offaly who has won one individual event since Portrush is four shots from his home Open. That should be the noise. The fact it's in question is the story.
