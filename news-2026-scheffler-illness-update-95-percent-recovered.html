Scheffler confirms 95 percent recovery heading into Tour Championship

Scottie Scheffler illness update: world No. 1 says he is 95 percent recovered from hand, foot and mouth disease. Why his BMW Championship T12 looked worse than it was.

BY GOLFRAW EDITORIAL PUBLISHED WED 26 AUG 2026 VERIFIED UPDATE 5 MIN READ
Medical & Playoff Status: August 26, 2026 | Tour Championship Preview | East Lake Golf Club

Key Takeaways

  • 95 Percent Recovered: Scottie Scheffler confirmed at East Lake that he is roughly 95 percent back to full strength after suffering from hand, foot and mouth disease (HFMD).
  • Grip Blisters Resolved: The painful cutaneous blisters on his hands—which directly altered his grip pressure and clubface release at Bellerive—have dried and completely healed.
  • The T12 in Context: Despite acute physical discomfort, Scheffler carded rounds of 72-70-69-68 at the BMW Championship, shooting 1 under par on the week and outscoring the field average on his back nines.
  • Level-Par East Lake Reset: With starting strokes eliminated under the 2026 Tour Championship format, Scheffler tees off on Thursday in an even-par 72-hole shootout without lingering physical limitations.

World No. 1 Scottie Scheffler arrived at East Lake Golf Club on Wednesday with clear hands, full swing speed, and a direct message regarding his physical condition: he is 95 percent recovered from the hand, foot and mouth disease that compromised his play at the BMW Championship.

Addressing reporters ahead of the season-ending Tour Championship, Scheffler laid out the full progression of the pediatric virus, which he contracted from his infant son, Bennett, following his eight-shot romp at the FedEx St. Jude Championship in Memphis. What began as low-grade fatigue and a hoarse throat quickly escalated into painful blisters on the palms of his hands and soles of his feet—the worst possible physical ailment for a golfer whose entire ball-striking supremacy relies on sensitive handle control.

"My hands were in pretty rough shape at Bellerive. Holding onto the club at the top of the swing and releasing it without flinching was tough. But the blisters are gone now, energy is back, and I feel about 95 percent."
— SCOTTIE SCHEFFLER, EAST LAKE PRESS CONFERENCE

The Memphis to Bellerive Timeline: How the Infection Unfolded

Understanding Scheffler's performance across the FedExCup Playoffs requires looking at the exact medical timeline between TPC Southwind and Bellerive Country Club:

  • Aug 13–16 (Memphis Dominance): Scheffler authored a historic tournament at TPC Southwind, matching the course record with a 61 on Friday and cruising to an eight-shot victory at 15 under par.
  • Aug 17–19 (Symptom Onset): Following travel home to Dallas and then up to St. Louis, Scheffler began experiencing initial systemic symptoms—chills, fever, and hoarseness—contracted from his toddler son.
  • Aug 20 (Round 1 at Bellerive): Scheffler opened the BMW Championship visibly compromised. With active lesions forming on his hands, he posted a 2-over 72, skipped post-round media with a hoarse voice, and began medical management.
  • Aug 21–23 (Battling Through Blisters): Refusing to withdraw, Scheffler fought through rounds of 70, 69, and 68, grinding out a T12 finish at 1 under par.
  • Aug 24–26 (Atlanta Recovery): With the contagious phase passed and skin lesions healed, Scheffler completed practice sessions at East Lake reporting zero pain in his grip.

Why His BMW Championship T12 Looked Worse Than It Was

When a player who has dominated the 2026 PGA Tour season finishes tied for 12th in a 50-man field, initial headlines often frame it as a sudden collapse in form. In reality, Scheffler's performance at Bellerive was an extraordinary display of competitive grit under severe physical handicap.

Hand, foot and mouth disease is notorious for causing fluid-filled, tender blisters on epidermal contact zones. In professional golf, where millimeter differences in grip pressure dictate shot curvature and launch windows, blistering on the lead hand makes maintaining friction nearly impossible without sharp pain during transition.

After an opening front nine of 38 on Thursday when the blisters were freshest, Scheffler adapted his delivery. Over his final 63 holes at Bellerive, he played 3 under par, punctuated by a bogey-free 2-under 34 on Sunday's back nine. A golfer with a broken swing does not shoot 68 on Sunday at a 7,448-yard championship layout.

Current Physical Condition Ahead of East Lake

Heading into Thursday's opening round at East Lake, Scheffler confirmed that the viral symptoms have dissipated. The three-day break between St. Louis and Atlanta allowed the skin on his hands to fully re-epithelialize, eliminating the sharp pain associated with full-speed driver releases.

TrackMan numbers during Tuesday and Wednesday practice rounds at East Lake showed Scheffler matching his season-long baseline ball speed of 178–181 mph with the driver. His short game touch and wedge proximity, which suffered slightly during the peak blister days, showed no signs of lingering compensation.

"When you're dealing with blisters on your fingers, you're subconsciously trying not to hurt yourself at impact. Being able to go at it at 100 percent without thinking about my hands makes all the difference."
— SCHEFFLER ON PRACTICE PREPARATIONS AT EAST LAKE

The Playoff Landscape: Level Par at East Lake

Scheffler's recovery arrives at a pivotal tactical juncture. Under the PGA Tour's 2026 rule revisions, starting strokes have been completely removed from the Tour Championship. In previous seasons, entering East Lake as the No. 1 seed granted a 10-under-par starting buffer; this week, all 30 qualified players begin Thursday at even par.

With bookmakers establishing Scheffler as the +310 betting favorite, his health update removes the primary uncertainty clouding his East Lake campaign. He is physically ready to compete across 72 holes of unhandicapped stroke play for the FedExCup trophy.

How recovered is Scottie Scheffler for the Tour Championship?

The Raw Take

The panic surrounding Scheffler's BMW Championship week was always an overreaction to a viral blip. Hand blisters are an acute mechanical nuisance, not a chronic swing flaw. Scheffler posted a Sunday 68 while barely able to squeeze the grip; with his hands healed and his speed back, the world No. 1 enters East Lake as dangerous as ever.
