Srixon ZXi Irons Review: Which Set Should You Hit First?

The ZXi7, ZXi5 and ZXi5+ share a premium price but suit different jobs. Compare lofts, carry spread and your common miss, then hit ZXi5 first as the middle point in a fitting.

BY GolfRaw Editorial PUBLISHED WED 23 SEP 2026 UPDATED 23 SEP 2026 11 MIN READ

Srixon wants $1,499.99 for a seven-club steel set of its new ZXi7, ZXi5 or ZXi5+ irons. At that price, "they felt great in the shop" is a lousy reason to pick one. All three have polished looks. The useful differences show up when your strike moves off center and when you compare how far the ball carries, club by club.

Quick Answer: The 2026 Srixon ZXi irons give you three choices: ZXi7 for control, ZXi5 for a mix of speed and forgiveness, and ZXi5+ for stronger lofts and added distance. Beginners should also try the more forgiving ZXiR or higher-launching ZXiR HL. Start with ZXi5, then let your carry distances and misses make the decision.

That answer hides a catch. A ZXi5+ 7-iron has 29.5 degrees of loft. The ZXi7 7-iron has 32 degrees. Comparing the numbers stamped on their soles won't tell you which set helps you hit more greens.

Built on: Srixon's September 2026 announcement, its published specifications, and independent reviews from Golf Monthly and Plugged In Golf. Golf Raw has not conducted its own hitting test of these irons.

Srixon announced the second-generation ZXi7 and ZXi5, plus the new ZXi5+, on September 8, 2026. The stated US launch date is October 16, 2026. Older reviews cover the first-generation 2025 clubs unless they say otherwise. Srixon's launch announcement makes that distinction clear.

If you play twice a month and rarely find the middle of a 6-iron face, there's no prize for choosing the smallest head.

Is the ZXi7 too demanding for an ordinary golfer?

The ZXi7 is the one that might surprise you. It has the shortest blade, narrowest sole and least offset of the three new performance models. Srixon uses an S15C forged-steel body and puts tungsten in the toe of the long and middle irons. Its published 7-iron offset is 2.8 mm, compared with 3.2 mm in both the ZXi5 and ZXi5+. Srixon lists those measurements in its model specifications.

Small doesn't automatically mean punishing. In its 2025 player's-iron test, MyGolfSpy ranked the first-generation ZXi7 first for both accuracy and forgiveness within that category. That's a good reason to hit the current model. It is not proof that the 2026 ZXi7 will beat a ZXiR for a beginner with scattered contact.

For the new head, Plugged In Golf's reviewer reported consistent carry on both centered strikes and mishits. Golf Monthly's reviewer liked the slimmer topline and feel but called the changes from the previous generation small. Both are individual tests. Neither can tell you what your low-face miss will do.

Try the ZXi7 if you already hit your irons high enough and want the ball to travel the number you planned. Give it a fair test even if your handicap has two digits. Then look at your worst few shots. If the ZXi7 5-iron keeps falling short, switch that end of the set to ZXi5 before giving up on the short irons.

Why is ZXi5 the starting point for most fittings?

Stand behind a ZXi5 and you still see a restrained iron head. You get a wider sole than ZXi7, plus a face Srixon calls MainFrame iQ. Srixon says it varies face thickness to help preserve speed across the face. That's a design claim, not a promise that a toe strike will finish beside the flag. Srixon describes the construction here.

There is useful independent evidence, with a date attached. MyGolfSpy placed the 2025 ZXi5 in the top ten for accuracy, distance and forgiveness in its player's-distance test. That result belongs to the previous generation. In a September 2026 review, Plugged In Golf found the new ZXi5 fast and forgiving, though its tester sometimes sent a shot farther than intended after changing face angle or delivered loft.

That's the trade you should test. A longer shot feels terrific on a range screen. From the fairway, an unexpected jump can put your ball through the back of the green. Record your shortest and longest usable carries with the ZXi5, then compare that spread with the ZXi7 and ZXi5+. A tight group is worth something, even when its longest shot doesn't win.

If you're moving on from starter irons and your contact has become steadier, ZXi5 is a sensible first stop. It also makes sense for a golfer who likes the ZXi7's shape but needs help in the 4-, 5- or 6-iron.

Does ZXi5+ give you free distance?

The ZXi5+ is the new club in the lineup. It replaces the outgoing 2025 ZXi4's place among Srixon's ZXi performance irons, while the ZXiR range handles the game improvement job. Golf Monthly describes the ZXi5+ as a forged hollow-body iron. Srixon gives it a longer blade, wider sole and stronger lofts than ZXi5.

The spec sheet tells you what to watch. The ZXi5+ 7-iron is 29.5°, against 31° in ZXi5 and 32° in ZXi7. Its pitching wedge is 43°. Srixon's ZXi5+ specifications also list a 49° approach wedge. If you buy the pitching wedge and jump straight to a much higher-lofted specialty wedge, you may leave a hole in the distances you use most.

Golf Monthly's tester reported strong speed and stable results on toe and heel strikes. His experience makes the ZXi5+ worth a swing if your present irons come up short. It doesn't establish a distance gain for every golfer, and the article doesn't publish a controlled, matched-loft comparison across all three new heads.

Give the ZXi5+ a job. Perhaps your current 6-iron won't carry the front bunker. See if its higher speed solves that shot while the ball still lands at an angle that holds the green. If the shot flies farther but runs through the back, you've swapped one problem for another.

Should a beginner buy ZXiR instead?

Picture the long par 3 at your usual public course. You've got enough club, but your 6-iron comes off low and skitters into the front trap. Buying a compact head because it looks tidy behind the ball won't make that shot easier.

Srixon calls ZXiR its game improvement iron. Its 7-iron has 28.5° of loft and 3.5 mm of offset. The ZXiR HL goes a different direction: 32.5° in the 7-iron and a build Srixon describes as suited to low-to-moderate swing speeds.

Those choices solve different problems. Test ZXiR if you want ball speed and help when contact wanders. Test ZXiR HL if your ball starts low and struggles to stay in the air. Don't choose from loft alone. Your shaft, strike and delivery can change the result, which is why both belong in the fitting bay.

A bigger head can also be the right choice for only part of the bag. Srixon shows a ZXiR long-iron and ZXi5 short-iron combination. That's a practical answer when your 8-iron is fine and your 5-iron keeps letting you down.

What about the V-shaped sole?

Every new performance model uses Srixon's Tour V.T. Sole, with different sole widths and shaped front and rear edges. Srixon says it helps the head move through turf and rough. The company details the sole on its ZXi5 page, and independent testers at Plugged In Golf and Golf Monthly liked how their test clubs moved through the ground.

You still need to hit shots from grass. A mat can hide where the club enters the turf. If you tend to strike behind the ball, pay attention to what happens to carry distance from an ordinary fairway lie, then try the same club from a tighter patch. The sole may suit your swing. The name alone tells you nothing.

Three pieces of ZXi advice worth ignoring

  • "The longest 7-iron wins." The ZXi5+ has 2.5° less 7-iron loft than ZXi7, according to Srixon's specifications. Check carry, height, landing angle and the next club's gap before celebrating the biggest number.
  • "ZXi7 is only for scratch players." MyGolfSpy's 2025 test found that generation's ZXi7 exceptionally forgiving among player's irons. The 2026 model deserves a try if you strike the middle irons well. Your own misses get the final say.
  • "A V-sole fixes fat shots." Srixon makes a case for better turf interaction, and reviewers liked the feel. No sole can make contact behind the ball identical to contact with the ball first. Take a demo club onto grass before you spend set money.

Are the 2026 ZXi irons worth the price?

Srixon's US launch prices are $1,499.99 for seven ZXi7, ZXi5 or ZXi5+ irons with steel shafts. The ZXi5 and ZXi5+ graphite sets are listed at $1,599.99. A seven-club ZXiR or ZXiR HL steel set is listed at $1,099.99. Those are launch prices, so check the current listing before buying.

Owners of the 2025 ZXi5 or ZXi7 have a strong reason to pause. Srixon kept their published 7-iron lofts at 31° and 32°, respectively, in the new generation. Golf Monthly's ZXi7 tester thought its improvements might be too small to justify an immediate upgrade. Fresh clubs may feel lovely. Bring your current 7-iron to the fitting and make the new one earn the bill.

For a first serious set, budget for the whole bag. The ZXi5+ pitching wedge is 43° and its approach wedge 49°. The ZXi7 pitching wedge is 46° and its approach wedge 51°. Srixon publishes both sets of lofts. Check those gaps against the wedges you already own before deciding what the seven clubs should include.

How do you choose a ZXi combo set?

Start with one model through the set. Change a club only when it gives you a result you can name: a higher 5-iron flight, a steadier 8-iron carry, or a gap that no longer overlaps its neighbor. Srixon suggests ZXi5 in the 4- through 6-irons and ZXi7 in the 7-iron through pitching wedge as one blend. Its combo guide shows that build.

At the fitting, use the same ball for each model. Hit your current club first, then compare several usable shots with each candidate. Keep the mishits in the results. Ask for carry distance and landing angle, and check the transition clubs on either side of a proposed split.

Don't order a ZXi5+ long iron and ZXi7 short iron because the pairing sounds clever. The lofts differ across models. A fitter can check the actual carry gaps and adjust the build if two neighboring irons travel too close together. Use GolfRaw’s Bag Audit to map that gap.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are Srixon ZXi5 irons forgiving?

The new ZXi5 adds a wider sole and more speed-focused face design than ZXi7. In his 2026 hands-on review, Plugged In Golf's tester found substantial help on mishits, though he also saw occasional shots travel farther than planned. Compare the spread of your own carries before deciding that forgiveness has improved your scoring shots.

What's the difference between ZXi4 and ZXi5+?

ZXi4 is a 2025 model still listed by Srixon. ZXi5+ joins the 2026 lineup as a forged hollow player's-distance option with a slimmer-looking profile, according to Golf Monthly's comparison. Their published 7-iron lofts are 28.5° for ZXi4 and 29.5° for ZXi5+. Test them as different clubs.

When can I buy the second-generation ZXi irons?

Srixon lists October 16, 2026 as the US launch date for the second-generation ZXi7, ZXi5, ZXi5+ and ZXiU. Its September 8 announcement also describes a presale. Check the model year when shopping: the 2025 ZXi5 and ZXi7 remain on Srixon's site alongside the new heads.

Do I need a fitting for Srixon ZXi irons?

Yes, if you're considering a $1,499.99 set. A fitting lets you compare your current irons with ZXi7, ZXi5, ZXi5+ and, if needed, ZXiR. Look beyond the longest shot. Check your common miss, carry gaps and the flight of the longest iron you can hit with confidence. That's where the models separate.

The Raw Verdict

Hit the ZXi5 first. It gives you a useful middle point from which to judge what you need: more control from ZXi7, more speed from ZXi5+, or more help from ZXiR. The new range offers enough distinct heads to build around your weak clubs instead of buying seven copies of the club that won a single 7-iron contest.

If you already own the 2025 ZXi5 or ZXi7, take them to the fitting. Your money stays in your pocket unless the new set gives you a better result on the shots you hit most.
