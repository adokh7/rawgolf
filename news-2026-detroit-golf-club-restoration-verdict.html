Three 61s in Three Days. The Detroit Restoration Has Its Answer.

BY GOLFRAW EDITORIAL PUBLISHED: SUN 02 AUG 2026

Editor’s Note: This article covers the 54-hole standings and architectural analysis of the 2026 Rocket Classic at Detroit Golf Club prior to the final round conclusion.

The Quick Verdict

Davis Riley leads the final Rocket Classic at 15-under through 54 holes. That's 195 actual strokes. Last year, on the shorter par-72 version of Detroit Golf Club that everyone said was too easy, the 54-hole leader had taken 197. The restoration cost $16.1 million and shaved two shots off par, and the scoring has gone in the wrong direction. Also: three players have shot 61 this week. The course record was 63 two years ago.

I wrote a piece on Thursday saying we didn't know yet whether the renovation had made Detroit Golf Club harder. One round, new greens, a calm morning, not enough to call it.

We know now.

The number that settles it

Forget par. Par is what changed, so par is the one thing you can't compare across years.

Count strokes instead.

  • 2025: Aldrich Potgieter led after 54 holes at 19-under on a par-72 course. That's 197 strokes.
  • 2026: Davis Riley leads after 54 holes at 15-under on a par-70 course. That's 195 strokes.

The restored course, with its 91 bunkers and its deeper faces and its four par 4s over 500 yards and its extra fescue, has given up a 54-hole leading total two shots lower than the version everybody agreed was a pushover.

Riley is averaging 65.0 a round. Potgieter's winning total last year averaged 66.5.

There are caveats and they're real. Different players. Different weather. Different field. One year is one year. But there is no version of "the restoration made it harder" that survives a leader going round in fewer actual strokes than last year's leader did.

Three 61s. Count them.

Before the 2025 tournament, nobody had ever shot better than 63 at Detroit Golf Club. Jake Knapp broke that with a 61 in the second round last year and it stood as the mark.

This week:

  • Peter Malnati, 61, Thursday. Out in 28, bogey-free, lowest opening round on Tour all season.
  • Cameron Young, 61, Friday. Climbed 68 places on the leaderboard doing it.
  • Rasmus Højgaard, 61, Saturday. Also bogey-free. Moved him to 12-under and a share of third.

Three rounds of 61 in three consecutive days on a golf course that was rebuilt, in part, to stop exactly that. Twenty-four months ago the number 61 did not exist here.

One of the wire headlines called Højgaard's round a "record day." It wasn't. It's the same raw score Knapp shot last year, just against a lower par. That kind of mistake is going to keep happening, because everyone is still doing the arithmetic against a scorecard that changed in June.

Malnati has clearly had enough of this

The AP copy describes Detroit Golf Club as a relatively easy course. In the same story, it quotes Malnati saying he doesn't think he took advantage of a super easy course, and that he played great.

He's got a point and he's also, unfortunately for him, arguing against the scoreboard. Malnati, ranked 565th in the world, followed his opening 61 with rounds of 71 and 74 to fall back to 4-under—a stark reminder that while the course can give up a 61, it can quickly take those shots back off a player.

The Detroit News noted that the 17th and 18th have been two of the hardest holes all week. Riley made birdie on both of them Saturday, holing from more than fifty feet at 17 and ten feet at 18, and that is what put him in the final group.

So the course has genuinely hard holes. It also has ten other holes where good players make birdie whenever they feel like it. Both things are true and "easy course" flattens them into a lazy summary.

Why it's still soft, and what that means

The greens are twelve months old. That's the whole explanation and I keep coming back to it.

A restored Donald Ross course defends itself through firmness. Crowned greens reject approaches, false fronts feed balls away, and suddenly the angle of your second shot matters more than the length of your first. Grass planted last summer cannot do any of that. It holds whatever lands on it, and it's slow enough that players are rolling in 54-footers.

Whatever happens with incoming weather, it will not be a test of firm-and-fast architecture. The fair verdict on the restoration is this: it is probably a much better golf course now, and it will probably defend par properly in about three years. Those two things don't help the tournament that's leaving town.

The Real Reason Rocket Is Leaving

Rocket declined its 2027 option after thirteen years as a PGA Tour title sponsor, and the reason is more interesting than "attendance was down." Per AP reporting, the Tour is splitting into a two-tier model in 2028. Rocket had been spending about $15 million a year and was not willing to keep paying that to sit in the second tier—nor to double that investment to join the elite tier. This isn't a company walking away from Detroit because of attendance; it's a company being presented with a new price list and deciding the product wasn't worth either number.

What a normal golfer should take from this

Relabelling holes doesn't defend a golf course. Members everywhere do this. Turn two short par 5s into long par 4s, watch the winning score drop from 22-under to something respectable, declare victory. The golf is identical. The card is just meaner. If your club does it, your handicap will move and your ball-striking won't.

Firmness is the real defence, and it's the cheapest one. Detroit spent sixteen million on shape. What's actually stopping anybody right now is nothing, because the surfaces are young and wet. Any course that runs its greens firm and fast becomes dramatically harder without moving a cubic yard of dirt.

Riley's Saturday is the useful bit for your own game. Bogey-free, back-to-back. He made three bogeys on Thursday and none since. On a course where everybody is making birdies, the differentiator isn't making more of them, it's making no mistakes.

FAQ

Who leads the 2026 Rocket Classic after 54 holes?
Davis Riley, at 15-under 195 through 54 holes after a bogey-free 63, one clear of Michael Brennan and three ahead of Rickie Fowler's group.

Did the Detroit Golf Club renovation make the course harder?
The evidence says no, at least this year. The 54-hole leader has taken 195 strokes; last year's 54-hole leader took 197 on the shorter par-72 layout. Three players have shot 61 this week. The greens are only a year old and still soft, which is the most likely explanation.

How many rounds of 61 have been shot at Detroit Golf Club this week?
Three. Peter Malnati on Thursday, Cameron Young on Friday, Rasmus Højgaard on Saturday. Before 2025 the course record was 63.

Why did Rocket Mortgage end its sponsorship?
According to AP reporting, Rocket declined its option due to the PGA Tour's planned 2028 two-tier restructuring. Rocket was unwilling to pay $15 million annually for a second-tier event or double its spend to tier one.

What's at stake for Davis Riley?
A $1.8 million winner's cheque from a $10 million purse, his third career win, and a serious move toward the FedExCup Playoffs.

GOLFRAW's Take

Detroit Golf Club did not waste sixteen million dollars. The members got their Donald Ross back. Bigger greens with real contour, bunkers that make you think about which side of the fairway you want, a pond gone that was never in the original drawing. That course is going to be a joy to play for fifty years and none of that depended on the PGA Tour showing up. But the thing the tournament wanted, a scorecard that didn't embarrass everyone on television, they tried to buy with a pen instead of a bulldozer. Detroit spent $16.1 million trying to buy status with a scorecard, and Rocket declined to spend $30 million trying to buy it with a cheque. One of them at least got a golf course out of it.
