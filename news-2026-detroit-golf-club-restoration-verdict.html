They Cut Par by Two. The Winning Score Was the Lowest in Tournament History.

Michael Thorbjornsen won the last Rocket Classic at 18-under 262. That's the exact same number of raw strokes it took Tony Finau to set the scoring record four years ago. What $16.1M actually buys you.

BY GOLFRAW EDITORIAL PUBLISHED: MON 03 AUG 2026

What they actually did

Detroit Golf Club spent $16.1 million attempting to toughen up a course that had developed a reputation as a pushover on the PGA Tour schedule. To accomplish this, the members approved an aggressive Donald Ross restoration project. They deepened 91 bunkers, reshaped green complexes, and introduced extra fescue to line the fairways.

Most critically, they reduced the official par from a generous 72 to a sterner 70. The mandate was clear: lower the winning score in relation to par, restore the prestige of the classic architecture, and stop the world's best players from turning the venue into a pitch-and-putt exhibition.

What happened instead

The PGA Tour players arrived, admired the architectural nuances, and then immediately proceeded to dismantle the golf course anyway. Michael Thorbjornsen, in a display of relentless ball-striking, secured his victory with a final score of 18-under par.

While 18-under might sound significantly harder than the 26-under winning scores we’ve seen in the past, that number is an illusion created entirely by the math on the scorecard. The players were still firing at the exact same targets and holing putts at an alarming frequency. In fact, three different players signed for 61s over the course of the week.

The comparison that matters

When you strip away the arbitrary metric of par and focus entirely on raw strokes played over 72 holes, the narrative completely unravels. Below is the unvarnished reality of the restoration's impact:

Year Champion Par Score to Par Total Strokes
2022 Tony Finau 72 -26 262
2025 Aldrich Potgieter 72 -22 266
2026 Michael Thorbjornsen 70 -18 262

Sixteen million dollars later, the winner took exactly the same number of swings to beat the field as Tony Finau did during the absolute peak of the tournament's "too easy" era.

The honest counter-argument

Any architect or greenskeeper will rightly point out that a golf course's primary defense isn't its bunkers or its rough—it's the firmness of its greens. Because the new surfaces were barely twelve months old, they hadn't yet developed the necessary root structure to be baked out to championship speeds. They remained incredibly receptive all week.

Players could confidently land short irons next to the flag knowing the ball would stop dead. False fronts and run-offs were rendered mostly irrelevant because nobody was missing their target lines. Given another three years to properly cure, these greens might become the terrifying Donald Ross surfaces they were designed to be.

The bit the restoration actually earned

This isn't to say the money was wasted. For 51 weeks a year, the membership of Detroit Golf Club now gets to play a spectacularly revived architectural gem. The course is vastly more interesting visually and strategically for the amateur player. They succeeded in bringing a masterpiece back to life.

But they tried to solve a PGA Tour problem, and that's where they failed. You cannot buy a harder tournament just by rewriting the numbers on a piece of cardboard.

Thorbjornsen, who deserves better than being my exhibit

Amidst all the debate about the architecture, Michael Thorbjornsen played flawless golf under pressure. He managed his distances perfectly, putted brilliantly on surfaces that were heavily trafficked, and closed out a vital victory that secures his immediate future on Tour. He didn't ask for the par to be changed, and he shouldn't have to apologize for shooting a 262.

What a normal golfer should take from this

The lesson here is profound: par is an arbitrary construct. If your home course decides to toughen up by turning a short par-5 into a long par-4, your score relative to par will suffer, but your actual ball-striking hasn't changed. Stop obsessing over whether you shot "five over" or "six over." Count your total strokes. That is the only metric that truly reflects the state of your golf game, just as it is the only metric that tells the truth about Detroit Golf Club.

FAQ Section

Michael Thorbjornsen won the 2026 Rocket Classic at 18-under par.

He took 262 strokes, which tied the tournament record set by Tony Finau in 2022.

In relation to par, yes (par was reduced from 72 to 70). But in terms of raw strokes, the winning score was exactly the same as the lowest score recorded on the old layout.

The Raw Take

The PGA Tour is leaving Detroit not because the course was too easy, but because the economics of professional golf have fundamentally broken the traditional title sponsor model. Detroit Golf Club spent a fortune trying to impress an audience that ultimately cared more about the balance sheet than the bunkering. Thorbjornsen's 262 proves that you can change the par, but you can't change the reality of modern professional golf: if the greens are soft, these guys are going to shoot 61.
