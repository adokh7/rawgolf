“She's Here. She's Playing.” Team USA Plays Down Angel Yin's Fitness, and Flies In an Alternate Anyway

By Golf Raw Editorial Staff Published 08 Sep 2026 Updated 08 Sep 2026, 21:00 UTC 4 min read

U.S. Solheim Cup captain Angela Stanford said on Tuesday that Angel Yin is playing this week at Bernardus Golf, having completed two practice rounds, while former world No. 1 Lilia Vu is on site in the Netherlands as the Americans' alternate in case Yin can't go. Golf Channel reports Yin has played once since June because of stomach issues that put her in hospital for six days during the KPMG Women's PGA Championship, and that she has said she lost 35 pounds. She qualified for her fourth Solheim Cup automatically on points and carries a 5-3-1 record.

Read those two sentences together and you have the whole situation. A captain saying there's nothing to see, and a spare player in uniform. The section on what Yin herself has said explains why both are reasonable.

Golf Raw was not at Bernardus and has not spoken to Yin, Stanford or U.S. team medical staff. Health details are limited to what Yin and her captain have said publicly, as reported by Golf Channel, Golf Digest and Golfweek. Golf Raw does not know Yin's diagnosis and won't guess at it.

What the captain said, and what she didn't

Stanford's line on Tuesday, per Golf Channel, was direct: “She's playing. She's played two practice rounds.” She added that the team is still gauging and watching, and that Yin is happy to be there.

That's a captain doing her job. Three weeks earlier, reached by Golfweek and reported via Yahoo Sports, Stanford said she and Yin had been texting, that Yin was still evaluating her situation, and that “I am very confident in giving Angel all the runway that she needs.” She said the issue was not an injury and declined to reveal what it was.

What she hasn't said is whether Yin will play both sessions on a day, or which sessions, or whether the two practice rounds were full 18s. Nobody has published that, and pairings aren't announced until later in the week.

Golf Raw's read: the confidence is real and so is the contingency. Stanford has told the truth in both directions, which is more than most captains manage.

What Yin has actually been through

Six days in hospital and 35 pounds gone. That's the part the word “fitness” undersells.

Golf Channel reports Yin started experiencing stomach pain and nausea during the KPMG Women's PGA in late June, withdrew from the third round, was admitted to hospital and stayed six days. She hasn't disclosed what she's dealing with. She has said she lost 35 pounds and had to request different-sized uniforms.

She missed the season's last two majors, per Golfweek's primer via Yahoo Sports. Her one competitive appearance since, at the FM Championship in Boston, produced rounds of 81 and 76 and a missed cut. Golf Digest framed that week as her trial for the Solheim Cup, 15 days out.

Yin's own assessment of the second round, per Golfweek via DailyClubGolf, was that “I didn't make anything over a bogey,” and that going from 81 to 76 was an immense improvement for her even if it doesn't look like much in professional golf.

She also told Golfweek that if she felt she wasn't fit for the team, she'd let Stanford know. That's the sentence to hold onto. The player has said she'd pull herself.

Why Vu is there

Because the envelope has a name in it, and this year the name matters.

Golfweek's primer via Yahoo Sports explains the mechanism: the U.S. captain seals two alternate names when the team is announced, and if a player can't go, the first alternate steps in. Stanford said assistant captain Paula Creamer's input had been valuable because she's been both a pick and an alternate.

Golf Channel confirms Vu is on site and in uniform. She earned the clinching half-point at the 2024 Solheim Cup, per Golf Channel, but had missed 10 straight cuts and slipped to 235th in the world before this. Rose Zhang is also managing a minor back injury that forced a withdrawal in Canada last month.

So the alternate isn't a formality this week. Two of twelve players have something to manage, and the reserve has a Solheim Cup point on her record.

What a healthy Yin gives Team USA

A 5-3-1 record and two LPGA wins. That's why the captain wants her on the tee rather than in the team room.

Golf Digest notes she was a captain's pick in 2017 and 2023 and qualified via the world rankings in 2019, and that she made this team on her own despite the absence. Yardbarker's syndicated DailyClubGolf piece calls her a no-brainer when healthy, which is the plain truth.

One honest caveat about the coverage circulating this week. Some aggregated pieces describe Yin as “playing pain-free” and fully set to feature. Golf Raw could not find that in anything Yin or Stanford said on the record. What's verified is two practice rounds and a captain still gauging.

FAQ

Is Angel Yin injured for the Solheim Cup?

No. Stanford told Golfweek in August that the issue is not an injury. Yin experienced stomach pain and nausea at the KPMG Women's PGA in late June, was hospitalised for six days, and hasn't revealed the diagnosis. She has said she lost 35 pounds. She's completed two practice rounds at Bernardus.

Who replaces Yin if she can't play?

Lilia Vu, who is on site at Bernardus Golf in uniform as the Americans' alternate, per Golf Channel. Vu holed the clinching half-point at the 2024 Solheim Cup but had missed 10 straight cuts and dropped to 235th in the world before this week.

What is Angel Yin's Solheim Cup record?

5-3-1 across three appearances in 2017, 2019 and 2023, per Golf Channel and Golf Digest. She was a captain's pick in 2017 and 2023, qualified via the world rankings in 2019, and made the 2026 team automatically on points despite missing two months.

The Raw Verdict

Stanford isn't bluffing and she isn't hiding anything either. Her player has done two practice rounds, her alternate is in uniform, and she's said out loud that she's still watching. That's the honest version of team golf, and it's rarer than it should be.

What to watch is simpler than the health talk. If Yin's name is in Friday morning's pairings, Stanford has seen enough in three days at Bernardus. If it isn't, don't read it as a lie exposed. Read it as a captain and a player who both said they'd make the call when they had the information, and then did.
