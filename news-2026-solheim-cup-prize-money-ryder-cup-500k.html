There's No Prize Money at the Solheim Cup. Until 2025, That Was True of the Ryder Cup Too.

By Golf Raw Editorial Staff Published 13 Sep 2026 Updated 13 Sep 2026, 16:00 IST 5 min read

No. There is no prize money at the Solheim Cup. Neither team is paid for winning, the losing team receives nothing, and no player is paid per point or for appearing. That applies to all 24 players, both captains and every vice-captain. What players do receive is the week itself: flights, accommodation, food and drink, team clothing, bags and gear, plus sponsor bags.

That arrangement used to describe the Ryder Cup too. It stopped in 2025, and the reason it stopped is the most interesting part of this question.

Europe regained the Cup at Bernardus Golf on Sunday, reaching the 14.5 points they needed when Lottie Woad beat Auston Kim 2&1 to make it 14-10 and Carlota Ciganda guaranteed herself a half against Jennifer Kupcho. Anna Nordqvist's side takes the trophy back from Angela Stanford's Americans, who won 15.5-12.5 in Virginia in 2024. Nobody on either side will be paid a penny for any of it.

Golf Raw has not seen any Solheim Cup, LPGA or LET financial documents. Everything below comes from published reporting by Golf Monthly, Golf News Net, Golfmagic, ESPN, CBS Sports and Sky Sports, with the year attached to anything from the Ryder Cup's 2024 and 2025 decisions.

So what do Solheim Cup players actually get?

The week, paid for.

Golf News Net's Ryan Ballengee sets out what's covered: players are flown to the event site, accommodated, and have their food and beverage handled. Clothes are provided for the week, along with unique golf bags and other gear, and there are sponsor bags on top.

Golfmagic confirms the same absence from the other direction. There is no prize fund attached to the Solheim Cup, and players do not receive money based on their performance or their team's finish. Golf Monthly puts it plainly: no prize money for either team, regardless of the result.

The part most explainers skip is that it extends past the 24 players. Captains and assistant captains aren't paid either. Ballengee describes making the team, or being named captain or assistant captain, as among the highest honours in the sport.

So Nordqvist worked two years for a trophy and a team photograph. So did Stanford.

The part that isn't a perk

Here's the line item nobody puts on the list. During the Solheim Cup, players wear team uniforms rather than their usual branded apparel, according to Golf Reviews Guide. Treat that as a reported claim from a smaller outlet rather than a confirmed policy document, but it matches what you can see on any broadcast.

Think about what that costs. A player's apparel deal pays for exposure. The Solheim Cup is the most-watched week her sport gets, and it's the one week her sponsor's logo isn't on her chest.

The same source argues the offset is indirect: raised profile, new sponsorship opportunities, especially for young or first-time players. That's plausible and it's also unmeasurable. Nobody has published a figure for what a Solheim Cup appearance is worth to a player's endorsement income, and Golf Raw isn't going to invent one.

The men's version of this question changed in 2025

For 25 years, the Ryder Cup answered this the same way the Solheim Cup still does.

From 1999, each US player had ,000 allocated in his name, strictly for charity. That figure didn't move once, through six Ryder Cups, while television money and purses climbed everywhere else in golf.

Then in December 2024, the PGA of America's board approved a new scheme, first applied at the 2025 Ryder Cup at Bethpage Black. Each of the 12 US players and the captain now receives ,000: ,000 to distribute to charity, and a ,000 personal stipend. ESPN reported it as the first time Americans have been paid to play in the event.

PGA of America president Don Rea explained the reasoning to ESPN in December 2024. “We're talking 25 years that it's been at ,000,” he said, adding that ,000 “seemed like the right place to land.” In a letter to past Ryder Cup captains, he wrote: “We added the ,000 stipend out of respect for the players ... so the players could have a say in where the money goes.”

The detail that should stop you is in CBS Sports' reporting from the same week. The PGA of America confirmed that no current players were pushing for the increase. It was an organizational decision.

US captain Keegan Bradley framed it as overdue housekeeping in September 2025: “[PGA of America] wanted to bring the Ryder Cup into the present day. The charity dollars hadn't changed since 1999.” Scottie Scheffler, asked what he'd do with his, told reporters: “I've never been one to announce what we do. I don't like to give charitable dollars for some kind of recognition.” Several US players said they planned to donate the full ,000.

Every quote in this section is from the men's event. No equivalent conversation about the Solheim Cup has been reported anywhere Golf Raw could find.

Three team events, three different answers

The lazy version of this comparison is men versus women. It doesn't survive five minutes of checking.

European Ryder Cup players aren't paid either. CBS Sports noted in December 2024 that when reports of the US change surfaced, members of the European team pushed back at the idea of being paid at all. Rory McIlroy and Shane Lowry were among those who said they'd pay for the privilege of playing rather than the other way round.

ESPN also reported that Rea described the new arrangement as similar to what the PGA Tour does for the Presidents Cup, so the US structure wasn't invented from nothing.

What nobody has published

Being honest about the gaps matters more here than usual, because this is a money story where almost none of the money is disclosed.

No figure exists for what the Solheim Cup spends per player on travel, accommodation, food and apparel. Every source describes it qualitatively. No source distinguishes between what European and American players receive. Nothing has been published about whether captains or vice-captains get anything beyond the same treatment.

Most striking: Golf Raw found no reporting, in either direction, of the LPGA, the Ladies European Tour or the Solheim Cup's organizers ever discussing payment. Not a proposal, not a rejection, not a player asking. That silence isn't evidence of a decision. It's evidence that the conversation the PGA of America had in 2024 simply hasn't happened over here.

And no Solheim Cup player has been reported commenting on the Ryder Cup change. Nelly Korda, speaking before this year's matches, described playing for something bigger than the individual as one of the most enjoyable parts of the event. That's a paraphrase from a smaller outlet, not a direct quote, and it's the closest thing to a player's view on the subject that Golf Raw could verify.

FAQ

Do Solheim Cup players get paid anything at all?

Not in prize money or appearance fees. There is no purse, no payment per point, and nothing for winning or losing. Players receive flights, accommodation, food and drink, team clothing, golf bags, gear and sponsor bags, per Golf News Net. Captains and assistant captains are unpaid too.

Do Ryder Cup players get paid?

The American ones have since 2025. The PGA of America gives each US player and the captain ,000, split into ,000 for charity and a ,000 personal stipend, replacing a ,000 charity-only allocation that had stood since 1999. European Ryder Cup players are not paid.

Who pays for the players' travel and clothing?

The event covers it. Golf News Net reports players are flown in, put up, and have food and beverage handled, with clothing, bags and gear provided for the week. No published figure exists for what that costs per player, and Golf Raw won't estimate one.

The Raw Verdict

This isn't a demand that anyone start writing checks. Every published account has the players describing the honour as the point, and the Europeans who beat the Americans at Bernardus on Sunday looked like people who'd have paid for the week rather than been paid for it. Ask the Ryder Cup's European team and you'd get the same answer.

What deserves naming is the asymmetry in the conversation, not the asymmetry in the bank transfer. An organization looked at its showcase event in 2024, decided a 25-year-old arrangement had aged badly, and changed it without a single player asking. The same review hasn't happened on this side of the sport, in public, ever. Whether the answer should be the same one is a fair argument. The fact that nobody in women's golf has been asked the question out loud is the part worth noticing.
