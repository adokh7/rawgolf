Fifteen Months Ago He Was Playing NAIA Golf. Today He's in the U.S. Amateur Final at Merion.

Jack Whaley required a 16-man playoff just to qualify for the match-play draw as the No. 60 seed. Jay Leng Jr. entered the summer with a modest 2-6-2 college match-play record. On Sunday at historic Merion Golf Club, both square off over 36 holes for the Havemeyer Trophy.

BY GOLFRAW EDITORIAL PUBLISHED SUN 16 AUG 2026 MATCH PLAY FINAL 4 MIN READ
Championship Match Play: August 16, 2026 | 126th U.S. Amateur Championship at Merion

Quick summary: The 126th U.S. Amateur Championship final at Merion Golf Club presents one of the most compelling contrasts in recent championship history. Former NAIA standout Jack Whaley, who survived a 16-man playoff as the 60th seed, faces reigning Western Amateur champion Jay Leng Jr. over 36 holes for the Havemeyer Trophy, Masters invitations, and U.S. Open berths.

Jack Whaley’s Unlikely Ascent

Fifteen months removed from competing at Dalton State College—where he won the 2025 NAIA individual national championship—22-year-old English transfer Jack Whaley has become the defining underdog of the championship. After surviving a 16-for-11 playoff on Wednesday morning, the Florida State rising senior knocked off five consecutive higher-seeded opponents across 86 grueling holes.

His run mirrors Edoardo Molinari’s 2005 triumph at Merion, where the Italian advanced via a 19-man playoff before claiming the title. If Whaley prevails, he becomes the first Englishman to lift the Havemeyer Trophy since Matt Fitzpatrick in 2013, automatically earning a spot on the 2026 Great Britain and Ireland Walker Cup squad.

"To survive a 16-man playoff at Merion and knock off five straight top seeds over 86 holes is pure match-play grit."
— USGA CHAMPIONSHIP DISPATCH

Jay Leng Jr.’s Historic Summer Surge

Stanford sophomore Jay Leng Jr., the No. 15 seed and World No. 23 amateur, arrives fresh off winning the prestigious 124th Western Amateur at Beverly Country Club. Eleven years after capturing the Boys' 7-9 division of the Drive, Chip and Putt Championship at Augusta National, Leng has established himself as an elite match-play tactician.

A victory today places Leng on an exclusive list of golfers to win both the Western Amateur and U.S. Amateur in the same calendar year—a feat shared by Tiger Woods and Jack Nicklaus.

The Brutal Test of Merion’s 18th Hole

Merion’s 501-yard par-four closing hole has defined the knockout stages, yielding just a single birdie against fifteen bogeys or worse throughout the week. Whaley closed out his semifinal against Georgia’s Carter Loflin with a five-foot bogey putt on the 18th to secure a 1-up victory.

  • Major Exemptions Secured: Both finalists have already earned exemptions into the 2027 Masters Tournament and the 2027 U.S. Open at Pebble Beach (provided they remain amateurs).
  • Walker Cup Implications: A Whaley victory seals an automatic berth for Great Britain & Ireland.
  • Morning Match Status: Leng took a 2-up lead through the opening 18-hole session entering the decisive afternoon round.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is competing in the 2026 U.S. Amateur final?

Jay Leng Jr. (Stanford, No. 15 seed) faces Jack Whaley (Florida State, No. 60 seed) in the 36-hole final at Merion Golf Club in Ardmore, Pennsylvania.

How did Jack Whaley reach the championship match?

Whaley survived a 16-man sudden-death playoff for the final match-play berths before winning five straight matches against top seeds, including an 86-hole stretch over four days.

What major exemptions do the finalists receive?

Both finalists receive invitations to the 2027 Masters Tournament at Augusta National and the 2027 U.S. Open at Pebble Beach, provided they retain amateur status.

Has an Englishman won the U.S. Amateur recently?

Matt Fitzpatrick was the last Englishman to win the championship, taking the title in 2013 at The Country Club in Brookline.

The Raw Take

While the professional game fractures over purse distributions and contractual disputes, the U.S. Amateur at Merion delivers pure competitive theater. Two young players with radically different paths battle on one of golf's ultimate cathedrals, proving that pure match play remains the sport’s most unforgiving examination.
