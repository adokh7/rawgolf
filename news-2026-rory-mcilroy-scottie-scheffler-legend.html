Scottie Scheffler Is Breaking Every PGA Tour Record, But He'll Never Catch Rory McIlroy's Legend

By Golf Raw Editorial Staff Published 09 Sep 2026 Updated 09 Sep 2026, 15:00 UTC 4 min read

As of September 2026, Scottie Scheffler is the most dominant golfer on the planet. He has 22 PGA Tour wins, four majors, two FedEx Cups, and this month passed Tiger Woods as the Tour's all-time leading money earner with more than $130 million, per the PGA Tour. Rory McIlroy has six majors, 30 Tour wins and, since the 2025 Masters completed his career Grand Slam, the biggest cultural footprint in the sport. Golf Raw's position is that Scheffler will retire with better numbers, and McIlroy will remain the bigger legend, because being a legend requires something Scheffler has said he doesn't want.

The gap between the two shows up in a poll, and in one sentence from Scheffler himself. Both are in the third section.

Golf Raw hasn't interviewed either player. Records come from the PGA Tour's results and payout data, Golf Channel's win lists, and Wikipedia's major championship entries. Popularity figures are YouGov's, as published. The judgment about legend status is ours.

The statistical case for Scheffler is airtight

Twenty-two wins in 170 starts. That's the pace, per the PGA Tour's report from East Lake.

His 2026 alone: three victories including the Tour Championship, five runner-up finishes, two thirds and three fourths, per the Tour. ESPN's season stats have him leading the Tour in strokes gained total, tee to green, greens in regulation, scrambling and scoring average. The PGA Tour's own July analysis rated his field-adjusted strokes gained as the 12th-best season since 1983, better than any single season by McIlroy, Rahm, Spieth or Dustin Johnson.

The majors are real: the 2022 and 2024 Masters, the 2025 PGA Championship, the 2025 Open Championship, per Wikipedia. The money is real too. His $10 million Tour Championship cheque took his career official earnings to $130,390,661, past Woods' $120,999,166, a mark that had stood since 2000.

He is, by any measure that involves a golf ball, historically great at hitting it. Nothing in this article disputes that, and anyone who does is wrong.

Rory's legend was built by the years he didn't win

Six majors. The last two came 11 years after the fourth.

McIlroy won the 2011 U.S. Open, the 2012 and 2014 PGA Championships and the 2014 Open Championship, then went a decade without a major, per Golf Channel's win list. The 2025 Masters ended that drought and completed the career Grand Slam. The 2026 Masters, won from a record six-shot 36-hole lead by a single stroke over Scheffler, made it back to back, per the PGA Tour.

That's the mechanism. Scheffler's story is a straight line up. McIlroy's is a decade of near-misses at Augusta, public heartbreak, a final-round collapse in 2011 that everyone remembers, and a green jacket at 35 followed by another at 36. One of those arcs produces highlight packages. The other produces mythology.

The wins list matters too. Golf Channel puts McIlroy at 30 PGA Tour titles, tied 16th all-time with Horton Smith, with three FedEx Cups. Sports Illustrated noted his 2025 Pebble Beach win made him the first player to reach 27 since Mickelson in 2005.

Math versus myth: where the gap actually shows

Here's the payoff. YouGov's 2026 ranking of the most popular contemporary golfers in America has McIlroy third, with a 29% positive opinion, and Scheffler seventh at 22%, per the package's polling source.

Seven percentage points and four places, at the peak of Scheffler's dominance, in his own country.

Then the sentence that explains it. Scheffler has said, per PGA Tour reporting in 2026, that he “doesn't care to be a superstar.” Read alongside his press conferences this year, that isn't false modesty. ESPN quoted him in July 2025 saying the feeling of winning lasts a few minutes and that golf isn't where he looks for satisfaction. After his Tour Championship win this month, per EssentiallySports' account of the transcript, he talked about wind, fighting back from slow starts, and travelling with his wife and sons.

Viktor Hovland, asked what makes Scheffler so good after losing to him at East Lake, said he's just good and played good every day, per Yahoo Sports. That's the most accurate description anyone has offered, and it's also the least quotable one in the history of the sport.

Golf Raw's reading of that table: the left column wins every row that measures golf, and loses the only row that measures how people feel. The two aren't correlated because the second one runs on narrative, and Scheffler has spent his career declining to supply any.

The claim that gets this wrong

“Scheffler is the new Tiger in every way.” That's the line, and the second half of it doesn't survive contact with the evidence.

Woods held the money list for 26 years with 82 wins in 378 starts, and CBS's Tour Championship final round this month drew its biggest audience, 5.728 million, since Woods won in 2018, per Golf Monthly. The record fell to Scheffler on a Sunday when the audience came for the finish, and the finish included McIlroy shooting 63 to get into it.

Dominance and legend overlapped in Woods. They don't in Scheffler, and the reason isn't a flaw. He's told everyone. He doesn't care to be a superstar, and a sport can't make a man into one against his will.

One honest limit. This is a comparison of cultural standing as of late 2026, not a prediction of final trophy counts. Scheffler is 30. If he wins six more majors, the numbers argument becomes overwhelming. The legend argument still doesn't move, because it was never about the numbers.

The Raw Verdict

Scheffler will likely retire with better numbers than McIlroy, and he'll be perfectly happy about it. He's said as much in every way a person can. But golf fans don't fall in love with the strokes gained leader. They fall in love with the man who lost the Masters in 2011 and won it 14 years later while half the sport cried with him.

McIlroy is the legend of this era. That was settled at Augusta in April 2025, and no number of Scheffler victories will reopen it. If you're a casual fan trying to work out who to root for, that's your answer too: watch Scheffler to learn how golf is played, and watch McIlroy to remember why you care.
