Jon Rahm's “LIV 1.0” Answer at Doonbeg: Why He Won't Say Whether He Wants Back on the PGA Tour, and Why the Door Isn't Open Anyway

By Golf Raw Editorial Staff Published 08 Sep 2026 Updated 08 Sep 2026, 23:00 UTC 4 min read

Jon Rahm declined on Tuesday to say whether he wants a pathway back to the PGA Tour, telling BBC Sport Northern Ireland at Trump International Golf Links Doonbeg that “I still have a contract with LIV 1.0 that I'm more than willing to fulfill,” per Heavy's account of the interview. Asked whether he'd return if the chance arose, he said “We'll see what happens,” per Golf.com. In his formal pre-tournament press conference ahead of the Amgen Irish Open, the only LIV question drew a four-word reply: “No updates since Indianapolis.”

The phrase worth stopping on is “LIV 1.0.” Golf.com flags it as deliberate, and the section on what that contract is worth explains why a two-word qualifier is doing so much work.

Golf Raw was not at Doonbeg and did not interview Rahm. Quotes come from BBC Sport Northern Ireland's interview as reported by Golf.com, Heavy and OutKick, and from Field Level Media's account of the press conference. Contract figures are reported estimates, not documents Golf Raw has seen.

What Rahm said, in order

Three answers to three attempts, and each one shorter than the last.

BBC Sport NI's Stephen Watson asked first about Rahm's future with LIV. OutKick's report has him saying “There's a lot of things that could happen,” and that time would tell.

Then the pathway question. Rahm's answer pointed at his existing deal and repeated that time would tell. Golf.com notes the interviewer pressed a third time, asking whether he'd want to play the PGA Tour if his contract fell through or the league went under, and got four words.

That's not evasion for its own sake. Read as a sequence, it's a player who has decided the only thing he'll confirm is that a contract exists and he intends to honour it.

Field Level Media adds that he plans to play next week's BMW PGA Championship and that he'd gone home after LIV's finale in Indianapolis before this trip.

What “LIV 1.0” is worth, and why the wording matters

Here's the payoff. Rahm didn't say LIV. He said LIV 1.0, and Golf.com's read is that the distinction is the whole message.

LIV CEO Scott O'Neil has been pitching a restructured LIV 2.0 built around player equity, with reporting from the Financial Times via Reuters describing a possible Chapter 11 filing as soon as this week and settlement offers to players at a few cents on the dollar. Golf.com's interpretation is that the new-look league may not be able to fulfil the contract Rahm signed, and that Rahm may be making the point that if LIV pays him what he's owed, he'll keep playing.

What he's owed is disputed. Yardbarker's syndicated Field Level Media report says more than $100 million remains on the deal. EssentiallySports, via Yardbarker, puts it at around $150 million and calls him the league's biggest creditor. The original contract has been reported at $300 million by Yahoo Sports, $350 million by Golfmagic, and $600 million by CBC when he signed in December 2023. No figure has been confirmed by Rahm or LIV.

Golf Raw's opinion, clearly labelled: with that much unpaid money attached to a contract he's publicly committed to honouring, saying “I want to play the PGA Tour” out loud would be a strange thing to volunteer. Not because we know his contract terms, which we don't, but because nobody negotiating with a creditor announces where they'd rather be.

The door people keep pointing at is shut

The PGA Tour's Returning Member Program was offered to four players and closed on February 2, 2026, per CBS Sports. Koepka took it. Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau and Cameron Smith publicly committed to LIV for 2026 at the league's media week instead.

PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp said in late August, per Golf.com's earlier reporting, that there are no current plans to reopen that pathway, and that any returning player must demonstrate they're free of contractual or tail obligations.

So the question Rahm was asked at Doonbeg is a question about a route that doesn't currently exist. Reports on social media claiming he's agreed 2027 terms with the Tour, including a $25 million charity payment, remain unconfirmed by anyone with a name. Golf Raw could not find any statement from Rahm or the Tour supporting them.

That's the part the “he's angling for a return” coverage skips. Even a player who wanted back tomorrow would be waiting on a policy nobody has announced.

The one deal he did sign this year

Europe kept him. That's the quiet fact underneath all of this.

Field Level Media reports Rahm struck a deal with the DP World Tour in May to retain his membership and Ryder Cup eligibility. He's at Doonbeg this week and Wentworth next week on that basis. Yahoo Sports and Golfmagic have reported that European LIV players including Rahm, Tyrrell Hatton, Sergio García and Lee Westwood were weighing their 2027 Ryder Cup eligibility as LIV's future wobbled.

Rahm told Field Level Media a year is a long time, which is about as much as he'd give on the Ryder Cup.

So the ledger for 2026 reads: a settlement with the tour that can put him on a Ryder Cup team, a contract with a league that may not survive the month, and silence toward the tour that closed its door in February. He's playing links golf in Ireland while every other party works out what it owes whom.

What's still unknown

The contract terms. Whether LIV files this week. Whether LIV 2.0 can pay what LIV 1.0 promised. Whether the PGA Tour ever writes a second return policy.

Rahm, 31, has won LIV's individual title in all three of his seasons there, per Field Level Media. He's coming off a season in which the league's finale was moved up and its Team Championship cancelled. Everything he said on Tuesday is consistent with a man who knows the answers depend on people he can't control, and said so in fewer words than the questions took.

FAQ

Has Jon Rahm agreed to return to the PGA Tour in 2027?

No announcement exists from Rahm or the PGA Tour. The Tour's Returning Member Program closed on February 2, 2026, with only Brooks Koepka using it, and CEO Brian Rolapp said in late August there are no current plans to reopen it. Social media reports of agreed terms are unconfirmed.

What does Rahm mean by “LIV 1.0”?

The contract he signed in December 2023 under the league's original, PIF-funded structure. Golf.com reads the phrasing as a signal that a restructured, equity-based LIV 2.0 might not be able to honour that deal, and that Rahm is committing to the contract rather than to whatever replaces it.

Can Rahm play the 2027 Ryder Cup?

He struck a deal with the DP World Tour in May to retain his membership and Ryder Cup eligibility, per Field Level Media. He's playing the Irish Open this week and the BMW PGA Championship next week. He said only that a year is a long time when asked about it.

The Raw Verdict

Stop reading Rahm's four-word answers as clues. A player owed a reported nine figures by a league that may be in bankruptcy court within days is not going to tell a television camera where he'd rather play. He said he'll honour the contract he signed and that time will tell. That's the answer, and it's the correct one.

The bit worth keeping is that Europe did the practical thing. It settled with him in May, kept its Ryder Cup star and put him on a links course in Clare this week. The American side closed a door in February and now asks him about it through reporters. If you want to know where Rahm plays next year, watch what LIV files in New Jersey, not what he says in Doonbeg.
