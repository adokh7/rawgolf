The PGA Tour's Answer to Detroit Is a Quarter-Inch of Grass

Detroit spent $16.1m to get harder and gave up three 61s. Sedgefield defends itself with a quarter-inch of rough and 12½-foot greens.

BY GOLFRAW EDITORIAL PUBLISHED: TUE 04 AUG 2026

Last week the Tour played a Donald Ross par 70 that had just swallowed $16.1 million trying to become harder. It gave up three rounds of 61 and a winning total that tied the lowest in the tournament's history.

This week it plays another Donald Ross par 70, two hundred yards shorter, that has had almost nothing done to it.

The difference in how Sedgefield Country Club is being protected? The rough is a quarter of an inch longer than last year, and the greens are running at twelve and a half feet.

That's it. That's the whole defence budget.

What's actually at stake

The Wyndham is the last event of the PGA Tour's regular season. The top 70 in FedExCup points on Sunday night go to Memphis for the playoffs. Everyone else is done until the autumn.

Two leaderboards, then. One decides who wins a golf tournament. The other decides who still has a season.

Jackson Koivun sits exactly 70th. He's 21, he won the 3M Open two weeks ago for his first Tour title, and he couldn't build any cushion in Detroit. Andrew Putnam slipped to 73rd after missing the cut there. Johnny Keefer is 74th, having also gone home early. Jason Day is in that same neighbourhood.

Everyone ranked 61 to 125 is in the field, which tells you exactly who needed to show up. Only one player in that range skipped it.

But let's be honest about the drama

The previews will tell you the whole week is a knife fight. It isn't, quite.

The top 59 are already mathematically safe. They cannot be caught. That's why only three players from the world's top 20 bothered to come — Cameron Young, Ryan Gerard and Alex Fitzpatrick — and Young's here because he's defending a title he won for the first time last year.

So the tension involves about eleven playoff spots and the players genuinely fighting for them. Which is plenty. The whole field is not having a nervous breakdown, whatever the trailer says.

Though I'll give the Tour this: technically, everyone who committed down to Webb Simpson at 181st is still mathematically alive. Simpson won here in 2011 and is the tournament's all-time leading money winner. He'd need something absurd. It's still nice that the arithmetic allows it.

The bit of trivia I enjoyed most

The playoff field is 70 players. Unless it's 71.

There's a provision in the Returning Member Program that would expand it if Brooks Koepka, currently 86th, climbs into the bubble. A five-time major champion could end up as the reason the field has an extra locker.

Golf's rulebooks are full of these. Somebody sat in a room and wrote a clause for exactly this situation, years before it nearly happened.

Back to the grass, because this is the real story

Detroit Golf Club is a Ross course. Sedgefield is a Ross course. Both par 70. Detroit plays 7,328 yards. Sedgefield plays 7,131.

Detroit spent $16.1 million on rebuilt greens, deeper bunkers, seven new ones, fresh fescue, new drainage, and converted two par 5s into par 4s to cut par from 72 to 70. Three players shot 61 during the week and the winner tied the lowest 72-hole total in tournament history.

Sedgefield is nearly two hundred yards shorter, has had nothing built, and is known for low scoring — so the Tour grew the rough a quarter-inch and rolled the greens to twelve and a half.

I'm not saying Sedgefield will play harder. It probably won't; it's a birdie course and always has been. What I'm saying is that the two levers available to anyone who wants to protect a golf course are construction and conditioning, and only one of them costs sixteen million dollars.

Cameron Young, incidentally, shot 61 at Detroit on Friday. Now he defends at Sedgefield. Same architect, same par, two hundred yards, one quarter-inch of grass.

What this means for your golf

Ask your greenkeeper for speed before you ask your committee for a bulldozer. Every club that wants to be "more of a test" starts talking about new tees. The cheapest and most effective changes are green speed, firmness, and where the rough line sits. They can be reversed on Tuesday if everyone hates them.

A quarter of an inch is a lot. It doesn't sound like anything. It's the difference between a lie you can spin a wedge from and one you can only hack out. Next time you're annoyed at how a course is playing, look at the grass rather than the yardage board.

Twelve and a half feet is quick. Most club greens run somewhere between eight and ten. If you've ever played a course after a competition setup and wondered why you three-putted everything, that's the number that changed, and nothing else did.

Short doesn't mean easy, and long doesn't mean hard. 7,131 yards for a professional is nothing. The course still produces a champion every year because it asks questions that aren't about distance. Your own course is the same. The back tees are usually the least interesting way to make it difficult.

FAQ

What is the Wyndham Championship and why does it matter?
It's the final event of the PGA Tour's regular season, played at Sedgefield Country Club in Greensboro, North Carolina, from 6-9 August. The top 70 in FedExCup points afterwards advance to the playoffs; everyone else's season ends.

Who is on the FedExCup bubble in 2026?
Jackson Koivun holds the 70th and final spot entering the week. Andrew Putnam is 73rd, Johnny Keefer 74th, with Jason Day nearby. The top 59 are already mathematically safe.

What does the winner get?
500 FedExCup points, $1.53 million, a two-year Tour exemption, and places in the 2027 Players Championship, Masters and PGA Championship. The trophy is the Sam Snead Cup.

What kind of course is Sedgefield Country Club?
A Donald Ross design playing to par 70 at 7,131 yards, on the schedule as the regular-season finale since 2008. It's known for low scoring. This year the rough is up a quarter of an inch to 2¾ inches and the greens are set to run at 12½ feet on the Stimpmeter.

Could the playoff field be more than 70 players?
Yes. A Returning Member Program provision would expand it to 71 if Brooks Koepka, currently 86th, climbs into the bubble.
