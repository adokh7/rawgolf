Rocket Classic Odds Outlook: Cameron Young Tops the Board, but Detroit Is a New Test

BY GOLFRAW EDITORIAL PUBLISHED MON 27 JUL 2026

Cameron Young is the confirmed betting favorite for the 2026 Rocket Classic, but there is no single universal price attached to him.

Published sportsbook snapshots from July 26 and 27 listed Young between +980 and +1200. FanDuel had him at +1200 when the PGA Tour published its odds outlook, while Hard Rock Bet listed +1100 and a DraftKings snapshot showed +980.

Those differences matter. They also illustrate why saying a player is “on top of the odds board” does not tell bettors whether the available price offers value.

Young has an obvious case for favoritism. He is No. 3 in the world, third in the FedExCup standings and a two-time PGA Tour winner in 2026. He also arrives after finishing second at The Open Championship.

The complication is Detroit Golf Club.

The course has undergone a $16 million renovation, shifted from a par 72 to a par 70, restored every green to its Donald Ross-inspired shape and added seven bunkers. The new configuration means historical results remain relevant, but they are not directly comparable with the course players faced before 2026.

Young is the player to beat according to the market. Whether he is the player to bet depends on the price, the rebuilt course and how much confidence should be placed in his previous Detroit results.

Is Cameron Young really the Rocket Classic favorite?

Yes. Every current market source reviewed for this article placed Young at the top of its outright winner board.

The precise price varied substantially:

Sportsbook or published source Young’s price Date published Implied probability
DraftKings snapshot +980 July 26 9.26%
Hard Rock Bet +1100 July 27 8.33%
FanDuel via PGA Tour +1200 July 27 7.69%

The probabilities are simple conversions from American odds and do not remove the sportsbook’s margin. They should not be treated as objective forecasts of Young’s chance of winning.

They demonstrate two important points.

First, even the tournament favorite had a market-implied probability below 10% in these snapshots. Outright golf markets are inherently volatile because one player must defeat the rest of a full PGA Tour field across four rounds.

Second, shopping for a price can materially change a potential return. A successful $100 bet at +980 would produce $980 in profit. The same stake at +1200 would produce $1,200—a difference of $220.

Odds can change without warning. Readers should verify the current line at a legal sportsbook before making any decision.

Why Cameron Young leads the odds board

Young’s position is supported by current performance, not merely reputation.

He has won twice during the 2026 season, capturing The Players Championship and the Cadillac Championship. He then finished second to Ryan Fox at The Open Championship, closing with a 64 and forcing Fox to make a 12-foot putt on the final hole.

Young has also recorded eight top-10 finishes in his past 20 major appearances, according to the PGA Tour.

His statistical profile appears compatible with the renovated Detroit layout. Young ranks fourth on the PGA Tour in par-four scoring at 3.96. That matters because the new configuration contains only two par-five holes, down from four in previous editions.

Detroit is not an unfamiliar venue for him either.

Young finished tied for second behind Tony Finau in 2022 and tied for sixth in 2024. Those results strengthen his overall case, but they need to be interpreted carefully because both came before the course renovation.

The best argument for Young is therefore not simply “he has played well here before.” It is the combination of elite current form, power, strong par-four scoring and prior comfort on the property.

The most important betting change: Detroit Golf Club is different

Much of the early Rocket Classic coverage emphasizes historical course performance. That approach carries more risk than usual in 2026.

Detroit Golf Club has completed the first competitive debut of a major renovation oversaw by architect Tyler Rae. According to the PGA Tour’s tournament preview, the work included:

  • Restoring every green to its original Donald Ross shape
  • Increasing the bunker total from 84 to 91
  • Rebuilding and reshaping greens
  • Updating tee boxes, irrigation and tree placement
  • Converting the seventh and 17th holes into par fours
  • Reducing the total number of par fives from four to two
  • Removing the pond in front of the 14th green and replacing it with native grass

The seventh will play as a 505-yard par four, while the 17th is listed at 537 yards. Both should place additional stress on driving and long-iron play.

The renovation also changed the scorecard from a 7,370-yard par 72 in 2025 to a 7,328-yard par 70 in 2026.

That does not make previous Detroit results useless. Players still face familiar sightlines, terrain and parts of the routing. It does make raw historical scores and repeated claims about “proven course fit” less reliable.

A player who finished at 20-under on the old par-72 course did not face the same scoring framework or green complexes that the 2026 field will encounter.

What skills should matter at the renovated course?

No competitive PGA Tour data exist yet for the renovated layout. Any course-fit projection therefore remains an informed estimate rather than a proven model.

The changes suggest four areas deserve particular attention.

Par-four performance

With two former par fives converted into long par fours, the field will face 12 par-four holes.

Players who consistently avoid bogeys and create opportunities across demanding par fours may have an advantage. Young’s 3.96 par-four scoring average is one reason his market position is defensible.

Approach play

Every green has been restored, so players will encounter putting surfaces with shapes and target areas that differ from recent editions.

Strong approach players should be better equipped to find the correct sections and avoid leaving difficult recoveries. This strengthens the cases for players such as Si Woo Kim and Russell Henley.

Driving quality, not only distance

Length remains valuable on a 7,328-yard par-70 course, especially at the 505-yard seventh and 537-yard 17th.

Distance alone will not be enough. The addition and repositioning of bunkers can punish aggressive lines, while restored greens may place a greater premium on approaching from the correct side of the fairway.

Adaptability

Every member of the field is effectively learning parts of the course again.

Players making their tournament debut may be at less of a disadvantage than they would have been before the renovation. Conversely, experienced Detroit players will need to avoid relying too heavily on old targets and strategies.

Leading 2026 Rocket Classic contenders

The following odds were among the prices published on July 27. They are included as market context, not guaranteed live lines.

Cameron Young: approximately +980 to +1200

Young owns the strongest overall combination of form, ranking, power and previous Detroit performance.

The principal concern is price. Backing any player near 10-1 in a full-field event requires a high degree of confidence, particularly when the course has no competitive data in its current form.

Young is a logical favorite. That does not automatically make him an attractive wager at every number.

Xander Schauffele: approximately +1600 to +1800

Schauffele is making his Rocket Classic debut, but that matters less following the renovation.

The two-time major winner has five top-10 finishes in 2026 and ranks 15th in the FedExCup standings. His balanced game makes him a credible contender on a course expected to demand more than pure birdie accumulation.

The risk is that the price reflects his established reputation even though he has not yet competed on the property.

Chris Gotterup: approximately +1700 to +1800

Gotterup has already won three times in 2026 and enters as the seventh-ranked player in the world.

His previous Rocket Classic results are less persuasive—his best finish in three appearances was tied for 26th in 2025. The renovation gives bettors a reasonable basis to reduce the importance of that record.

Gotterup offers current winning form and power, but his price is close enough to Schauffele’s that bettors must decide whether recent victories outweigh the difference in long-term consistency.

Jackson Koivun: approximately +1750 to +2600

Koivun arrives after winning the 3M Open at 25-under in only his third start as a professional. He held off world No. 1 Scottie Scheffler by three strokes and moved to No. 70 in the FedExCup standings.

His price differed dramatically across early boards. Hard Rock Bet listed +1750, FanDuel was reported at +2000, and DraftKings published +2600.

That range reflects uncertainty surrounding a highly talented but inexperienced player. It also makes Koivun one of the clearest examples of why comparing prices is essential.

Koivun missed the cut at Detroit as an amateur in 2024. Given his development since then and the course renovation, that result should carry limited predictive weight.

Wyndham Clark: approximately +2000

Clark enters after an outstanding summer.

The 2026 U.S. Open champion recorded six consecutive finishes of 15th or better during a run that included two victories. He is ninth in the world and fourth in the FedExCup standings.

Clark’s Detroit record is mixed: tied for 17th in 2019, tied for eighth in 2022 and a missed cut in 2025. His current form is more relevant than that uneven history.

The question is whether the market has already fully priced in his recent surge.

Si Woo Kim: approximately +1850 to +2000

Kim presents one of the strongest statistical cases among the leading alternatives to Young.

He ranks third in Strokes Gained: Approach and fifth in Strokes Gained: Total, according to the PGA Tour. He has 10 top-10 finishes in 2026, one fewer than Scheffler, and finished tied for sixth at The Open.

Kim has made the cut in each of his four previous Detroit appearances, although his best finish was tied for 14th in 2022.

His approach numbers may be particularly relevant on the restored greens. The drawback is that he has not won since the 2023 Sony Open.

Patrick Cantlay: approximately +2150 to +2250

Cantlay finished tied for second in his Detroit debut in 2022 before placing tied for 32nd in 2025.

He is 43rd in the FedExCup standings and has not won since the 2022 BMW Championship. A victory would provide a major boost toward qualifying for the Tour Championship.

Cantlay’s overall ability is not in doubt. His putting has been the principal concern during 2026, and the newly restored greens add another unknown.

Russell Henley: approximately +2500

Henley may be one of the more interesting course-fit cases.

He won the Charles Schwab Challenge earlier in 2026, finished tied for 12th at the Travelers Championship and tied for ninth at The Open. He also ranks first on Tour in fairways hit.

Accurate driving and reliable approach play could become more valuable following the renovation. Henley finished tied for 10th in his most recent Detroit appearance in 2022.

His comparative lack of distance is the obvious concern on the two newly created long par fours.

What about defending champion Aldrich Potgieter?

Potgieter was listed around +10000 after winning the 2025 tournament in a five-hole playoff over Max Greyserman and Chris Kirk.

He became the sixth-youngest PGA Tour winner of the previous decade when he captured the title at 20 years, nine months and 16 days.

His defending-champion status creates an appealing narrative, but the course where he won is not identical to the one he will defend. Potgieter also enters with only two PGA Tour top-10 finishes during the 2026 season.

The 100-1 price recognizes both his power and the substantial uncertainty surrounding his form.

Other former champions in the field include two-time winner Cam Davis, 2023 champion Rickie Fowler and 2022 champion Tony Finau.

What most Rocket Classic previews are missing

The dominant preview narrative is straightforward: Young is in excellent form, owns two strong Detroit finishes and deserves to be favorite.

That conclusion is reasonable, but incomplete.

Three details deserve greater emphasis.

First, the course has changed enough that older results should be discounted. Every green was restored, two par fives became par fours and the bunker layout changed.

Second, Young’s price varied from +980 to +1200 across published snapshots. A bettor can agree that Young is the most likely winner while still rejecting a short price.

Third, no player has a high absolute probability of winning. Even a +1000 favorite carries a raw implied probability of only 9.09% before removing the sportsbook’s margin.

The most defensible outlook is therefore conditional: Young deserves to lead the market, but the renovated course and price variation make certainty impossible.

Is Cameron Young worth backing?

The evidence supports Young as the tournament’s most likely individual winner. It does not establish that every available price represents value.

A bettor who estimates Young’s true win probability at 10% would consider +1200 attractive but +980 much less compelling. Someone who estimates his chance at 7% would reject both prices.

That personal probability estimate is the part an odds board cannot provide.

Young’s current form, par-four scoring and overall skill set all support his position. His previous Detroit finishes offer additional reassurance, but they should carry less weight after the renovation.

The best conclusion is not “bet Young” or “fade Young.” It is that the decision changes with the number.

GOLFRAW's Take

Cameron Young deserves to be the Rocket Classic favorite. He arrives with two victories in 2026, a runner-up finish at The Open, elite par-four scoring and two strong previous results in Detroit. No other player combines those elements as convincingly.

The price is a separate question. Published odds between +980 and +1200 show that bettors can reach very different value conclusions while evaluating the same player. The difference matters even more in a full-field tournament where the favorite’s implied probability remains below 10%.

Detroit Golf Club’s renovation adds another layer of uncertainty. Previous course history still matters, but it should be treated as supporting evidence rather than the foundation of a forecast. Young sits on top of the board for good reason. The smartest part of the outlook is recognizing that being the favorite and being a good bet are not automatically the same thing.
