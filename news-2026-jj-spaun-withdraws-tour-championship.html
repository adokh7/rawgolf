J.J. Spaun Withdraws From the Tour Championship: What Actually Happened

Bogey, double bogey, bogey. Then a tee shot on the sixth and a walk to the cart. That was the "stunning last-minute decision," and it was neither stunning nor much of a decision.

BY GOLFRAW EDITORIAL PUBLISHED WED 02 SEP 2026

J.J. Spaun withdrew from the 2026 Tour Championship on August 27 after five holes of the first round with a right shoulder injury. He was 4 over at the time. The withdrawal left him 30th in the final FedExCup standings, worth $355,000, and reduced the East Lake field to 29 players with no alternate added.

One thing that summary can't show you is what the withdrawal cost him five days later, when Brandt Snedeker named his Presidents Cup picks. That's in the fifth section.

Built on: the PGA Tour's own report from East Lake, Golf Channel and ESPN coverage of the withdrawal, NBC Sports' season summary, USA Today reporting via Golfweek, and Golf Monthly's roundup of the finalised Presidents Cup rosters.

The five holes before he stopped

Four over. Three dropped shots in three holes, and a swing he couldn't complete.

The PGA Tour's report has Spaun going bogey at the 2nd, double bogey at the 3rd and bogey at the 4th, then hitting his tee shot on the par-5 6th before calling it. Golf Channel quotes him saying it's just hard to complete a backswing right now.

He'd been paired with Min Woo Lee, who finished the round alone and shot 62.

That's the whole event. A man with a bad shoulder tried three holes of tournament golf, dropped four shots, and stopped before he made it worse. Any weekend player who's ever tried to swing through a back spasm recognises the arithmetic.

Where the injury came from

A sharp pain near his right shoulder blade on Tuesday morning. His own theory was a bad night's sleep.

The PGA Tour's report has Spaun explaining he woke Tuesday with the pain, that it had happened before but never persisted this long, and that he'd felt the same thing at last year's Open Championship. He and his physio worked through it and hit balls Tuesday, at which point it flared badly enough that he couldn't play any holes.

USA Today's reporting, via Golfweek, attributes his own explanation to a poor sleeping position. He said he'd tried to push through it, but the torque and force in a golf swing made it impossible to compete at the level required.

He told reporters he wished the tournament had started a day or a week later, and that a few days off should sort it.

Why he still got paid

Thirty-first place doesn't exist at East Lake. The 30th-place cheque is $355,000, and multiple outlets report it went to Spaun.

Golf Channel, Golfmagic and Last Word On Sports all state he finishes 30th in the FedExCup and earns $355,000 despite completing five holes. NBC Sports confirms the 30th-place finish.

One honest wrinkle. The PGA Tour's own published payout list from Sunday itemised 29 players and totalled $39,645,000, exactly $355,000 short of the $40 million purse. The reporting says Spaun receives it. The Tour's table doesn't show it. Both are true as published, and the discrepancy is the Tour's to explain.

The reason nobody replaced him comes from the Tour's playoff rules, which state that when a qualified player can't compete, the field is shortened and no alternate is added. Thirty became 29. Nobody from 31st got a call.

What the season looked like before Thursday

One win, four top-10s, 15 cuts in 22 starts. That's NBC Sports' summary.

The win was the Valero Texas Open in April, his third PGA Tour title and his first since the 2025 U.S. Open at Oakmont. EssentiallySports puts his 2026 earnings at just over $5 million entering the week.

He came into the playoffs ranked 22nd and had shared the first-round lead at the BMW Championship, where he celebrated his 36th birthday, before weekend rounds of 72 and 73 dropped him to T22. He was T34 at the FedEx St. Jude.

World No. 16, per Golf Channel. A U.S. Ryder Cup debut at Bethpage Black last autumn.

That's not a player in decline. It's a player who had one rough weekend and then woke up on a Tuesday with a shoulder that wouldn't turn.

The cost came five days later

He wasn't picked. That's the part the breaking-news posts never got round to.

Golf Channel and ESPN both reported that Spaun sat 10th on the U.S. Presidents Cup points list entering the week and was in contention for one of Snedeker's six captain's picks. He said as much himself, hoping the captain would consider him despite what he called a huge letdown of a week.

Golf Monthly's roundup published the finalised roster after Snedeker's announcement on Tuesday evening. The six picks: Patrick Cantlay, Xander Schauffele, Justin Thomas, and debutants Jacob Bridgeman, Chris Gotterup and Jackson Koivun.

No Spaun.

Nobody has published Snedeker's reasoning, and I'm not going to invent it. What I'll say is that a captain choosing between a healthy player and one who couldn't finish a backswing five days earlier isn't making a hard call, and Spaun would have known that walking off the sixth tee.

Five things about this withdrawal that don't hold up

The story has been dressed up beyond what happened.

  • "A stunning last-minute decision." He woke with the injury on Tuesday, couldn't play holes after a range session that day, and stopped after five competitive holes on Thursday. That's a two-day medical situation, not a shock.
  • "He quit because he was playing badly." He said explicitly he didn't want to make a naive decision by pushing through to finish 30th, and that he couldn't complete a backswing. Four over in five holes was the symptom.
  • "Withdrawing forfeits the money." He receives the 30th-place share of $355,000, per Golf Channel, Golfmagic and Last Word On Sports, though the Tour's own payout table didn't itemise it.
  • "Someone else got his spot." Nobody did. The Tour's playoff rules add no alternate. Lee played Thursday alone and Cameron Young played Friday alone.
  • "His Presidents Cup chances are still alive." They ended Tuesday. Snedeker's six picks, published by Golf Monthly, don't include him.

FAQ

Why did J.J. Spaun withdraw from the Tour Championship?

A right shoulder injury. He woke on Tuesday of tournament week with sharp pain near his right shoulder blade, which he attributed to a poor sleeping position, and it flared badly during a range session. He withdrew after five holes on Thursday, four over par, saying he couldn't complete a backswing.

Did Spaun still get paid?

Yes, according to Golf Channel, Golfmagic and other outlets, which report he finishes 30th in the FedExCup and earns $355,000. The PGA Tour's own published payout table listed only the 29 players who completed 72 holes, leaving that $355,000 unitemised.

Did anyone replace him in the field?

No. Under the PGA Tour's playoff rules, the field is shortened rather than filled when a qualified player can't compete, so the Tour Championship continued with 29 players. Min Woo Lee finished Thursday's round alone and shot 62. Cameron Young played alone on Friday and also shot 62.

Did Spaun make the Presidents Cup team?

No. He sat 10th on the U.S. points list and was considered a candidate for a captain's pick, but Brandt Snedeker's six selections, announced on September 1, were Patrick Cantlay, Xander Schauffele, Justin Thomas, Jacob Bridgeman, Chris Gotterup and Jackson Koivun.

How was Spaun's 2026 season?

One win at the Valero Texas Open, four top-10s and 15 cuts made in 22 starts, per NBC Sports, with earnings just over $5 million entering the Tour Championship. He shared the first-round lead at the BMW Championship before finishing T22, and entered East Lake ranked 22nd in the FedExCup.

The Raw Verdict

A man walked off a golf course because his shoulder wouldn't let him swing, collected the money his season had already earned, and lost a team spot he was probably going to lose anyway the moment he couldn't finish a backswing. There's no shocker in that, whatever the caps-lock headlines want.

The useful part for you is the sentence he said about not making a naive decision by pushing through. Most amateurs finish the round with the bad back, the sore wrist, the knee that's gone, because quitting feels like losing. He finished 30th and got paid. You'd finish 18 and get eight weeks of physio.
