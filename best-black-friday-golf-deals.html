Here's the short version. Black Friday 2026 falls on November 27, with Cyber Monday on November 30. The best club deals are on models about to be replaced, which in November means most 2025 drivers and any 2026 line that already has a successor. Consumables like balls and gloves are fine to buy any time a discount appears. Newly released tech rarely discounts, but the generation it replaced does.

Does Black Friday actually beat January for golf clubs?

For clubs, Black Friday and the January launch window are the two annual discount peaks, and they're close. Consumer pricing analysis at DontPayFull describes three windows every year: post-holiday clearance in January and February, end-of-season clearance in October and November, and Black Friday itself, with the deepest club markdowns arriving in mid to late January when new models are announced.

The reason is the launch calendar. GolfPass's 2026 preview and Golfers Authority both describe the same pattern: TaylorMade, PING, Titleist and Callaway release flagship drivers and irons between mid-January and early February, timed around the PGA Merchandise Show. PING runs its G-series on a two-year cycle, so G430 arrived in January 2023 and G440 in January 2025.

That's why the outgoing model is cheap in November. Retailers know what's coming.

One honest caveat on 2026. Reporting from Next2New Golf says TaylorMade will not launch a new driver model for the first time since 2001, which would leave Callaway and Cobra as the only major brands on annual driver cycles. I couldn't confirm that with TaylorMade directly. If it holds, Qi4D discounts this Black Friday will be shallower than a normal outgoing TaylorMade flagship.

What actually goes on sale, category by category

Drivers and fairway woods. The discounts arrive before the replacement does, and this year they arrived early. MyGolfSpy reported PING cutting G440 MAX, LST and SFT drivers, fairway woods and hybrids to $449 roughly five months ahead of a January replacement, earlier than PING's historical pattern. The G440 K, which launched in January 2026, was excluded and isn't due for replacement until 2028. Titleist took up to $200 off GT drivers back in January 2026, per Golf.com, and its own GTS2 successor reached tour in April.

Callaway's 2025 Elyte line has an official price drop across drivers, fairways, irons and hybrids, listed on Callaway's own site. The successor, the Callaway Quantum driver, released in August 2026. That makes Elyte the textbook Black Friday club: a full generation behind, officially discounted, and still current enough that shafts and adjustability match what a fitter would recommend today.

Golf Monthly's December 2025 roundup showed the same dynamic last year, with the Elyte driver at $499.99 on Amazon against a $619.99 list and eleven outgoing drivers discounted ahead of 2026 launches. Expect the 2026 version of that list to feature Elyte and G440 heavily.

Rangefinders and GPS. New tech doesn't discount in November. The previous generation does. Bushnell released the Tour V7 Shift in 2026 at the same $399.99 list price as the outgoing Tour V6 Shift, per PlayBetter's testing summary and The Hackers Paradise. The V6 Shift is the model to watch for a markdown.

Balls, gloves, bags, shoes. These are the safest Black Friday buys because nothing is about to make them obsolete. The reference page's own 2025 list was dominated by apparel, shoes and consumables for exactly that reason. Golf Monthly's 2025 tracking noted a TaylorMade TP5 four-for-three deal. I found no Tier 1 evidence that November reliably beats the spring ball promotions, so buy balls when the price is right rather than waiting for a specific date.

Is last year's driver actually worse?

Not by any measure that shows up on a scorecard. MyGolfSpy's analysis from May 2026 found the Titleist GT3, a driver launched in August 2024 and selling for $449, had five PGA Tour wins in the season's first 18 events. The next-best models, the PING G440 LST, TaylorMade Qi4D and Callaway Ai Smoke TD Max, had two each.

A driver more than a generation old out-won every newer model on the biggest stage in the sport. The PGA Tour Superstore's own buying guide makes the retail version of the same point: previous-generation clubs from a current flagship line keep the carbon crowns and high-MOI designs and drop in price once the new one lands.

Consumer pricing analysis puts previous-year models at 85 to 90 percent of current performance while retailing 25 to 50 percent lower. Treat those figures as a rough guide, not a measurement.

The one check before buying any discounted club

A cheap driver and a legal driver aren't always the same thing. Rule 4.1a of the Rules of Golf requires conforming clubs, and the USGA maintains a List of Conforming Driver Heads within its equipment database, updated every Monday. Any current model from a major brand is on it. A bargain-bin driver from a brand you don't recognize, or one advertising extra spring in the face, may not be.

Model Local Rule G-1 lets a committee require your driver head to appear on that list, which the USGA recommends only for elite competitions. Driver heads made before 1999 are exempt from that condition. The USGA is blunt that using conforming clubs is the player's responsibility.

The same applies to rangefinders. Rule 4.3a(1) allows distance-measuring devices unless a committee prohibits them, but the slope and wind functions must be switched off, with a two-stroke penalty for a first breach in stroke play. Any rangefinder you buy for competition needs a physical slope switch.

Which Black Friday golf deals fit which golfer

  • If you slice and want a driver: buy the outgoing draw-biased model, not the new one. Elyte X and G440 SFT are the two most relevant 2025 heads, and both carry official price drops.
  • If you already hit it straight: the neutral Elyte, G440 MAX or Titleist GT2 at $449 are the value plays. Skip the Triple Diamond or LST low-spin models unless a fitter told you to buy one.
  • If you play competitions: a rangefinder with a slope switch is the best money you'll spend this Black Friday. The outgoing Tour V6 Shift is the candidate.
  • If you're brand new: a full boxed set discounted on Black Friday beats a $449 driver you can't yet hit. The reference page's list was full of package sets. Buy one and get lessons with what you saved.
  • If you're waiting on a fitting: wait. Nothing on Black Friday beats the right shaft and loft, and January prices on the same clubs are close enough that a fitting in December costs you nothing.

What to skip on Black Friday

  • Anything that launched in 2026. The G440 K, the Tour V7 Shift, the Titleist GTS line and the Callaway Quantum drivers are a year from their first real markdown. A "deal" on any of them in November is a small percentage off a full price.
  • A driver without a fitting if you swing under about 90 mph. Loft and shaft matter more than model, and no discount fixes the wrong ones.
  • Any club from an unfamiliar brand promising extra distance. Check the USGA conforming list first. If it isn't there, it isn't a deal.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is Black Friday 2026?

Friday, November 27, 2026, the day after Thanksgiving in the United States, with Cyber Monday following on November 30. Retailer promotions typically start earlier in November and run through Cyber Monday. Golf-specific sales at PGA TOUR Superstore, Amazon and manufacturer sites usually mirror that window.

Are Black Friday golf deals better than January sales?

They're similar for clubs, with January often slightly deeper because new models arrive then. Black Friday's advantage is selection, since shafts and lofts on outgoing models sell out by February. If you want a specific configuration, November is safer. If you only care about the lowest number, January edges it.

Do golf balls go on sale for Black Friday?

Some do, usually as multi-dozen bundles rather than straight discounts. Golf Monthly's 2025 tracking noted a TaylorMade TP5 four-for-three offer. Manufacturers also run spring promotions, and no reliable evidence shows November consistently beating those. Buy when the price is good rather than waiting for a date.

Is a discounted older driver still legal?

If it's on the USGA's List of Conforming Driver Heads, yes. Every current major-brand model is. Rule 4.1a requires conforming clubs, and the USGA updates the list weekly. Older heads from major brands are generally listed, but always check an unfamiliar brand or any club marketed as high-COR or non-conforming.

Where to start

Decide what you're actually buying before the sales start. If it's a driver, pick the outgoing model that matches your miss and confirm the loft and flex you'd want from a fitting. If it's a rangefinder, look for the generation that just got replaced and make sure it has a slope switch. If it's balls, gloves or a bag, buy the moment the price looks right.

And if your current driver is a couple of years old and you hit it fine, the most useful thing you can do on November 27 is nothing. The GT3 won five tournaments this year at $449. Your two-year-old driver is closer to that than to obsolete. Check the current price on the two picks above if you've decided you need one. Otherwise put the money into rounds.
