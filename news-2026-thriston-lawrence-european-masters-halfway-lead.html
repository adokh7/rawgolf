Thriston Lawrence Leads the Omega European Masters at Halfway, Chasing a Record Only Seve Holds

He hadn't finished inside a top 10 since March. He'd missed the cut by a single shot four or five times this year. Then he went round Crans-sur-Sierre twice without making a bogey.

By GOLFRAW Editorial Published 04 Sep 2026 Updated 04 Sep 2026, 23:20 CEST 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • 36 holes without a bogey: Thriston Lawrence leads by two at 13 under after rounds of 64 and 63.
  • Chasing Seve Ballesteros: A third title would make him only the second player since 1972 to win three times at Crans-Montana.
  • Nacho Elvira & Jeremy Paul in pursuit: Both share second at 11 under, with Elvira signing for a week-low 62.
  • Cut fell at 3 under: Nicolai Højgaard, Miguel Ángel Jiménez and Jorge Campillo were among those to miss the weekend.
  • Altitude effect: Crans-sur-Sierre sits above 1,500m, where thinner air reduces drag and boosts carry across the field.

He hadn't finished inside a top 10 since March. He'd missed the cut by a single shot four or five times this year. Then he went round Crans-sur-Sierre twice without making a bogey.

Thriston Lawrence leads the 2026 Omega European Masters by two shots at 13 under after a second-round 63 at Crans-sur-Sierre. The defending champion has played 36 holes without a bogey. Jeremy Paul and Nacho Elvira share second at 11 under, with Keita Nakajima at 10 under. The cut fell at 3 under.

A third win here would put him alongside Seve Ballesteros in a very short list. What that summary hides is how narrow the list actually is, and why the record is harder to reach than it sounds. Fourth section.

Built on: Golf Today's second-round report and full leaderboard from Crans-sur-Sierre, the DP World Tour's own tournament preview and 2025 champion report, Today's Golfer's tournament data, and Wikipedia's Omega European Masters records.

The round that built the lead

Seven birdies, no bogeys, and one shot from a bunker that saved the whole thing.

Golf Today's report from Crans has Lawrence starting on the 10th tee and making his first birdie at the par-3 13th, passing up chances at the two par 5s that followed. Then six consecutive birdies across the turn took him clear of the field.

The bogey-free card needed rescuing once. He found a greenside bunker at the 5th and chipped in for par, then played his last four holes in par figures for a 63 and 13 under.

Lawrence told reporters he'd played well all year but been unfortunate, missing cuts by one shot four or five times, and that he's in a better mental space with putts dropping.

That's a man describing a season most club golfers would recognise. Fine golf, nothing to show for it, and then one week where the putter turns up.

Who's chasing him

Two players at 11 under, and one of them shot the lowest round of the week.

Nacho Elvira signed for a 62 with nine birdies and a single bogey, including chip-ins at the 3rd and 9th before four straight gains on his back nine. He was runner-up at the British Masters last week.

Jeremy Paul added a 64 on tournament debut, recovering from bogeys at the 7th and 9th with a chip-in eagle at the 15th and a closing nine of 29. He has his wife caddying this week and credited their relaxed approach.

Keita Nakajima's bogey-free 63 put him at 10 under. Ashun Wu, who led after round one with a 63, sits at 9 under alongside Rafa Cabrera Bello and Paul Casey.

Cabrera Bello's week is the odd one. Golf Today notes he played the opening 36 holes with a makeshift set including irons belonging to Adam Scott.

The record he's playing for

Two wins here already, 2022 and 2025. A third would make it two men in fifty-plus years.

Golf Today reports that another victory would make Lawrence only the second player, after Seve Ballesteros, to win three times at Crans-Montana since the event joined the DP World Tour schedule in 1972. He'd also become the sixth three-time champion across the tournament's 103-year history.

The Golfing Hub dates Ballesteros' three wins to 1977, 1978 and 1989.

Here's why the record is harder than it looks. This is the 79th edition at Crans-Montana and the 91st overall, per the DP World Tour, and in all that time exactly five men have won it three times. Only one has managed it in the modern era.

Lawrence is 29. He has five DP World Tour titles.

What's happened to the rest of the field

The cut fell at 3 under, and it took some names with it.

Nicolai Højgaard missed the weekend at 3 over. So did Miguel Ángel Jiménez, the 2010 champion, who Golf Circus notes was playing his 36th appearance here weeks before his 63rd birthday. Jorge Campillo, Connor Syme and Ryan Peake also went home.

Harry Hall's day tells its own story. He reached 10 under through nine holes before a hip injury disrupted his round, and three bogeys over his last five holes left him at 7 under, level with leading Swiss player Joel Girrbach.

Race to Dubai leader Patrick Reed sits 7 under, six back. Sergio García shot 63 on Friday to reach the same mark. Matt Wallace, the 2024 champion and last year's runner-up, is 6 under.

A cut at 3 under on a par 70 tells you everything about the scoring conditions up here.

Why the scores are so low at Crans

Altitude, and it hands everybody the same gift.

The DP World Tour describes altitude as the biggest on-course factor affecting players at Crans-sur-Sierre, which sits above 1,500 metres. Thinner air means less drag, so the ball flies further and curves less for every player in the field.

Today's Golfer lists the course at par 70 and 6,830 yards. ESPN's tournament page says 6,808. Two sources, 22 yards apart, and at this elevation neither number describes how it actually plays.

The purse has the same problem. Today's Golfer and Golf Today both list $3.75 million. ESPN and Wikipedia say $3.25 million. I couldn't open a figure from the tour itself, so quote it with a caveat.

What isn't disputed is the scoring history. Lawrence won last year at 22 under, which Wikipedia records as a tournament aggregate record of 258.

Four things about this leaderboard worth checking

  • "A two-shot lead at Crans is a strong position." Lawrence led by one going into last year's final round here and opened bogey-bogey-bogey, per the DP World Tour's own account. He still won, but the margin evaporated inside three holes.
  • "He's been in form all season." He hadn't recorded a top-10 finish since the Hainan Classic in March, per Golf Today. His own explanation was missed cuts by one shot, repeatedly.
  • "Ballesteros is the only three-time winner." He's the only one since 1972. Golf Today notes Lawrence would be the sixth across the event's 103-year history, so five men have done it in total.
  • "The course is 6,830 yards." Or 6,808 per ESPN. And at 1,500 metres it plays considerably shorter than either figure, which is why the winning score last year was 22 under.

The Raw Verdict

Thirty-six holes without a bogey is the number that should worry the field, more than the two-shot margin. Anyone can shoot 63 up a mountain. Playing two rounds without giving a shot back is a different discipline, and it's the one that survives a Sunday.

I'd still hedge, because Lawrence did exactly this last year and then made three bogeys in his first three holes on Sunday. He won anyway. What that tells you, and what it should tell you about your own Saturday medal, is that a bad start isn't the round. He answered it with an eagle at the sixth. Most of us would have answered it by giving up on the fourth tee.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who leads the 2026 Omega European Masters at halfway?

Thriston Lawrence, at 13 under after rounds of 64 and 63 at Crans-sur-Sierre. He's two clear of Jeremy Paul and Nacho Elvira at 11 under, with Keita Nakajima a further shot back at 10 under. Lawrence has played 36 holes without a bogey.

What record is Thriston Lawrence chasing?

A third Omega European Masters title, which would make him only the second player after Seve Ballesteros to win three times at Crans-Montana since the event joined the DP World Tour in 1972. He'd be the sixth three-time champion in the tournament's 103-year history.

When did Lawrence win the European Masters before?

In 2022 and 2025. Last year he took a one-shot lead into the final round, opened with three straight bogeys, then eagled the driveable par-4 sixth and closed with a 66 to win by two at 22 under, a tournament aggregate record of 258.

Where did the cut fall?

At 3 under. Nicolai Højgaard, Miguel Ángel Jiménez, Jorge Campillo and Connor Syme were among those to miss the weekend. Harry Hall made it at 7 under after a hip injury disrupted a round that had reached 10 under through nine holes.

What's the prize money at the 2026 Omega European Masters?

Reports conflict. Golf Today and Today's Golfer list $3.75 million, while ESPN and Wikipedia say $3.25 million. The event carries 5,000 Race to Dubai points and 1,500 Ryder Cup points, and runs September 3 to 6 at Crans-sur-Sierre.
