Grandma, A Vibes Caddie, And A 63: Muzzy's Wild Debut

BY GOLFRAW EDITORIAL PUBLISHED SAT 25 JUL 2026

The Quick Verdict

Start with the name, because nothing about Muzzy Donohue's week makes sense until you understand where "Muzzy" came from. Family members pinned it on him as a kid, borrowed from the fuzzy green lead character in the BBC cartoon "Muzzy in Gondoland." It stuck through school, through hockey, through golf, and now it's stuck through a PGA Tour debut that nobody outside Minnesota saw coming. Donohue shot an eight-under 63 on Friday at the 3M Open, close enough to his hometown that the gallery felt like a family reunion, and it was enough to squeeze into a tie for 24th and make the cut in his very first PGA Tour start.

The road here wasn't smooth

Donohue split his junior athletic career between hockey and golf at St. Thomas Academy before picking clubs over pucks in college, grinding out most of his years at Boston College before finishing up at Santa Clara. He's 25 now, and before this week's sponsor exemption, his PGA Tour-sanctioned experience amounted to a single start on the Latinoamerica circuit. He didn't even earn a card on that tour this year, stumbling in a bad final round at Q School, and by his own account he's played poorly on mini-tours since.

What changed things was a win at the Minnesota State Open two weeks ago. That victory gave him something to build on heading into this week, though nothing could have prepared him for what he'd carry onto the course alongside it. His grandmother, Marcey, passed away on Monday.

Thursday looked like the end, Friday flipped the script

Donohue opened with a 73 on Thursday and looked like a missed cut waiting to happen. Then he came out Friday and made nine birdies, four of them in a row on his front nine after starting his round on the 10th. On the same day Michael Kim shot a 59, Donohue found himself eight-under through 13 holes, with a shot at the same magic number if he could find four more birdies. He got one, at 17, before a three-foot miss on 18 turned into a bogey.

"Woke up from my blackout and missed the putt," Donohue said.

Even with the bogey, the hometown crowd gave him the kind of reception he'd never experienced. "It was unreal. It's hard to put words to it," Donohue said. "I've never really experienced this before. This is my first PGA Tour event. The most fans I've had watching are probably 50 at the State Open a few weeks ago. I just kind of tried to stay between the ropes and focus on what I could control. My caddie did a really good job of kind of keeping me present."

He didn't shy away from crediting something beyond golf either. "Yeah, my grandma was definitely with me this week, too. Or today. Yesterday, she left me for the first 10 holes or so, then she kind of came into play. Yeah, she's looking down on me and looking out for me. It was a good day."

Sharing a leaderboard with Scheffler, and feeling every bit of it

Donohue admitted the nerves hit hard once he realized who he was actually playing against this week, World No. 1 Scottie Scheffler included, a man sitting just one shot ahead of him through 36 holes. Watching players he'd only seen on TV warm up on the range left him rattled, until a familiar face talked him down.

"Steven Fisk is a big one," Donohue said of the tour pro and friend from his time in Sea Island, Georgia. "He kind of sat me down on Wednesday and gave me a little pep talk and told me that I belong here, and that they're just normal guys out there. I felt like I was getting pretty intimidated by all of the big names… but he really calmed me down. He gave me some advice to stay in the ropes, too, which is pretty cool."

The caddie who knows less about golf than he admits

Most players making their PGA Tour debut would bring in a seasoned professional caddie. Donohue brought his cousin Harrison instead, a decision built entirely on comfort rather than golf IQ.

"He tells you he knows a little bit about golf, but he knows even less than that. He's been purely just like the vibes guy," Donohue said. "I've been getting my yardages and stuff, which is a little adjustment. I haven't been super comfortable trusting my yardages, particularly on holes like 17 and 18 where there's water short. I've been clubbing up probably a little bit more than I should and playing it a little safer. But it's been great having him on the bag. He just gets my mind off golf. I can kind of be a little bit of an over-analyzer, so the less I think about golf the better for me."

A childhood split between the fairway and the ice

Donohue grew up on the private North Oaks Golf Club outside Minneapolis, spending summers on the course and winters back on the ice for hockey. Whichever sport was in season became the favorite, at least until college forced a decision.

"I think that probably stunted my development a little bit in terms of golf skill," Donohue said, "but I think it also built a lot of other skills and maybe made me a little bit more well-rounded. Then come college I really started to focus on golf."

Why he refuses to think about the cut line

Asked what he expected walking into the week, Donohue gave an answer that says more about his mental approach than any scorecard could.

"I think if I tried to put like an outcome-based goal in my mind, then if it is to make the cut, then a lot of the time I teeter right around it," he said. "It kind of limits myself. Like, I would hypothesize that if I thought, 'OK, let's make the cut this week,' I would have been thinking about the cut being at three or four under and once I got there, maybe I make the turn and start to get a little nervous and a little fearful, then make a couple bogeys instead of making a couple more birdies."

Carrying grandma into the weekend

Through everything this week, Donohue has kept "Grandma" written on both his golf ball and his ball marker, a quiet tribute running underneath a very loud story. As he heads into the weekend chasing a leaderboard that includes the best player in the world, he's leaning on that connection to get him through.

"Hopefully," he said, "she's not sick of me and will stick around the next couple days."
