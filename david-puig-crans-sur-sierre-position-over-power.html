David Puig at Crans-sur-Sierre: Why "Power's Not That Important" Is the Right Call at Altitude

A 24-year-old who hits it as far as anyone says distance isn't the point at 1,500 metres. Why he's right, and the rule your rangefinder can't bend.

By GOLFRAW Editorial Published 03 Sep 2026 Updated 03 Sep 2026, 16:00 CEST 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Crans-sur-Sierre sits above 1,500 metres. Thin air means more carry and less curve for everyone, not just the long hitters.
  • David Puig is 24 and already a DP World Tour winner. He won the 2025 BMW Australian PGA Championship after leaving Arizona State for LIV Golf.
  • Position beats power here. At a short par-70 where altitude gives the field extra yards, the next shot's angle matters more than another ten off the tee.
  • Your rangefinder can measure, not recommend. Slope, wind and altitude-adjusted club advice breach Rule 4.3a(1) in competition.
  • The reporting has gaps. Yardage and purse figures conflict, so this article labels the numbers rather than pretending the tour settled them.

Harry Hall compared the place to Las Vegas. Not the lights. The air, and what it does to a golf ball.

David Puig is making his debut at the 2026 Omega European Masters at Crans-sur-Sierre, a par-70 course above 1,500 metres in the Swiss Alps, prioritising positioning over distance. The DP World Tour describes altitude as the biggest on-course factor affecting players this week. Puig, 24, won the 2026 season opener in Australia for his first DP World Tour title.

The quote in the headline is being circulated from his debut week, and I couldn't open the original interview to verify the wording. What I can verify is why the philosophy behind it is correct on this specific golf course, and that's most of what follows.

Built on: the DP World Tour's tournament pages and player profile, Golf Monthly's field preview, Today's Golfer and ESPN tournament data, Golf Circus' course history, and the USGA's published guidance on distance-measuring devices.

What the mountain does to the ball

More than 1,500 metres up. That's the number that reorganises every yardage in your head.

Golf Circus notes the course sits above 1,500 metres of altitude, surrounded by some of the most recognisable peaks in the Alps, with playing conditions shaped by the lower density of the air. The DP World Tour's own tournament page states plainly that altitude is the biggest on-course factor affecting players this week.

Thinner air means less drag. Less drag means the ball flies further and curves less. Every club in the bag goes longer, and the gaps between them shift.

The DP World Tour's own coverage quotes Harry Hall comparing a fast start on his debut to being back at home in Las Vegas, which sits at elevation too.

So a player who already hits it a long way arrives at a course where everybody hits it a long way. The advantage evaporates. What's left is knowing where the ball stops.

The golf course, and a yardage nobody agrees on

Par 70. Somewhere between 6,808 and 6,830 yards, depending on who you read.

Today's Golfer lists Crans-sur-Sierre at 6,830 yards. ESPN's tournament page says 6,808. Twenty-two yards apart, neither corrected, and at altitude the difference is one easy swing anyway.

The purse has the same problem. Golf Monthly and Today's Golfer put it at $3.75 million. ESPN and GoandPlay list $3.25 million. Half a million dollars is a real discrepancy, and I couldn't find the tour's own figure to settle it.

What isn't disputed is the history. Golf Circus records the tournament's origins in the 1923 Swiss Open, a permanent home at Crans-sur-Sierre since 1948, and a redesign by Seve Ballesteros in the 1990s that led to the main course carrying his name since 2002. Thriston Lawrence defends after winning at 22 under, his second title here after 2022.

Twenty-two under on a par 70 tells you the scoring. This isn't a course that punishes. It's one that rewards the player who's in the right place on every approach.

Who David Puig is

The first college player to sign with LIV Golf, and now a DP World Tour winner.

LIV's own profile records that Puig left Arizona State to join the league in 2022, that he was the youngest on its roster until Caleb Surratt arrived, and that he won the 2025 BMW Australian PGA Championship. Wikipedia lists him at 6 feet 2 inches, born December 7, 2001 in La Garriga, Spain.

The DP World Tour's feature on 2026's first-time winners describes the path: a cut made at his home open as a 19-year-old amateur in 2021, a cut at his U.S. Open debut in 2023, back-to-back top fives at the Open de España and Dunhill Links in 2024, then the breakthrough in Australia in his 15th DP World Tour event, following Seve as a Spanish winner there.

He was named LIV Golf's Breakout Star of the Year for 2025, reached a career-high 59th in the world in May 2026, and finished T18 at this year's PGA Championship. He plays for Fireballs GC under Sergio García.

Golf Monthly notes this is his first start since the LIV season ended.

Why position beats power here specifically

Because power is free this week. Everyone got the same upgrade from the air.

Puig can hit it as far as anyone. His Asian Tour win at the 2024 Malaysian Open came with closing rounds of 62 and 62, per Wikipedia. He made an albatross at LIV Adelaide this year. Length isn't something he needs to prove.

At Crans, the calculation flips. A par 70 under 7,000 yards at altitude means driver is often too much club, and the question on every tee is which side of the fairway leaves the right angle in.

Here's my read, and it's the same one I'd give any long hitter at a short course. Distance is an advantage only where the other bloke can't match it. When conditions hand everyone 15 extra yards, the player who wins is the one who used those yards to hit less club and find the right spot, not the one who added them to a drive that was already long enough.

That's what the quote means, whether or not those were his exact words.

The rule that stops your rangefinder doing the altitude maths

You can measure distance. You cannot buy a device that tells you the adjusted number.

The USGA's rules guidance states that distance-measuring devices are permitted unless the committee prohibits them, but that using slope or wind functions breaches Rule 4.3a(1). A first breach costs two strokes in stroke play or loss of hole in match play.

Golf Digest's rules explainer on altitude-adjusting calculators addresses this exact scenario, and the principle is the same one the USGA and R&A set out in their joint statement on electronic devices: anything recommending a club, a shot, or a corrected distance is out.

So the adjustment Puig and his caddie are making for the mountain is done in their heads. That's the entire skill, and the rule exists to keep it that way.

At your course, this matters more than you think. Half the golfers with a slope-enabled rangefinder are using the slope function in competition without realising it's illegal.

The field he's up against

Eighteen Spaniards, five LIV players and a 62-year-old making his 36th appearance.

Golf Circus counts 18 Spanish players in the field led by Miguel Ángel Jiménez, the 2010 champion, playing his 36th Omega European Masters weeks before his 63rd birthday. Golf Monthly notes Sergio García is here on a sponsor's invitation for his first Crans appearance since 2019, having won the event in 2005 and previously called Crans-Montana home.

Golf Monthly lists Race to Dubai leader Patrick Reed, Ryan Gerard fresh from third in the FedExCup, Eugenio Chacarra and Casey Jarvis among the favourites. Five LIV golfers are in the field including Puig, García, Paul Casey, Thomas Detry and Adrian Meronk.

The DP World Tour reported 13 first-time winners in the first 29 events of 2026. Puig was the first of them.

Four claims about this week that don't hold up

Fact-check

  • "Long hitters dominate at altitude." Everyone gains distance in thin air. Lawrence won at 22 under last year, and the DP World Tour calls altitude the week's biggest factor for the whole field, not for one type of player.
  • "The course is 6,830 yards." Or 6,808, per ESPN. Two numbers, no correction, and at this elevation neither one tells you much about how it plays.
  • "The purse is $3.75 million." Two credible outlets say so. Two others say $3.25 million. I couldn't open the tour's own figure, so quote it with a caveat.
  • "Puig is just a LIV player dabbling in Europe." He won the DP World Tour's 2026 season opener, made cuts at majors going back to 2023, and holds a career-high world ranking of 59. This is a dual-tour career, not a visit.

Verification block

Confirmed: Crans-sur-Sierre's altitude and par-70 layout; the DP World Tour's description of altitude as the week's biggest on-course factor; Puig's age, Spanish birthplace, LIV and DP World Tour path; the 2025 BMW Australian PGA win; and the USGA/R&A distance-device rule cited below.

Unsettled: the exact circulating quote, the course yardage (6,808 or 6,830), and the purse ($3.25m or $3.75m). Those discrepancies stay visible.

The Raw Verdict

A young player with every bit of speed he needs turned up at a course that gives speed away for free and said it wasn't the point. He's right, and it took a lot of long hitters a lot of missed cuts up that mountain to learn it.

You don't play at 1,500 metres. You do play courses where everyone in your fourball hits it far enough, which is most of them. On those, the game is where the ball finishes, not how far it went. Puig said it on his debut. Most amateurs never say it at all.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where is the Omega European Masters played?

At Crans-sur-Sierre Golf Club in Crans-Montana, Switzerland, a par-70 course above 1,500 metres in the Alps. The tournament has been held there continuously since 1948, per Golf Monthly and Golf Circus, and the main course has carried Seve Ballesteros' name since 2002 after his 1990s redesign.

Why does altitude matter at Crans-sur-Sierre?

Thinner air reduces drag, so the ball flies further and curves less for every player. The DP World Tour describes altitude as the biggest on-course factor affecting players this week, and Harry Hall compared the conditions to playing at home in Las Vegas.

Who is David Puig?

A 24-year-old Spanish golfer from La Garriga who left Arizona State to become the first college player to sign with LIV Golf in 2022. He won the BMW Australian PGA Championship in November 2025 for his first DP World Tour title and was LIV's 2025 Breakout Star of the Year.

Can I use a rangefinder that adjusts for altitude?

Not in competition. The USGA states that distance-measuring devices are allowed unless prohibited, but slope and wind functions must not be used under Rule 4.3a(1). Devices recommending clubs or adjusted distances fall foul of the USGA and R&A joint statement on electronic devices.

What's the prize money at the 2026 Omega European Masters?

Reports conflict. Golf Monthly and Today's Golfer list $3.75 million, while ESPN and GoandPlay list $3.25 million. The event carries 5,000 Race to Dubai points and 1,500 Ryder Cup points, per Today's Golfer, and runs September 3 to 6.
