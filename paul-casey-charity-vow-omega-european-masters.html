Paul Casey's Charity Vow at the European Masters: What He Said, and What He's Actually Playing For

He shot 29 on the front nine, leads by three, and pledged every dollar of Sunday's cheque to the Crans-Montana fire victims. The whole picture.

By GOLFRAW Editorial Published 05 Sep 2026 Updated 05 Sep 2026, 21:30 CEST 8 min read

Six birdies in seven holes. A 29 on the front nine. Then a 49-year-old with a bad wrist and a bad back stood on the 18th green in the town where he now lives and cried.

Paul Casey leads the 2026 Omega European Masters by three shots at 16 under after a third-round 63 at Crans-sur-Sierre. He has pledged his entire prize money to victims of the New Year's Day fire at Le Constellation bar in Crans-Montana, which killed 41 people and injured 115. Four players sit second at 13 under.

That's the headline. The thing it hides is how narrow the path to actually winning is on Sunday, and it has to do with the thirteen players stacked five and six shots behind him. That's a few sections down.

Built on: Golf.com's report from Crans-sur-Sierre by Josh Schrock, Golf Channel's third-round coverage, the DP World Tour's post-round interview and results pages, and NBC News' reporting on the Crans-Montana fire as cited by Golf.com.

What Casey said after the round

The vow came after the tears, and both were about the same place.

Golf.com's report has Casey explaining that Switzerland is now home, that this feels like a home week, and that a win would mean an awful lot. Then the pledge: whatever happens Sunday, he'll play hard, but he'll make sure all my prize money as well is going to the charity and the fire victims in Crans-Montana.

The fire he's referring to killed 41 people and injured 115 at Le Constellation bar in the early hours of New Year's Day, per NBC News reporting cited by Golf.com. Crans-Montana is the resort hosting this tournament.

Casey said he has a lot to play for. Given what he's tied the cheque to, that's not a figure of speech.

The round that got him there

Sixty-seven, sixty-four, sixty-three. Every round lower than the last.

Golf Channel's account has Casey starting Saturday four back of Thriston Lawrence, birdieing six of seven holes on the front nine for a 29, then closing with a birdie at the 18th from what it calls a spectacular approach. Golf.com adds the middle: a dropped shot at the 14th, recovered immediately with a birdie at the 15th.

Sixteen under after 54 holes on a par-70 course, per the DP World Tour's tournament page.

He wasn't the only one going low. Golf Channel counts three other 63s on Saturday, from Maximilian Steinlechner, Erik van Rooyen and Bernd Wiesberger. Casey's was the one that started four back and ended three clear.

Who's chasing, and why the number matters

Four at 13 under. Thirteen more at 10 under.

Golf.com names the group tied for second: Eugenio Chacarra, Nacho Elvira, van Rooyen and Steinlechner. Golf Channel then lists a 13-way tie for tenth at 10 under that includes the defending champion Lawrence, who shot 73 on Saturday after his bogey-free 63 the day before, along with Masters winners Patrick Reed and Danny Willett, plus Harry Hall, Matt Wallace and David Puig.

Wiesberger got into that group from 10 shots back with his 63, per Golf Channel, eagling the par-5 14th along the way.

Here's the loop I opened at the top. Crans-sur-Sierre sits above 1,500 metres and the DP World Tour calls altitude the biggest factor affecting play this week. A 63 is available to anyone. Casey has six shots on seventeen players and three on four, on a course that surrendered four 63s in a single afternoon.

That's not a cushion. That's a head start in a sprint.

The five years he's trying to close

His last trophy was the 2021 Dubai Desert Classic. That's Golf.com's timeline, and it's a long one.

Casey left for LIV Golf in 2022 and played for Bryson DeChambeau's Crushers. Golf.com reports he earned $31.8 million there without winning a solo LIV title, losing twice in playoffs. His last major appearance came at the 2022 Open Championship. His last DP World Tour top 10 was the Porsche Singapore Classic in 2024.

He's 49, per Golf.com, and dealing with wrist and back problems. He holds 15 DP World Tour wins.

Five LIV players are in this field, per Golf Monthly's preview. Casey is the one leading it, at a tournament run by a tour that has warned LIV members about fines and suspensions for playing opposite its events.

My read after fifty years of watching men chase last wins: the 49-year-olds who close these out are the ones who stop thinking about the significance. Casey spent Saturday evening telling everyone how much it means. That's honest, and it's the hardest possible way to sleep.

What the money would actually be

The purse has two published figures, so the pledge does too.

Golf Monthly and Today's Golfer list the Omega European Masters purse at $3.75 million. ESPN and Wikipedia list $3.25 million. I couldn't find the DP World Tour's own figure to settle it, and no source I opened states the winner's share.

Whatever the number, Casey has said all of it goes to the fire victims, win or lose. Second place, tenth place, the same promise applies.

Worth putting alongside the $31.8 million Golf.com reports he earned on LIV. This week's cheque, whatever size, is the one he's chosen to give away.

Four things about this story worth checking before you repeat them

  • "Casey hasn't won anything since LIV." He hasn't won since the 2021 Dubai Desert Classic, per Golf.com. That predates LIV. He also lost two LIV playoffs, so he's been close.
  • "He's playing for the winner's cheque." He's pledged whatever he earns, win or lose. The vow covers every finishing position.
  • "A three-shot lead at Crans is safe." Four players shot 63 on Saturday alone. Lawrence led by two on Friday and shot 73 on Saturday. This golf course doesn't hold leads.
  • "The purse is $3.75 million." Two outlets say so. Two others say $3.25 million. Quote it with a caveat, because the tour's figure wasn't available in any source I could open.

Frequently Asked Questions

What did Paul Casey pledge at the Omega European Masters?

His entire prize money from the tournament, regardless of where he finishes, to charity supporting the victims of the New Year's Day fire at Le Constellation bar in Crans-Montana. Golf.com reports the fire killed 41 people and injured 115, citing NBC News. Casey now lives in Switzerland.

What did Casey shoot in the third round?

A 7-under 63, built on six birdies in seven holes on the front nine for a 29, a bogey at the 14th, and birdies at the 15th and 18th. His rounds of 67, 64 and 63 put him at 16 under, three clear of the field after 54 holes.

When did Paul Casey last win a tournament?

The 2021 Dubai Desert Classic, per Golf.com. He joined LIV Golf in 2022, earned $31.8 million with the Crushers and lost twice in playoffs without a solo win. A victory on Sunday would be his 16th DP World Tour title.

Who is chasing Casey on Sunday?

Eugenio Chacarra, Nacho Elvira, Erik van Rooyen and Maximilian Steinlechner share second at 13 under. Thirteen players are tied for tenth at 10 under, including defending champion Thriston Lawrence, Patrick Reed, Danny Willett, Harry Hall, Matt Wallace and David Puig.

Why are scores so low at Crans-sur-Sierre?

Altitude. The course sits above 1,500 metres, and the DP World Tour describes thin air as the biggest on-course factor this week. The ball flies further and curves less for everyone, which is why four players shot 63 on Saturday and Lawrence won last year at 22 under.

The Raw Verdict

The pledge is real, the lead is real, and the two things are in tension. A man who's just told the world what Sunday means to him now has to play 18 holes as if it doesn't, on a course where a three-shot lead evaporates in four holes.

If he does it, it'll be one of the better stories this sport produces all year, and it'll have nothing to do with which tour he plays on. If he doesn't, the money still goes where he said it would. Either way, watch the 12:00 groups posting 63s while he's still on the range. That's where this gets decided.
