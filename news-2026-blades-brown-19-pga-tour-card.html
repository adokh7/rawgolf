Blades Brown at 19: Two Paths to a PGA Tour Card

He made three cuts in eight PGA Tour starts last year. Now he has two live routes to a 2027 card before turning 20. Here's where he stands.

BY GOLFRAW EDITORIAL PUBLISHED TUE 01 SEP 2026

He made three cuts in eight PGA Tour starts last season. That's the number people leave out when they call him a phenom, and it's the one that makes this year interesting...

The hype machine around teenage golfers tends to work backwards: start with a highlight reel, declare a generation changed, and ignore the missed cuts on Friday afternoon. With Blades Brown—the Nashville teenager who broke Bobby Jones's stroke-play co-medalist age record at the 2023 U.S. Amateur before turning pro at 17—the real story isn't that he's invincible. It's that he has built two distinct, viable mathematical avenues to earn full PGA Tour status for 2027 before his twentieth birthday.

What Special Temporary Membership actually gives him

Under PGA Tour regulations, a non-member who earns non-member FedExCup points equal to or greater than the player who finished No. 150 on the prior year's points list is eligible for Special Temporary Membership (STM). For Brown, STM is the golden ticket that removes the standard restriction of seven sponsor exemptions per season.

With STM in hand, Brown can accept unlimited sponsor exemptions into full-field events throughout the regular season and the FedExCup Fall. It doesn't put him into Signature Events, but it ensures that every cut made and every top-25 finish deposits live points directly onto the non-member points ledger.

The record he'd break, and by how much

If Brown locks up his card for 2027, he will be doing so at age 19. Modern PGA Tour cards earned directly through competitive points (without relying on discretionary commissioner categories) are vanishingly rare for teenagers. Jordan Spieth famously earned his card at 19 in 2013 by parlaying sponsor exemptions into a win at the John Deere Classic.

Should Brown cross the threshold via points alone without an outright victory, he would join an elite historical echelon of young ball-strikers who bypassed college golf entirely to secure top-tier status on merit.

The week that made people notice

The inflection point came at the Myrtle Beach Classic. After an opening 72, Brown blitzed The Dunes Golf and Beach Club with a 10-under 61 in the second round—matching the lowest score by a 16- or 17-year-old in PGA Tour history. He went on to finish T26, demonstrating that his ceiling is already capable of dismantling a PGA Tour setup.

That 61 wasn't a product of flukey 40-foot putts; it was built on elite iron precision, gaining over 3.5 strokes on approach in a single round. For scouts and tournament directors with sponsor exemptions to hand out, that Friday scorecard ended the debate.

The part that isn't a highlight reel

Behind the viral scorecards lies the brutal reality of professional golf: five missed cuts in eight starts. When driver dispersion gets loose or putting speeds shift from week to week, raw talent doesn't rescue a scorecard on a tight bermudagrass layout.

The gap between a promising teenager and a top-125 Tour member isn't measured in best rounds; it's measured in bad rounds. Turning a 76 into a 72 when your swing feels foreign is what decides whether a player earns $8,000 or $80,000 on a Sunday afternoon.

The amateur record that started it

Brown didn't arrive on the PGA Tour out of nowhere. In August 2023 at Cherry Hills, he shot rounds of 64-72 to earn co-medalist honors at the U.S. Amateur at age 16, breaking Bobby Jones's 103-year-old record as the youngest medalist in U.S. Amateur history.

Backing that up with consecutive Tennessee State Open and Junior Amateur titles proved that his temperament was already seasoned well beyond his years.

Five things being said about Blades Brown that need a second look

  1. “He's guaranteed a Tour card.” False. He must still amass enough FedExCup points to finish inside the top 125 of the final Fall standings, or earn his way through Korn Ferry Tour finals.
  2. “Skipping college golf was a reckless gamble.” With NIL deals and current PGA Tour pathways, jumping directly into professional starts provided immediate exposure to tour-level setups and accelerated his learning curve.
  3. “He only gets starts because of his name.” While 'Blades' is a catchy headline, tournament directors grant exemptions based on scoring potential—proven by his second-round 61 at Myrtle Beach.
  4. “He has unlimited access to Signature Events.” STM only removes exemption limits on full-field regular and Fall events; Signature Events require explicit qualification or rare sponsor picks.
  5. “His game is purely distance-reliant.” Brown's statistical strength in his made cuts has been approach play and wedge proximity, not just brute force off the tee.

The Raw Verdict

Blades Brown has two legitimate routes to a 2027 PGA Tour card before turning 20: the direct FedExCup top-125 sprint via sponsor exemptions, and the Korn Ferry Tour safety net. His 61 proved his peak is tour-ready; his five missed cuts proved the grind has only just begun. Bet on the trajectory, but respect the math.

FAQ

How old is Blades Brown and where is he from?

Blades Brown is 19 years old, born in May 2007 in Nashville, Tennessee. He emerged as a standout amateur and junior champion before turning professional.

What is PGA Tour Special Temporary Membership?

Special Temporary Membership allows non-members who earn non-member FedExCup points equal to or greater than No. 150 from the previous season to accept unlimited sponsor exemptions for the remainder of the season.

What are his two pathways to earn a full 2027 PGA Tour card?

His primary path is earning enough non-member FedExCup points to finish inside the top 125 of the final FedExCup Fall standings. His secondary path is through Korn Ferry Tour status and PGA Tour Q-School presented by Korn Ferry.

What record could Blades Brown break?

He could become one of the youngest players in modern PGA Tour history to earn a full tour card before turning 20, joining an elite club that includes Jordan Spieth.

What is Blades Brown's lowest competitive round on the PGA Tour?

Blades Brown fired a blistering 10-under 61 in the second round of the Myrtle Beach Classic, one of the lowest scores by a teenager in PGA Tour history.
