Michael Block's Ally Challenge: A 36-Hole Record, Then 74

He set the tournament's 36-hole scoring record, led by two, then shot 74. Why a T3 is still the best result of his senior career, and what it buys him.

BY GOLFRAW EDITORIAL PUBLISHED MON 31 AUG 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Block opened The Ally Challenge with 66-65 for 13-under 131, the event's 36-hole scoring record, before a final-round 74 left him T3 at 11-under 205.
  • The $2.2 million purse belonged to the event; Block reported a $132,000 payout, and his T3 secured entry into the Sanford International.
  • The weekend was evidence of senior-tour progress: a second consecutive top-five finish after T9 at DICK'S Open and T5 at the Rogers Charity Classic.

The official DICK’S Open leaderboard records Block's debut as 66-70-67 for T9, while the official Rogers Charity Classic leaderboard records 65-65-67 for T5.

Anatomy of the 36-Hole Record

The record was built over two very different rounds. Block shot 66 on Friday, then followed it with a bogey-free 65 on Saturday. A birdie run and an eagle on the par-5 first helped him reach 13-under 131, two clear of the field after 36 holes. The PGA TOUR Champions round-two report confirms the 13-under halfway total and his two-shot lead; the opening-hole eagle detail was reported by Loop Golf's round-two account.

Sunday Reality Check

Sunday changed the leaderboard without erasing the week. Block shot 74 and finished T3 at 11-under 205, while Steven Alker closed with a 68 to win by two at 15-under. The official final-round report lists it as Alker's third win of 2026 and 13th career win on the PGA TOUR Champions.

Calling it a classic choke misses the better explanation: the official result shows a difficult final round after two career-best senior-tour rounds, and Golf Digest's round report identified three back-nine bogeys. That is a Sunday to study, not a reason to discard the first 36 holes.

Career Senior Impact

The Ally Challenge is a 54-hole PGA TOUR Champions event with a $2.2 million purse, according to the tournament's official release. That is the size of the event purse, not Block's individual share. Block said the T3 paid $132,000, a meaningful return that also helped secure him a spot in the Sanford International.

More important than one cheque is the pattern. The official tournament release notes his T9 at DICK’S Open and his exemption into The Ally Challenge; the new T3 gives a player who arrived through sponsor opportunities a second consecutive top-five-level signal on the circuit.

The 2023 Oak Hill Comparison vs 2026 Senior Reality

Oak Hill made Block famous because one week turned a club professional into a major-championship contender. The senior-tour results are a different kind of evidence: three rounds, repeated starts and a finish that followed T9 and T5. The comparison is not that the moments are identical. It is that 2026 is beginning to look less like a single emotional run and more like a player learning how to stay on a Champions leaderboard.

His 2023 Charles Schwab Challenge also showed the other side of the volatility: the PGA TOUR report records an opening 81. The gap between that week and a 66-65-74 senior-tour T3 is why the Sunday result deserves context rather than a meme.

5 Claims Fact-Checked

  • “He choked.” The final 74 was costly, but the stronger fact is T3 at 11-under after a 13-under halfway record.
  • “The 13-under was his final score.” No. It was his 36-hole total; the 54-hole total was 11-under 205.
  • “The purse was Block’s payout.” No. $2.2 million was the event purse; $132,000 was the reported payout for his T3.
  • “Alker only won because Block faded.” Alker still had to close with 68 and finished 15-under, two shots clear.
  • “This was another novelty act.” The better reading is three strong Champions results across consecutive starts, including T9, T5 and T3.

The Raw Verdict

Michael Block's Ally Challenge was not a lost win. It was a record-setting 36-hole performance followed by a 74 that still produced the best finish of his senior career. That combination matters: the record demonstrates ceiling, the T3 demonstrates resilience, and the next start will show whether the pattern holds.
