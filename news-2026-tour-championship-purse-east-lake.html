2026 Tour Championship Purse: The Winner Gets $10 Million

The 2026 Tour Championship pays $10 million to the winner. But the $100 million FedExCup pool was already handed out. Here is who got paid, and when.

By GOLFRAW Editorial Published 27 Aug 2026 Updated 27 Aug 2026, 17:00 CEST 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • The 2026 TOUR Championship features a standalone $40 million official purse, with $10 million awarded to Sunday's champion.
  • The $100 million season-long FedExCup bonus pool was formally settled upon completion of the BMW Championship, decoupling season points from the final tournament score.
  • Scottie Scheffler banked $23 million for capturing the FedExCup regular season and playoff points crown before hitting a shot at East Lake.
  • All 30 qualifiers start at even par in straight 72-hole stroke play; starting strokes and artificial handicaps have been retired.
  • Every finisher at East Lake clears six figures, with 8th place earning over $1 million and 30th place taking home $280,000.

The math at East Lake Golf Club has finally been separated from the mythology. For years, the PGA Tour attempted to fuse two conflicting concepts into a single Sunday afternoon: a season-long consistency bonus and an individual 72-hole stroke-play tournament. The result was a convoluted starting-strokes system that confused fans, distorted official records, and left players answering awkward questions about whether they had truly won a golf tournament or simply held a pre-assigned head start.

In 2026, that confusion is gone. The PGA Tour established a clean structural partition: the roughly $100 million FedExCup bonus pool was distributed when the final putt dropped at the BMW Championship. East Lake is now what it was always meant to be—an elite, 30-man, straight-up stroke-play championship with a massive $40 million official purse.

The $100 Million Bonus Pool: Settled Before Atlanta

The biggest misconception entering Tour Championship week is that the winner on Sunday takes home the entire FedExCup pot. In reality, the FedExCup points champion has already been decided and paid.

By amassing an insurmountable points cushion across the regular season, the FedEx St. Jude Championship, and the BMW Championship at Bellerive, Scottie Scheffler locked up the No. 1 spot in the season standings. That achievement earned him a $23 million bonus deposit into his account before he ever stepped foot in Atlanta.

The 2026 Tour Championship Purse: $40 Million at Stake

With the season bonus paid out, East Lake operates as a pure, uncompromised tournament. The 30 qualifiers compete for an official $40 million purse, where 1st place receives an unprecedented $10 million winner's check.

Unlike previous seasons where victory at East Lake didn't register as an official PGA Tour victory or official money due to staggered handicaps, every dollar won this week counts toward official PGA Tour career earnings. Furthermore, all 30 players begin at even par, meaning 72 holes of unassisted golf decide the outcome.

Writer Picks & Value Analysis

With an even-par starting baseline, betting models and editorial projections can focus purely on course fit rather than overcoming staggered deficits.

Outright Favorite: Scottie Scheffler (+310)

Having confirmed he is 95 percent recovered from his recent bout with illness, Scheffler remains the benchmark for ball-striking on Donald Ross greens. Free from the mental burden of protecting a pre-tournament lead, Scheffler's strokes-gained tee-to-green profile makes him the deserved tournament favorite.

Prime Challenger: Xander Schauffele (+1200)

No player in modern golf owns a more prolific scoring record around East Lake than Schauffele. Without starting strokes penalizing his prior-week positions, Schauffele's precision approach play and clinical short game make him the most dangerous contender in the 30-man field.

Value Sleeper: Cameron Young (+2200)

Young enters Atlanta fresh off a top-four finish in the FedExCup bonus pool standings. His driving distance gives him a distinct advantage on East Lake's extended par-4s, making his +2200 price compelling value.

The Golf Raw Verdict

For seven years, the PGA Tour tried to convince golf fans that a player who started a tournament at 10-under par and shot four rounds of 71 had legitimately won a tournament against a player who started at 2-under and shot four rounds of 65. It was mathematically absurd and competitively unsatisfying.

By giving Scheffler his $23 million reward on Sunday in St. Louis and resetting all 30 scorecards to zero in Atlanta, the Tour restored competitive integrity. On Sunday at East Lake, whoever hoists the trophy will have shot the lowest score over 72 holes. And they will take home $10 million in cold, hard, official cash for doing so.
