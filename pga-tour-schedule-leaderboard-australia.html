PGA Tour 2026 Guide for Australian Fans: Schedule, Leaderboard, Rankings and How to Watch

Trying to find “PGA golf today” can lead to several completely different pages. One may show the score in this week’s tournament, another may show the season-long FedExCup table, and another may rank players from tours around the world.

BY GOLFRAW EDITORIAL PUBLISHED: WED 29 JUL 2026

The simplest way to follow the PGA Tour is to use three separate tools:

  • Tournament leaderboard: who is leading the event being played now
  • FedExCup standings: how PGA Tour members rank across the season
  • Official World Golf Ranking: how players rank across eligible tournaments worldwide

As of 29 July 2026, the next PGA Tour event is the Rocket Classic, played from 30 July to 2 August at Detroit Golf Club. Scottie Scheffler leads the FedExCup standings and is also world No. 1, although the two rankings use different systems.

Where to find the PGA Tour leaderboard

The official PGA Tour leaderboard is the best starting point for live scores, tee times, player positions, completed holes and projected FedExCup movement.

During a tournament, the page normally shows:

  • Each player’s score relative to par
  • The hole currently being played
  • Round-by-round scores
  • Tee times
  • Whether a player is above or below the projected cut
  • Projected movement in the FedExCup standings

The leaderboard automatically changes to the current tournament. Before the first round begins, it may show groupings and starting times rather than scores.

A static article should never claim to be the definitive live leaderboard. Use it to understand the competition, then use the PGA Tour’s official page for the changing numbers.

How the FedExCup works in 2026

The FedExCup is the PGA Tour’s season-long points competition. Players earn points according to where they finish in eligible events, with larger allocations available at majors, The Players Championship, Signature Events and playoff tournaments.

The current 2026 overview lists 34 official regular-season events. You may still find older articles saying 35 because the original schedule included The Sentry. That event was later cancelled, reducing the live schedule by one.

After the Wyndham Championship:

  • The top 70 enter the FedEx St. Jude Championship.
  • The top 50 advance to the BMW Championship.
  • The top 30 reach the Tour Championship.

The PGA Tour describes the overall FedExCup bonus pool as nearly US$100 million. The 2026 Tour Championship has a US$40 million purse, with US$10 million listed for the winner.

Why the top-70 line matters

Players around 70th place are not merely fighting for a slightly better ranking. They are competing for entry into the playoffs.

That pressure was visible at the 3M Open, where rookie Jackson Koivun won in his third professional start and moved 124 positions to 70th in the FedExCup standings. His place remained vulnerable because two regular-season events were still to be played.

Leaderboard vs FedExCup standings vs world ranking

These terms are frequently mixed together, but they answer different questions.

Tournament leaderboard

  • Question answered: Who is winning this week?
  • It resets for each tournament and is based on the player’s score in that event.

FedExCup standings

  • Question answered: Who has performed best during the PGA Tour season?
  • Players collect points across eligible PGA Tour events. The standings determine playoff qualification and influence playing status.

Official World Golf Ranking

  • Question answered: How does a player rank across eligible professional golf worldwide?
  • The OWGR uses results from eligible tournaments on multiple tours. Points depend partly on the strength of the field and are maintained through a rolling ranking period. Recent results receive greater weight before gradually depreciating.

At the research cutoff, Scottie Scheffler was first in both the FedExCup standings and the OWGR. That does not make the systems identical; it means he led both under their separate rules.

Is the PGA Tour the same as the PGA?

No. “PGA” can refer to several organisations and tournaments.

PGA Tour
The PGA Tour runs the main US-based weekly professional circuit, the FedExCup, PGA Tour Champions, the Korn Ferry Tour and related competitions. Its modern organisation dates to 1968, when touring professionals separated from the PGA of America.

PGA of America
The PGA of America is a membership organisation for club professionals, coaches, operators and other golf-industry professionals. It also organises the PGA Championship.

PGA Tour of Australasia
This is the professional tour serving Australia and the wider Australasian region. It is not the same competition as the US PGA Tour.

DP World Tour
This is the tour formerly widely known as the European Tour. Some tournaments are co-sanctioned with other circuits, which allows the same event to count on more than one tour.

Understanding these distinctions prevents a common search mistake: looking for a PGA Tour result when the event is actually a PGA of America major, a DP World Tour tournament or an Australasian event.

Is the PGA Championship a PGA Tour event?

The PGA Championship is run by the PGA of America, not the PGA Tour. However, it is included in the PGA Tour schedule, attracts leading PGA Tour players and awards FedExCup points to eligible members.

The 2026 PGA Championship took place at Aronimink Golf Club in Pennsylvania during the week of 11–17 May.

This dual relationship explains why the tournament appears on PGA Tour schedule pages even though another organisation owns and operates the championship.

How to watch the PGA Tour in Australia

Australian viewers can generally find PGA Tour coverage through Fox Sports and Kayo Sports. Event guides during the 2026 season have repeatedly identified those services as the principal Australian options.

However, check the listing for the specific tournament before assuming that coverage begins with the first tee time.

An event can offer several separate feeds internationally:

  • Main television broadcast
  • Early-round coverage
  • Featured groups
  • Featured holes
  • Digital-only streams
  • Weekend network coverage

The Australian rights package may not include every feed. In one r/GolfAustralia discussion, several viewers complained that Kayo’s listed coverage began hours after the first groups had started. That is anecdotal evidence from one event, but it highlights why the platform schedule should be checked each week.

The most reliable Australian viewing routine

  1. Check the official PGA Tour schedule to identify the tournament.
  2. Open the event page and note the US broadcast window.
  3. Check Kayo or the Fox Sports TV guide for the Australian start time.
  4. Keep the official leaderboard open if the television coverage begins after play has started.
  5. Remember that Australian dates may roll into the following morning because of the time difference.

What does the Australian Open’s PGA Tour partnership mean?

Golf Australia announced a new agreement involving the PGA Tour and DP World Tour in June 2026.

The formal PGA Tour partnership covers the 2027, 2028 and 2029 Australian Opens. It is intended to improve the event’s calendar position, international field and prize money. It does not retroactively turn the 2026 Australian Open into a standard PGA Tour event.

For the 2026 tournament, readers should continue to follow the Australian Open, DP World Tour and Challenger PGA Tour of Australasia channels for the relevant field, leaderboard and broadcast details.
