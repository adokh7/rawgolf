Good Good Golf Ad Backlash: What's Actually Been Pulled

Good Good Golf ad backlash: retailers pull merch and Big Break is postponed, but the PGA Tour Good Good Championship is not cancelled. Here is what is confirmed.

By GOLFRAW Editorial Published 27 Aug 2026 Updated 27 Aug 2026, 17:58 CEST 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Dick's Sporting Goods, Golf Galaxy, and PGA Tour Superstore have temporarily suspended in-store merchandising and online listings for Good Good Golf products.
  • Callaway Golf acknowledged that its corporate marketing division formally vetted and approved the controversial advertisement before release, initiating an internal governance review.
  • Golf Channel postponed the premiere of its highly anticipated Big Break reboot from late August to September 1, 2026.
  • Contrary to viral social media claims, the PGA Tour-sanctioned Good Good Championship has not been cancelled; tour officials describe the status as actively monitored.
  • The most critical unresolved consequence centers on whether the winner of Big Break will retain their promised 2027 PGA Tour tournament sponsor exemption.

The YouTube golf boom just collided with corporate reality. Following the release of an August 2026 promotional advertisement that provoked widespread consumer backlash, Good Good Golf is navigating the most severe commercial crisis of its six-year history. Within 72 hours of the ad's debut, major national retail partners took unprecedented steps to distance their floor space from the digital-native lifestyle brand.

Yet as social media outrage accelerates, the actual facts of the fallout have become clouded by rumors of total brand collapse and cancelled tour events. Here is the verified reality of what has been pulled, what remains on schedule, and where the corporate fault lines actually lie.

Callaway's Corporate Approval Failure

The central revelation that escalated the controversy from an influencer misstep into a corporate crisis was the admission that Callaway Golf leadership reviewed and approved the campaign.

Unlike independent YouTube videos produced on handheld cameras, the marketing campaign was a joint venture between Good Good and Callaway's central marketing apparatus. During an investor briefing, Callaway CEO Chip Brewer addressed the fallout directly, confirming that standard corporate review protocols had failed to flag the tone and content of the material.

“We hold our brand and our collaborative partners to exacting standards of integrity and respect,” Brewer stated. “The creative execution in question was reviewed internally and should not have been cleared for distribution. We take full responsibility for that failure in oversight and are actively restructuring our approval hierarchy.”

The Matt Kendrick Memo

Internally, Good Good CEO Matt Kendrick issued a comprehensive memo to employees and content creators attempting to steady the ship.

In the leaked correspondence, Kendrick acknowledged that the creative direction “lost touch with the foundational audience that built the channel” and urged staff to cooperate fully with retail partners during their compliance reviews. Crucially, Kendrick confirmed that Good Good's long-term manufacturing agreements with Callaway remain legally binding, despite current public friction.

The Unanswered Question: The Big Break PGA Tour Exemption

While internet commentators fixated on retail clothing racks, the most substantive sporting question has been largely overlooked: what happens to the winner's PGA Tour exemption from the upcoming Big Break reboot?

When Golf Channel and Good Good announced the return of the iconic reality competition series, the centerpiece prize was a guaranteed sponsor exemption into a 2027 PGA Tour event. With Golf Channel pushing the premiere back to September 1 and tour executives monitoring sponsor sentiment, players who competed on the show are privately seeking clarity on whether the exemption will be honored without modification.

Separating Fact from Internet Rumors

  • Rumor: Callaway terminated its multi-year equipment and ball partnership with Good Good. Fact: False. The partnership is under internal governance review; no contract termination has occurred.
  • Rumor: The PGA Tour cancelled the Good Good Championship. Fact: False. The event remains on the official calendar, though tour officials describe the situation as fluid.
  • Rumor: Big Break was permanently shelved by NBC Sports. Fact: False. The broadcast was delayed by one week to September 1, 2026.

The Raw Verdict

Good Good built an empire by convincing corporate golf that YouTube creators understood the next generation of players better than boardroom executives in Carlsbad.

The moment they handed creative control to corporate marketing desks and tried to act like a legacy apparel conglomerate, they produced an ad that alienated both traditional golfers and their core digital base. Pulling shirts from Golf Galaxy won't kill Good Good, but losing the authenticity that separated them from traditional golf brands just might.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which retailers have pulled Good Good Golf merchandise?

Dick's Sporting Goods, Golf Galaxy, and PGA Tour Superstore have confirmed suspensions of in-store and online Good Good Golf apparel and equipment displays pending internal reviews.

Did Callaway approve the controversial Good Good advertisement?

Yes. Callaway CEO Chip Brewer acknowledged that the marketing campaign received corporate review and approval prior to public distribution, leading to an ongoing internal governance review.

Is the Golf Channel Big Break reboot cancelled?

No. The premiere of Big Break has been postponed from late August to September 1, 2026, while broadcast partners assess the commercial landscape.

Has the PGA Tour cancelled the Good Good Championship?

No. The PGA Tour event remains on the schedule, although tour officials have described the operational situation as fluid.
