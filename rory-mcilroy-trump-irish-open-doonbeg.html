Rory McIlroy Admits the Irish Open Will Feel 'Different' as Trump Heads to Doonbeg, and Here's What That Means for Your Ticket

By Golf Raw Editorial Staff Published 08 Sep 2026 Updated 08 Sep 2026, 18:30 IST 4 min read

Rory McIlroy said on Tuesday that the 2026 Amgen Irish Open will feel different with U.S. President Donald Trump expected to attend the weekend rounds at Trump International Golf Links Doonbeg, the County Clare resort Trump owns. Speaking to BBC Sport Northern Ireland ahead of Thursday's opening round, the defending champion said players would keep their heads down and play, while acknowledging the visit brings logistical challenges and that weekend crowds will be limited. Trump is in Ireland on an official two-day visit and is expected at Doonbeg on Saturday and Sunday, September 12 and 13, according to reporting by the Press Association and Irish Golfer Magazine.

McIlroy's line about limited crowds is the one nobody has followed up. The section on getting into Doonbeg explains what's actually been announced, and what hasn't.

Golf Raw did not attend Doonbeg or interview McIlroy. Quotes come from his BBC Sport Northern Ireland interview as reported by the Press Association via Yahoo Sports and by Irish Golfer Magazine. Access rules come from Clare County Council, An Garda Síochána advice as reported by Clare FM, the Clare Echo and the Clare Herald, and the DP World Tour's ticket page.

What McIlroy actually said

Three sentences, and none of them are political.

The world No. 2 told BBC Sport Northern Ireland, per the Press Association, that “it's going to be different with him being here,” before adding that the players are there to play a tournament on a good golf course. On the weekend visit he said that “crowds will be limited, obviously there's a lot that goes on.”

Then the line every headline grabbed. Irish Golfer Magazine's account has him acknowledging the logistics before finishing: if Trump wants to come and watch the Irish Open, “who are we to say 'no?'”

That's a professional describing an owner turning up at his own property. Read anything more into it and you're doing the tabloids' work for them.

McIlroy is defending the title he won at The K Club in 2025, beating Joakim Lagergren on the third playoff hole after a 28-foot eagle putt on the 72nd, per BBC coverage. Irish Golfer Magazine notes this is the first major professional tournament Doonbeg has hosted.

Getting into Doonbeg: what's confirmed

Here's the part that matters if you're holding a ticket. No parking, no walking in, and one road closed.

The Clare Echo reports that Clare County Council has confirmed the N67 will close from Doonbeg village at its junction with the L-2030 to the N67/R483 junction at Clonadrum, and the R484 from Creegh village to Behan's Cross, from 6am on Friday, September 11 until 11.59pm on Monday, September 14. The council says the closures are at the request of An Garda Síochána for operational and security reasons.

Gardaí have said access to the event is by Park and Ride or shuttle bus only, per Clare FM, with parking banned in Doonbeg village from Tuesday, September 8 and vehicles left on approach roads liable to be towed. The Clare Echo lists a free Park and Ride running from Wednesday, September 9 to Sunday, September 13 at two sites, north at Eircode V95 P7N2 and south at V15 HK77, with ticket holders required to choose a site and register a car registration when buying. Extra shuttles run from Kilkee, Kilrush and Miltown Malbay.

Locals need paperwork. Clare FM reports residents inside the cordon require a Garda pass or photo ID plus proof of address.

Then the searches. Clare FM reports Gardaí saying everyone attending will be subject to a security search, with bags limited to roughly airline under-seat size and a prohibited items list published by the DP World Tour and Gardaí. The Irish Times, citing a security source, describes it as one of the most complex operations ever staged in the State, with advice not to rely on satnavs. Clare FM calls it the largest security operation in the country this year.

The last row is the open loop. The DP World Tour has not published any figure for reduced weekend admission that Golf Raw could find. Whether “limited” means fewer tickets sold or galleries held back from wherever Trump is standing hasn't been stated by anyone with authority to state it.

What the ticket actually costs you

General admission from €40. Ticket+ at €155. Hospitality on the 18th from €549 before VAT, per the DP World Tour's ticket page as of September 8, 2026.

That's the price of watching links golf in West Clare this week. The shuttle time, the search queue and whatever “limited” turns out to mean are on top of it, and none of that is refundable.

Golf Raw's view: the players will be fine. McIlroy said as much, and pros treat a venue as a piece of turf. The person absorbing this is the one who paid €40, parked in a field with a registration number on a list, and boarded a bus to be searched before seeing a golf shot. That's the story the political coverage skipped.

The timeline

Monday, September 7: Garda advice issued to spectators, per The Irish Times, including the warning about satnavs.

Tuesday, September 8: Parking banned in Doonbeg village. McIlroy speaks to BBC Sport Northern Ireland.

Wednesday, September 9: Park and Ride begins, per the Clare Echo.

Thursday, September 10: Round one.

Friday, September 11, 6am: N67 and R484 close.

Saturday and Sunday, September 12 and 13: Trump expected on site, per the Press Association and Irish Golfer Magazine.

Precise arrival times and whether he'll walk holes or stay at the lodge haven't been published. Neither has any tee sheet Golf Raw could verify, so check the DP World Tour's site for McIlroy's Thursday time rather than any secondhand listing.

FAQ

Will general admission ticket holders get in at the weekend?

Nobody official has said otherwise. Access is by Park and Ride and shuttle for all ticket holders, per Gardaí. McIlroy expects crowds to be limited on the days Trump attends, but the DP World Tour has published no reduced capacity figure. Treat weekend access as confirmed for existing tickets, with movement on course likely restricted near the presidential party.

Is Trump playing in the tournament?

No. Reporting by the Press Association and Irish Golfer Magazine describes him attending as a spectator on Saturday and Sunday during an official two-day visit to Ireland. His Doonbeg resort is hosting the event.

How early should I arrive?

Gardaí advise arriving early to allow for security checks and carrying as little as possible, per Clare FM. With mandatory shuttles, bag searches and road closures from Friday, Golf Raw would add at least two hours to whatever you'd normally plan for a tournament day.

The Raw Verdict

McIlroy gave the only sensible answer available to a man trying to win a golf tournament rather than host a news debate: heads down, play. He's right, and the tabloids trying to turn “who are we to say no?” into a position are wasting everyone's time.

If you've got a Saturday or Sunday ticket, the practical advice is short. Register your car, pick a Park and Ride, bring a small bag, and leave two hours earlier than feels reasonable. The golf will be worth it. The bus won't be, and nobody in a blazer is going to apologise for it.
