Cam Smith Turned Down the Exit in February. In April, LIV Lost Its Funding.

Cam Smith says he'd "100%" return to the PGA Tour if LIV folded. But he turned down the exit route in February — and PIF pulled its funding in April.

BY GOLFRAW EDITORIAL PUBLISHED WED 05 AUG 2026

Cameron Smith stood at Bedminster on Tuesday and said that if LIV Golf folded, returning to the PGA Tour would be where everyone would want to be. A hundred per cent, he said. It'd go back to old times.

That's running everywhere as a headline. It's the least interesting thing he said.

Here's the interesting thing, and it needs a calendar.

The Door Was Open for Six Weeks

In December last year, Brooks Koepka left LIV. The PGA Tour responded by creating the Returning Member Program — a one-time pathway back, open only to players who'd won a major or the Players Championship between 2022 and 2025.

That was four men: Koepka, Jon Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau and Cam Smith.

The window opened in December and closed on 2 February 2026, two days before LIV's first event of the season.

Koepka went through it. The other three publicly said no. Smith's line at the time was that he'd made his bed and he'd sleep in it. He said he was in a good place in his career and his family life and didn't need to change it.

In April, Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund announced it would stop funding LIV Golf after this season.

Eight weeks between the door closing and the money leaving.

I don't say that to mock anybody. Smith made a decision with the information he had, and it was a decision plenty of people would have made. But the timeline is the story, and it's remarkable that in a week of wall-to-wall LIV coverage nobody has laid the two dates side by side.

What That Door Actually Cost

It's worth knowing what Koepka paid, because "he'd 100% go back" makes it sound like a free option.

To return under the programme, Koepka had to make a $5 million charitable donation. He can't receive PGA Tour equity grants for five years. He gets no FedExCup bonus money in 2026. He can't take sponsor exemptions into the Tour's $20 million signature events. The Tour's chief executive described the consequences as severe and justified, and he wasn't being coy about it.

Koepka was ranked 244th in the world when he took that deal.

So when a LIV player says they'd return if they had to, understand what's being described. Not a homecoming. A toll booth.

Read the Quote Properly

Smith's actual sentence had a condition bolted to the front of it: if it was the only option.

In the same press conference he said he wants LIV to work, that the team environment makes them better golfers and better people, and that he'd hate to see it go. He's contracted to the end of next year and expects to play out the deal and then renegotiate. He talked about Australian golf, junior golf, the Little Rippers programme, and what the Australian event has done for the game there.

That is not a man packing a bag. It's a man being asked a hypothetical by reporters who already knew the answer, and answering it honestly.

His teammate Marc Leishman has had no PGA Tour conversations at all and expects LIV to be back — though he accepts it'll look different, with fewer events and less money.

Meanwhile, Nobody Knows if There's a Tournament

Smith also said the players had been told to prepare as though the season-ending Team Championship in Michigan is going ahead, and that he's doing exactly that. He'd like to go back; the crowds were good last year.

Martin Kaymer, a team captain, has put the odds of that event happening at about five per cent.

LIV has said nothing. The $40 million finale, with $11.2 million to the winning team, is scheduled for 27-30 August at a course outside Detroit where, as of a week ago, nothing had been built.

There was a player meeting at Bedminster on Tuesday afternoon. If they were told anything definitive, it hasn't surfaced.

One Loop Worth Closing

Koepka, the one who left, is currently 86th in the FedExCup standings.

If he climbs into the bubble at this week's Wyndham Championship, a provision of the same Returning Member Program would expand the PGA Tour's playoff field from 70 players to 71.

The clause written for four LIV golfers in December could end up rewriting the size of the playoffs in August. Somebody drafted that sentence never expecting to need it.

What This Is Really About

Everyone who plays golf has made a version of this decision, at a scale of roughly nothing.

You commit to something — a club, a coach, a swing change, a set of irons you spent too much on — and then the conditions change and you're left holding a choice you made with the information available at the time.

The temptation afterwards is to pretend you knew. Smith isn't doing that. He said in February that he'd made his bed, and this week, asked the obvious question, he didn't pretend the bed was fine. He said he wants it to work, and that if it doesn't, he'd go where the golf is.

That's about as honest as anybody in professional golf has been all year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Cameron Smith leaving LIV Golf for the PGA Tour?

No. He said that if LIV folded and the PGA Tour were the only option, he would return. He also said he wants LIV to survive, is contracted to the end of next year, and expects to play out that deal.

Could Smith return to the PGA Tour if he wanted to?

Not through the route Koepka used. The Returning Member Program's transfer window opened in December 2025 and closed on 2 February 2026. Smith, Rahm and DeChambeau all declined it at the time.

What did Brooks Koepka give up to return?

A $5 million charitable donation, no PGA Tour equity grants for five years, no FedExCup bonus money in 2026, and no sponsor exemptions into the Tour's $20 million signature events.

Is the LIV Team Championship going ahead?

Unclear. Players say they've been told to prepare as though it is. Martin Kaymer has put it at around a five per cent chance. LIV has issued no statement. It's scheduled for 27-30 August in Michigan.

Why is LIV Golf in financial trouble?

Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund announced in April that it would stop funding the league after the 2026 season, having financed it through loans rather than equity — which leaves LIV with creditors rather than shareholders.

The Raw Take

The thing that strikes me about this whole saga is how much of it turns on paperwork nobody read at the time.

A clause defining who counts as an elite returner. A window with a closing date. A programme that let exactly four men out and charged one of them five million dollars for the privilege. A provision that might quietly turn a 70-man playoff into a 71-man playoff.

Golf presents itself as a game of shots and nerve and weather. At this level it's a game of documents, and the players are mostly finding out what's in them at the same time as the rest of us.

Smith said he'd made his bed. He's now lying in it while people outside argue about whether the house is being repossessed.
