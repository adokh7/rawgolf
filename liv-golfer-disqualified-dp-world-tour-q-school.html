A LIV Golfer Was Disqualified From DP World Tour Q School for Not Turning Up

Two rounds, 74 and 73, tied 66th and seven over. He needed a top 19 to advance. On Friday morning he wasn't on the first tee.

By GOLFRAW Editorial Published 04 Sep 2026 Updated 04 Sep 2026, 23:50 CEST 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Missed tee time equals DQ: Peter Uihlein was disqualified from Stage 1 of DP World Tour Q School at Huddersfield after failing to appear for round three.
  • Rule 5.3a has no flex: Arrive up to five minutes late and you take a two-stroke penalty; past five minutes, disqualification is mandatory.
  • Seven over par after 36 holes: Uihlein sat tied 66th with rounds of 74 and 73, well outside the top 19 and ties required to advance.
  • LIV players seeking alternatives: Three LIV golfers entered Huddersfield amid league financing uncertainty and potential Chapter 11 filing.
  • No official explanation: Neither Uihlein nor the tour has confirmed an illness, injury, travel issue or intentional withdrawal.

Two rounds, 74 and 73, tied 66th and seven over. He needed a top 19 to advance. On Friday morning he wasn't on the first tee.

Peter Uihlein was disqualified from the first stage of DP World Tour Qualifying School at Huddersfield Golf Club on Friday, September 4, 2026, after failing to appear for his third-round tee time. The 37-year-old had opened 74-73 to sit tied 66th at seven over par. Only the top 19 and ties advance to the second stage.

Nobody has published a reason for the no-show, and I'm not going to invent one. What's worth your time is the rule that governs this, because it's the fastest disqualification available in golf and most amateurs have never read it. Third section.

Built on: Field Level Media's report syndicated across multiple outlets, SportBible's account of the Huddersfield first stage, Yardbarker's relay of Bunkered's reporting, and the USGA and R&A's published Rules of Golf.

What actually happened at Huddersfield

Four over on Thursday. One better on Friday's second round. Then nothing.

Field Level Media reports Uihlein opened with rounds of 74 and 73 to sit tied for 66th at seven over par entering the third round, citing Golfweek, and that he was disqualified after a no-show for his third-round tee time at Huddersfield Golf Club in West Yorkshire.

Bunkered's Josh Lees broke it on X, per Yardbarker, confirming the DQ from the first stage and the missed tee time.

He was one of three LIV players in that field. Spain's Luis Masaveu and José Luis Ballester, also listed as Josele Ballester, made up the trio.

No explanation has been reported by any outlet I could open. Not injury, not withdrawal, not travel. Treat anyone offering a reason as guessing.

Why three LIV players were there at all

Because the exits are closing and Q School is one of the few left open.

SportBible's report frames it plainly: the three headed to Q School amid continued uncertainty over LIV's future, after the Public Investment Fund withdrew funding. LIV CEO Scott O'Neil has been working on a proposed investment for 2027 and beyond.

Reuters, carrying the Financial Times, reported that LIV may file for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection as soon as the week of September 7, with PIF expected to lend under $100 million in bankruptcy financing.

So a man with a LIV contract of uncertain value spent his week in West Yorkshire trying to earn a card the hard way.

That's the part of this story that deserves sympathy rather than mockery. Peter Uihlein was a world-class amateur and a PGA Tour player. In September 2026 he's shooting 74 at a first stage in Huddersfield because the league he signed with can't tell him whether it exists next year.

The rule that ended his week

Rule 5.3a, and it has no mercy built in.

The current Rules of Golf state that a player must start at their starting time and not before, with disqualification as the default penalty. There's one narrow escape: arrive at the starting point ready to play no more than five minutes late and you take the general penalty on your first hole instead, which is two strokes in stroke play.

Five minutes. That's the whole window.

The rule reads the time as exact. A 9:12 tee time means 9:12:00. Golf.com reported that Garrick Higgo took two penalty strokes at the 2026 PGA Championship for arriving late to a 7:18 a.m. start, turning an opening par into a double bogey.

Rule 5.4 sits alongside it and is even blunter, requiring you to play in the group the committee set, with disqualification for a breach and no two-shot alternative.

At your club, nobody enforces either one. Roll up two minutes late to your Saturday medal and the starter waves you through. That's a local courtesy, not the rule.

Rules of Golf Breakdown: Rule 5.3a & Rule 5.4

Rule 5.3a (Starting and Ending Round): The player must be ready to play at their exact starting time. If the player arrives ready to play within five minutes after the starting time, the penalty is General Penalty (two strokes in stroke play) applied to the first hole. Arrive more than five minutes late and the penalty is Disqualification.

Rule 5.4 (Playing in Groups): During a round, the player must remain in the group set by the Committee. Changing groups without committee approval results in Disqualification with no five-minute leniency window.

What Q School's first stage actually asks

Top 19 and ties. Out of a full field, at a course most of them have never seen.

Field Level Media confirms only the top 19 players with ties advance from Huddersfield to the next stage. Uihlein was 47 places and several shots outside that with 36 holes to play.

The honest read is that his week was probably over on Thursday afternoon. Seven over after two rounds at a first stage, needing to climb into the top 19, is close to a lost cause before the DQ arrived.

That doesn't make the no-show less costly. It makes it look like a decision rather than an accident, which is exactly the sort of thing nobody in the reporting can confirm.

The season he was having

Two thirds, back to back, to open the year.

Field Level Media notes Uihlein began the 2026 LIV season with consecutive third-place finishes at Riyadh and Adelaide. He's a member of RangeGoats GC and joined the breakaway league in 2022.

Bunkered's 2026 roster listing puts him on Bubba Watson's RangeGoats alongside Ben Campbell and Matt Wolff.

Two thirds in February, a first stage of Q School in September. That's the arc of a full year in professional golf when your tour's finances collapse underneath you.

Four things being said about this that don't hold up

  • "Jay Monahan has ruled out a path back for LIV players." Two outlets carrying this story name Monahan as PGA Tour commissioner. The Tour's CEO is Brian Rolapp, who said in late August there are no current plans to reopen the Returning Member Program. Same conclusion, wrong name.
  • "He withdrew." He was disqualified, per Field Level Media and Bunkered, for failing to appear. Those aren't the same thing on a scorecard or in the record.
  • "We know why he didn't show." Nobody has published a reason. Not the DP World Tour, not Uihlein, not any outlet I could open.
  • "He was in contention." He was tied 66th at seven over, needing a top 19 and ties to advance. The DQ ended a week that was already in serious trouble.

Verification block

Confirmed: Uihlein's 74-73 opening rounds; tied 66th position at +7; disqualification for missed round-three tee time; Top 19 and ties progression criteria at Huddersfield; Rule 5.3a and Rule 5.4 penalties under R&A/USGA Rules of Golf.

Unverified: The personal or logistical reason behind Uihlein's absence from the tee has not been disclosed by the player, his management, or the tournament committee.

The Raw Verdict

Two outlets ran this as a punchline about a millionaire who couldn't manage an alarm clock. What it actually shows is a good player, tied 66th at a first stage, in a week that was already gone before Friday morning.

The transferable lesson is the boring one. Rule 5.3a gives you five minutes and nothing else, and it applies at Huddersfield exactly as it applies at your club's championship. Nobody in the history of this game has ever regretted arriving twenty minutes early.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which LIV golfer was disqualified from DP World Tour Q School?

Peter Uihlein, 37, at the first stage held at Huddersfield Golf Club in West Yorkshire. He was disqualified on Friday, September 4, 2026 after failing to show up for his third-round tee time, having opened with rounds of 74 and 73.

Why was he disqualified?

For not appearing at his tee time. Rule 5.3a of the Rules of Golf requires a player to start at their starting time, with disqualification as the penalty unless they arrive ready to play within five minutes, which reduces it to the general penalty on the first hole.

Where did he stand before the DQ?

Tied for 66th at seven over par after 36 holes, according to Field Level Media citing Golfweek. Only the top 19 players and ties advance from the first stage to the second, so he needed a substantial move over the closing rounds.

Why are LIV players entering Q School?

Because their options are narrowing. SportBible reports Uihlein, Luis Masaveu and José Luis Ballester all entered amid uncertainty over LIV's future after PIF withdrew funding, with the Financial Times reporting a possible Chapter 11 filing as soon as the week of September 7.

Can LIV players return to the PGA Tour instead?

Not easily. The Returning Member Program window closed on February 2, 2026 and only Brooks Koepka used it. PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp said in late August that there are no current plans to reopen it, and that returning players must be free of contractual obligations.
