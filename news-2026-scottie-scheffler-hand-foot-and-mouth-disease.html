Scottie Scheffler battles hand-foot-and-mouth disease at Bellerive

Scottie Scheffler played the 2026 BMW Championship with hand-foot-and-mouth disease. The World No. 1 shot 68 on Sunday despite painful grip blisters.

By GOLFRAW Editorial Published 24 Aug 2026 Updated 24 Aug 2026, 16:00 CEST 5 min read

The short read

  • Scottie Scheffler competed throughout the 2026 BMW Championship with hand-foot-and-mouth disease (HFMD).
  • Painful blisters on his palms and fingers severely compromised his grip pressure during early rounds.
  • Scheffler adapted his routine and posted a final-round 68 (-4) at Bellerive Country Club.
  • He remains atop the FedExCup standings heading into the Tour Championship at East Lake.

Scottie Scheffler faced an unexpected test of endurance at the 2026 BMW Championship at Bellerive Country Club. The World No. 1 revealed following Sunday's round that he had been managing symptoms of hand-foot-and-mouth disease (HFMD), a viral illness known for causing painful skin eruptions and blisters on the palms and soles.

Despite the severe discomfort on every full-speed strike, Scheffler produced a 4-under 68 on Sunday, demonstrating remarkable fortitude during the penultimate event of the PGA Tour playoffs.

Managing grip pressure through painful blisters

Hand-foot-and-mouth disease causes viral blisters that make direct pressure on the palms and fingers exceptionally tender. For a golfer who relies on precise tactile feedback and firm release, every tee shot and long-iron approach became a test of pain management.

"It wasn't easy to hold the club on Thursday and Friday, but we managed it step by step and found a way to score." Scottie Scheffler on post-round recovery at Bellerive

Scheffler's team applied medical tape and protective barriers to ease friction on his lead hand, ensuring he could swing without tearing sensitive skin while maintaining compliance with tournament rules.
