Scottie Scheffler battles hand-foot-and-mouth disease at Bellerive

World No. 1 golfer Scottie Scheffler fought through painful blisters to finish T12 at the 2026 BMW Championship.

By GOLFRAW Editorial Published 24 Aug 2026 Updated 24 Aug 2026, 16:40 CEST 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Scottie Scheffler played the entire 2026 BMW Championship while suffering from hand-foot-and-mouth disease.
  • The virus caused a fever, a sore throat, and painful blisters on his hands that made gripping a golf club difficult.
  • Scheffler recorded a final-round 68 on Sunday to secure a tie for 12th place at Bellerive Country Club.
  • He retains the No. 1 seed heading into the FedEx Cup finale at East Lake Golf Club.

Scottie Scheffler played the entire 2026 BMW Championship with painful blisters covering his hands. The World No. 1 revealed on 23 August 2026 that he spent the week battling hand-foot-and-mouth disease. Despite the physical impairment, Scheffler managed to shoot a 2-under 68 in his final round at Bellerive Country Club to finish tied for 12th.

He confirmed the viral diagnosis to the media after walking off the 18th green. He already had the top playoff seed locked up before he hit his first tee shot on Thursday. But as reported across tournament broadcasts, Scheffler chose to play through the pain out of sheer competitive stubbornness.

What illness did Scottie Scheffler contract at the BMW Championship?

Scottie Scheffler contracted hand-foot-and-mouth disease. The highly contagious viral illness usually tears through daycares and preschools. It causes a fever, a sore throat, and painful blisters on the hands, feet, and mouth.

Reports confirmed that Scheffler dealt with those classic symptoms all week. He admitted it hurt just to grip his golf clubs during the early rounds. There is no cure for the virus, which meant Scheffler simply had to manage the fever and the friction on his hands without any medical relief.

From Memphis dominance to Monday night fever

The symptoms hit him on Monday night, 17 August 2026. Just 24 hours earlier, he had cruised to an eight-stroke victory at the FedEx St. Jude Championship in Memphis. The sudden fever meant he arrived at the BMW Championship completely compromised.

Scheffler told reporters that players often crash physically after winning a demanding, hot-weather tournament. But this fever hit him unusually hard. His condition improved slightly as the week wore on, which allowed him to close with that 68 on Sunday.

Why Scheffler refused to withdraw despite having the No. 1 seed locked

Scheffler admitted the smart medical move was to withdraw. His blowout win in Memphis already guaranteed he would enter the Tour Championship as the No. 1 seed. Playing Bellerive offered him absolutely nothing in the FedEx Cup standings.

He blamed his decision to play on his own stubbornness. Scheffler said he was hitting the ball so well before getting sick that he wanted to test himself. He figured if he could just survive the opening rounds, he might still have a chance to contend. Plus, he hates withdrawing in the middle of a tournament.

How the world's best golfer caught a pediatric virus

Adults rarely catch hand-foot-and-mouth disease. When they do, they usually get it from close contact with infected toddlers. Scheffler's son, Bennett, was born in May 2024, providing the standard environment for a parent to pick up a pediatric virus.

  1. The virus spreads through direct contact with unwashed hands, saliva, or respiratory droplets.
  2. Adults usually experience milder symptoms than kids, but blisters on the hands and feet remain the defining feature.
  3. A professional golfer applies intense friction to their hands on every swing. That pressure makes palmar blisters agonizing.

Why Scheffler caught himself before shaking Cameron Young's hand

The disease stays highly contagious as long as the blisters are present. That forced Scheffler to abandon his normal post-round routine. He told the media he almost made the mistake of shaking hands with his playing partner, Cameron Young, after finishing his final round on Sunday.

Professional golf treats the 18th-green handshake as a mandatory tradition. Scheffler managed to catch himself before making contact, sparing Young from the virus.

Will Scheffler play in the Tour Championship at East Lake?

Scheffler heads to the Tour Championship at East Lake Golf Club on Thursday, 27 August 2026, as the No. 1 overall seed. Thanks to the starting strokes format, he starts the tournament with a two-stroke lead over the field.

He confirmed on Sunday that his hands finally felt normal. The blisters had healed enough to handle his normal grip pressure, though his throat was still sore. He plans to rest through Wednesday to get ready for the season finale.

Hand-foot-and-mouth disease is a highly contagious viral illness that typically affects young children, characterized by a fever, sore throat, and painful blisters on the hands, feet, and mouth.

Can adults get hand-foot-and-mouth disease?

Yes, adults can contract hand-foot-and-mouth disease, typically from close contact with infected toddlers, though it presents less frequently than in children.
