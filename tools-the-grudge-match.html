Questions people actually ask

Does the lower handicap always win?

No, and it is not close to always. Over one 18-hole match a two-shot gap is roughly a 60/40 proposition, not a formality. Golf has enough variance in it that a single result tells you almost nothing about who is better — which is precisely why the loser always wants to go again, and why he is statistically entitled to.

Why is the fair number lower than the full difference?

In matchplay a hole is a hole. When the higher handicapper makes a nine it costs him one hole; when you make a six it costs you one hole. Giving the full difference hands him the upside of his bad golf without the downside. In strokeplay the effect reverses, because there every one of those extra shots stays on the card.

What does the consistency slider actually change?

The spread of your hole scores, not their average. A wild player and a steady player with the same handicap shoot the same number over a season and win completely different matches. Wildness wins you more blowouts and loses you more tight ones.

Is this a real simulation or a lookup table?

Real. Every run plays 180,000 individual holes in your browser, scoring each one from a distribution built off the two handicaps, then counts the results. Run it twice and the numbers will move slightly, because that is what probability looks like when nobody is rounding it for you.
