This Week in Golf: LIV's Filing, Tiger's Plea, and a Rebrand That's a Capital Letter

Three headlines, three different levels of certainty. One is a report about next week, one happened this morning with two different names attached, and one is the removal of an uppercase O.

BY GOLFRAW EDITORIAL PUBLISHED WED 02 SEP 2026

This week in golf: LIV Golf may file for bankruptcy protection as soon as the week of September 7, per the Financial Times. Tiger Woods resolved his Florida DUI case with pleas to reckless driving and refusal to submit to testing, receiving a five-year license suspension. The PGA Tour is reportedly dropping its all-caps "PGA TOUR" styling.

What that summary hides is how much of the week's noise rests on things that haven't happened yet. The section on what's confirmed versus what's reported is where this piece actually earns its keep.

Built on: Golf Monthly's This Week In Golf roundup, the Associated Press report from Martin County via WLRN, court-record reporting from Tampa Bay 28, Reuters' account of the Financial Times LIV story, and earlier verified reporting on the Presidents Cup format.

LIV Golf: reported, not filed

Nothing has been lodged in any court. Keep that sentence handy.

Golf Monthly's roundup relays the Financial Times report that LIV may file for bankruptcy protection as early as next week, and adds something the FT didn't: a source close to the situation told Golf Monthly that a financial restructuring is expected to be a necessary step in the transition to LIV 2.0, which would see the league majority-owned by any players who remain.

Reuters, carrying the same FT story, puts the possible filing in New Jersey federal court with PIF expected to lend under $100 million in bankruptcy financing. Reuters also stated it could not verify the report, and neither LIV nor PIF commented.

The settlement offers already sent to players owed guaranteed money beyond 2026 came in at a few cents on the dollar, per the FT. BC Partners' credit division has been negotiating a funding package for weeks, with executives attending LIV's final two events.

So the confirmed part is that a restructuring is being planned. The unconfirmed part is the date, the court and whether anyone signs.

Tiger Woods: resolved, with two names for the plea

He walked into Martin County court on Wednesday and walked out without a license for five years.

Court-record reporting from Tampa Bay 28 says Judge Darren Steele accepted an agreement amending the DUI with property damage charge to reckless driving, second offense, and resolving the refusal-to-submit charge, plus a careless driving citation. The penalty on the reckless driving count: a $1,000 fine, a five-year driver's license suspension and $18.44 to the state attorney's office.

Here's the wrinkle. The AP, via WLRN, reports the plea as no contest on both charges, and explains that a no-contest plea isn't an admission of guilt but is treated as a conviction. CBS News and several other outlets report a guilty plea.

Same outcome either way. Different legal posture. Nobody has published the filing.

Golf Monthly's roundup adds background: the March crash near his Jupiter Island home saw Woods roll his vehicle and climb out through the passenger window, pass a breath test, and refuse a urinalysis before pleading not guilty and travelling to Switzerland for treatment.

The PGA Tour rebrand is a capitalisation change

An O and a U and an R, demoted from uppercase.

Golf Monthly's roundup attributes the story to No Laying Up's Tron Carter, reporting that "the PGA TOUR" is removing the all-caps version of its name and becoming "the PGA Tour", while its headquarters, known as the Global Home, will be called PGA Tour Campus.

That's a Tier 2 outlet relaying a podcast personality. I couldn't find an announcement from the Tour itself, and I'd treat it as credible but unofficial until one appears.

Small as it is, there's something in it. The Tour's all-caps house style has been a running joke in golf media for years, the sort of thing copy editors quietly override. If the organisation is finally matching how everyone already writes its name, that's a tiny sign of an outfit that's stopped insisting.

Still. Anyone calling this a rebrand has never sat through a real one.

Presidents Cup rosters, and the dates people keep getting wrong

Twenty-four names, three American debutants, and a schedule that reads two ways.

Golf Monthly lists the finalised teams. Team USA: Scottie Scheffler, Cameron Young, Wyndham Clark, Sam Burns, Russell Henley and Collin Morikawa as automatic qualifiers, with Brandt Snedeker picking Patrick Cantlay, Xander Schauffele, Justin Thomas and debutants Jacob Bridgeman, Chris Gotterup and Jackson Koivun.

Team International: Si Woo Kim, Ryan Fox, Hideki Matsuyama, Min Woo Lee, Tom Kim and Adam Scott, with Geoff Ogilvy adding Christiaan Bezuidenhout, Corey Conners, Sungjae Im and debutants Nico Echavarria, Ryo Hisatsune and Nick Taylor.

Golf Monthly gives the event as Tuesday September 22 through Sunday September 27 at Medinah. Published format guides list competition from Thursday September 24, with the earlier days for practice. Both are right. Check what your ticket covers before you assume Tuesday means golf.

The Americans have won ten in a row. The International side hasn't won since 1998.

The number nobody's leading with

Five point seven million. That's the audience.

Golf Monthly reports that CBS delivered the most-watched Tour Championship final round since Tiger Woods' 2018 win, averaging 5.728 million viewers on Sunday, up 28% on 2025, with a peak of 6.675 million.

Scheffler came from three back, passed Woods on the career money list, and more people watched it than any finale in eight years.

Sit that next to the LIV story. One league is negotiating cents-on-the-dollar settlements with its stars. The other just posted its best finale audience since the most famous golfer alive was winning it. Whatever the merits of the past four years' arguments, the scoreboard this week isn't close.

The rest of the week, quickly

The Walker Cup at Lahinch, and a mound named Klondyke.

Golf Monthly reports the USA chasing a sixth straight win with seven of the world's top 10 amateurs, including No. 1 Preston Stout, who flew a practice-round drive over the Klondyke mound on the 4th, roughly 340 yards from the tee. GB&I's hopes rest on world No. 2 Tyler Weaver and No. 8 Luke Poulter.

The DP World Tour heads to Crans-sur-Sierre for the $3.75 million Omega European Masters, with five LIV golfers in the field. Jon Rahm is confirmed for the BMW PGA Championship in two weeks. Miguel Ángel Jiménez plays his 725th DP World Tour event, one short of David Howell's appearance record.

Five WTGL teams are finalised for a November debut, with Charley Hull, Minjee Lee, Lexi Thompson and Lottie Woad on rosters and Nelly Korda absent. And the USGA has placed Colt Knost's application to regain amateur status on hold while guidelines are reevaluated, per his own post relayed by Golf Monthly.

Five things being said about this week that don't hold up

Big news weeks produce bad summaries at scale.

  • "LIV has filed for bankruptcy." It hasn't. The FT reports it may file as soon as the week of September 7. Reuters couldn't verify it and neither party commented.
  • "Tiger pleaded guilty." The AP, reporting from the courtroom, says no contest. Other outlets say guilty. Both are convictions. Nobody has published the document.
  • "The PGA Tour rebranded." It's reportedly dropping its all-caps styling, per a podcast relayed by Golf Monthly. No official announcement was available.
  • "Golf's TV audience is shrinking." CBS's Tour Championship final round drew 5.728 million on average, the most since 2018 and up 28% on last year.
  • "The Presidents Cup starts Tuesday." The event week does. Competition begins Thursday September 24 according to format guides. Tuesday and Wednesday are practice.

FAQ

Has LIV Golf filed for bankruptcy?

No. The Financial Times reported on August 31 that a filing could come as soon as the week of September 7 in New Jersey federal court. A source told Golf Monthly a restructuring is expected as part of the transition to a player-owned LIV 2.0. Reuters could not verify the report.

What happened with Tiger Woods in court?

He resolved his Florida DUI case on September 2 with pleas to reckless driving, second offense, and refusal to submit to a lawful test, plus a careless driving citation. He received a five-year license suspension and a $1,000 fine. The AP reports the pleas as no contest; others report guilty.

Is the PGA Tour changing its name?

Not its name, its capitalisation. Golf Monthly relayed a report from No Laying Up's Tron Carter that "PGA TOUR" is becoming "PGA Tour" and its headquarters will be called PGA Tour Campus. No official announcement from the Tour was available.

When is the 2026 Presidents Cup?

The event week runs September 22 to 27 at Medinah Country Club near Chicago, with competition on September 24 to 27. Rosters were finalised this week, with Brandt Snedeker and Geoff Ogilvy each naming six captain's picks on Tuesday evening.

How many people watched the Tour Championship?

An average of 5.728 million on CBS for Sunday's final round, per Golf Monthly, up 28% on 2025 with a peak of 6.675 million. It was the most-watched Tour Championship final round since Tiger Woods won in 2018.

The Raw Verdict

Read this week in the right order. The Tour Championship audience is a fact. Tiger's five-year suspension is a fact with a disputed label. LIV's bankruptcy is a well-sourced plan. The rebrand is a keyboard setting.

If you only take one thing, take the ratings. Five and a half million people watched a man come from three back on a Sunday while the rival league negotiated pennies with its stars. Golf's civil war didn't end with a merger. It's ending with an audience, and the audience already picked.
