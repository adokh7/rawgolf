Scottie Scheffler's 2026 Tour Championship odds: +310

Scottie Scheffler is +310 at East Lake. We convert the 2026 Tour Championship odds into a break-even win rate and price the other 29 players against it.

Scottie Scheffler's 2026 Tour Championship odds start at +310. The number is useful only after you translate it. Positive American odds of +310 mean a $100 stake would return $310 in profit if it wins, and the break-even probability is 100 ÷ (310 + 100) = 24.39% before any bookmaker margin. That is the clean answer to the first question, not a prediction that Scheffler will win.

The reset matters. The PGA TOUR's 2026 format sends all 30 FedExCup qualifiers to East Lake at even par for 72 holes. There are no starting strokes to fold into the price, so the board is comparing the same four-round task for every player. The sources for the event facts are linked below; the prices and the 7.3x flat-field figure are the supplied inputs for this worked example.

What does +310 mean for Scottie Scheffler?

It means the price breaks even at 24.39% before margin. The formula is 100 ÷ (American odds + 100), so 100 ÷ (310 + 100) = 0.243902..., or 24.39% when rounded to two decimal places.

Break-even probability is not the same as a fair forecast. A sportsbook can shade a line, move it after a bet, or build margin into a complete board. Scheffler's +310 is a single price; it cannot tell you the no-vig probability of the whole field by itself.

How does the market split between Scheffler and the other 29?

Using the break-even rate as a field-share model leaves about 75.61% for the other 29 players. Divide that remainder by 29 and the average is 2.61%, which is approximately +3735 in positive American odds.

This is a consistency check, not a claim that every sportsbook posts +3735 on every other golfer. The 29-player average is a synthetic line created by the supplied +310 anchor and the assumption that the implied probabilities sum to 100%.

Why does the even-par reset matter to the board?

Every player begins at the same score, so Scheffler's +310 is attached to four rounds of relative performance rather than a starting-strokes advantage. The field split is easier to inspect because the format and the probability model are both explicit.

That does not make the number static. Course fit, health, weather, tee times, withdrawals and market limits can move a price. It only makes the starting condition unambiguous: 30 players, even par, 72 holes at East Lake.

What should a bettor check before using +310?

Audit the line before you compare it with your own estimate. A five-step check prevents a tidy percentage from becoming false precision.

  1. Timestamp the quote. Record the sportsbook, jurisdiction, market and exact time; +310 can move.
  2. Keep the full precision. Convert 100 ÷ 410 as a decimal and round only when displaying 24.39%.
  3. Separate break-even from no-vig. The former uses one price; the latter needs the complete 30-player board.
  4. Recheck the field. Confirm the 30 names, tee times and late withdrawals before using the other-29 average.
  5. Set a limit before staking. Compare the price with your own number, bet only where legal and age-appropriate, and do not chase a line.
