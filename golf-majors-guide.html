The 4 Golf Majors Explained: Winners, Venues & Prestige

Golf has four major tournaments: the Masters, the PGA Championship, the U.S. Open, and The Open. Here is the comprehensive guide to what makes each one special and why they matter.

BY GOLFRAW EDITORIAL VERIFIED & UPDATED: AUGUST 2026

You are watching golf with a friend who knows it well, and they say something like "this is a major, it actually matters." You nod along, but inside you are lost. Which tournaments are the big ones? Why do some count more than others? What is this Grand Slam everyone mentions?

Here is the short answer, right up front. Golf has four major tournaments: The Masters, the PGA Championship, the U.S. Open, and The Open Championship. These four are the biggest, most important events of the year. Win one and a player is set for life. Everything else is a regular tour event.

This is raw golf. Below is the comprehensive guide to the four majors, the qualification rules, historic prestige, and 20-year winner tables so you can track who has dominated the modern era.

What Makes a Tournament a "Major"?

Players compete almost every week, but only four tournaments a year are majors. It comes down to three things: history, prestige, and the field. The majors are the oldest and most respected events, they carry the most pressure and money, and they bring together the best players in the world all at once. Careers get measured by how many majors a player has won, not how many regular events.

There are exactly four, and they have been the recognized majors of modern golf for decades. Let us go through them in the order they are played each year.

1. The Masters Tournament

Played in April. The Masters is the only major played at the exact same course every single year: Augusta National Golf Club in Georgia. It is arguably the most famous and exclusive tournament in the world.

The winner gets the famous Green Jacket. It’s a tradition unlike any other, with pristine fairways, flowering azaleas, and rules that restrict everything from what commentators can say to where fans (called "patrons") can walk.

Who gets in? The Masters has the smallest field of the majors (around 90 players). It is an invitational. Winners of the Masters get a lifetime invitation, and the rest of the field is made up of past major winners, recent PGA Tour winners, and the top 50 in the world ranking.

2. The PGA Championship

Played in May. The PGA Championship moves around the United States to different elite courses. It is run by the PGA of America, the organization for club professionals (the men and women who teach golf and run local courses), rather than the PGA Tour (the professional playing circuit).

The winner gets the massive Wanamaker Trophy. The tournament is famous for regularly boasting the strongest field in golf based on world rankings, as it invites almost the entire top 100.

Who gets in? The top 15 from last year's PGA Championship, recent major winners, Ryder Cup players, and 20 club professionals who qualify through a special tournament. The rest are filled from the PGA Tour money list and world rankings.

3. The U.S. Open

Played in June. Run by the United States Golf Association (USGA), the U.S. Open is famously known as the toughest test in golf. The USGA intentionally sets up courses to be brutal: narrow fairways, thick rough, and lightning-fast greens.

The goal is to test every aspect of a player's physical and mental game. Winning scores are often around even par.

Who gets in? The U.S. Open is "open." While top players get automatic exemptions based on world ranking and past wins, nearly half the field is made up of players (both pro and amateur) who survive local and final qualifying tournaments. In theory, a scratch golfer off the street could win it.

4. The Open Championship

Played in July. Also called the British Open in the U.S., this is the oldest golf tournament in the world, dating back to 1860. It is always played on a links course in the UK—courses built on the sandy, windy land between the sea and arable farmland.

The Open is golf in its purest form. Players battle high winds, deep pot bunkers, and sideways rain. Creativity and controlling the flight of the ball matter more here than anywhere else. The winner is named the "Champion Golfer of the Year" and receives the legendary Claret Jug.

Who gets in? Like the U.S. Open, it has open qualifying along with automatic exemptions for the top 50 players in the world and winners of major global tours.
