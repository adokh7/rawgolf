Club Glove Pro Traveler: Is It Worth It If You Only Fly a Couple of Times a Year?

Two bags sell as the Club Glove Pro Traveler right now. What changed in 2025, whether your bag fits, the airline weight math, and when to buy the cheaper one.

BY GOLFRAW EDITORIAL PUBLISHED THU 03 SEP 2026 7 MIN READ

Quick Answer: The Club Glove Pro Traveler is the soft-sided golf travel bag most tour players use, and the 2025 version finally added the padding around the clubheads that the older one never had. If you fly with clubs three or more times a year, it's a sound buy that'll outlast several cheaper bags. If you fly once a year, the discounted prior-generation version does the same basic job for less. And if the thing you hate most about golf travel is dragging thirty-odd pounds through a terminal, a different bag entirely wins that fight.

Two bags share this name, and the difference matters

Right now you can buy two products called the Club Glove Pro Traveler, and retailers don't always tell you which one you're looking at.

The prior generation, sold by Club Glove as model CG24TVPRO, is the direct descendant of the bag golfers called the Last Bag for twenty years. Club Glove lists it with a 1000-denier water-resistant nylon shell, YKK zippers, ITW Nexus buckles, two external pockets, a Stiff Arm included, and a weight of 10.6 pounds. What it doesn't have is padding around the clubheads. The old Club Glove philosophy was that a burst-proof shell plus a Stiff Arm was enough, and for a lot of people it was.

The 2025 version, model CG25TVPRO, is a different bag. Club Glove's current page lists a 2000-denier polyester exterior, a 360-degree velour-lined padded clubhead area, a built-in TSA-approved combination lock, a shock-absorbing wheelbase, an interior stability strap, urethane wheels, and synthetic leather touchpoints. Weight goes up to 11.4 pounds. The Stiff Arm is still included.

The two generations are listed with different exterior figures and Club Glove doesn't say whether it changed the way it measures, so treat those numbers as the manufacturer's own rather than a like-for-like comparison. Don't read the 2025 as narrower until you've checked it against your bag.

If a listing says "velour" or "TSA lock," it's the 2025 bag. If it says "1000 Denier nylon" and "two external storage pockets," it's the prior generation, and it's often discounted under exactly that label.

One more naming wrinkle. You'll see listings titled "Titleist by Club Glove." That's the same company. Acushnet, the parent of Titleist and FootJoy, bought the Club Glove brand from West Coast Trends in February 2023, and some product listings picked up the Titleist name afterward.

Will your golf bag fit inside it?

Club Glove sizes the Pro Traveler as "large" and recommends it for stand bags and cart bags. That covers most people reading this.

If you carry a staff bag, the tour-size one with the big brand logo, you want the Tour Traveler instead, which Club Glove lists at 55 by 16.5 by 15 inches. Going the other way, if you travel with a slim carry bag and don't pack much else, the Tour Traveler SB at 52 by 13 by 13 inches or the Club Traveler (formerly the College Traveler) is a lighter fit. The Club Traveler uses a 1680-denier polyester shell and includes the same Stiff Arm.

The Pro Traveler's extra room is for the trip, not the clubs. Club Glove positions it for four-to-seven-day trips, and one owner review on Golf Galaxy describes fitting a stand bag, shoes, and four days of golf clothes in it with room to spare. That's the honest use case: one checked item instead of two.

How much protection are you actually getting?

More than the old bag gave you, and roughly as much as the best soft cases on the market. That's the fair summary.

The Stiff Arm is the part that matters most, and it's included with every Pro Traveler. It's an adjustable pole that sits in your bag above the tallest club so that anything dropped on top of the case compresses the pole, not your driver shaft. MyGolfSpy's travel bag guides single out this kind of support bar as the feature that stops shafts bending or snapping when a bag gets stacked in a hold.

The 2025 velour padding is the upgrade the previous bag lacked. Independent testing supports the change. The Hackers Paradise put a 2025 Pro Traveler through three airlines and six airports in a May 2026 review, deliberately leaving headcovers on and relying on the padding and Stiff Arm, and reported no club damage with only light scuffing on the wheelbase. That's a sample of one, but it's a described test rather than a listing bullet.

MyGolfSpy's 2026 travel bag test, which compared 25 bags, listed the Pro Traveler among the most durable options alongside the Sun Mountain ClubGlider Meridian, the PING Rolling Travel Cover, and the OGIO Renegade. One recurring point from MyGolfSpy readers who own both a Club Glove and a ClubGlider is that Club Glove's plastic base wraps further up the bottom of the bag, which they credit for better protection at the point baggage handlers drop it on.

Warranty is where I'd push back a bit. Club Glove's product pages list a one-year limited warranty. Some cheaper competitors on Amazon, CaddyDaddy for instance, advertise eighteen-month or two-year full replacement coverage. A bag at this price with a shorter warranty than a budget bag is a real trade-off, even if Club Glove's reputation for lasting a decade is well earned.

The airport math nobody does before buying

The Pro Traveler weighs 11.4 pounds before you put anything in it. Most US airlines start charging overweight fees at 50 pounds. That leaves you 38.6 pounds for your golf bag, fourteen clubs, shoes, balls, rain gear, and whatever clothes you stuffed in.

A loaded cart bag eats most of that. Weigh the packed travel case on a bathroom scale before you leave for the airport, because a $100 overweight fee each way turns a one-trip bag into a very expensive one.

On the rules side, Delta counts one golf bag per passenger as a standard checked item, applies excess-weight fees above 50 pounds, and won't accept anything over 115 linear inches. American, United, and Southwest publish similar treatment, with Southwest's two free checked bags being the outlier that makes it the cheapest carrier for golfers. Policies change, so check the airline actually operating your flight. Some carriers' fine print also recommends hard-sided cases and may limit their liability for damage inside a soft cover, which is worth reading before you file a claim rather than after.

The built-in TSA lock on the 2025 bag is a convenience, not a security feature. TSA agents can open it with a master key, which is the point. It stops the zipper drifting open in a hold and it deters casual tampering. Don't put anything in there you'd be upset to lose.

Where it loses to the Sun Mountain ClubGlider

If your main complaint about golf travel is your back, the Pro Traveler isn't the answer.

MyGolfSpy named the Sun Mountain ClubGlider Meridian its best overall travel bag in both 2025 and 2026. The reason is a retractable leg mechanism with pivoting wheels that lets the bag stand on four points and roll upright, so you steer it with a finger instead of dragging thirty pounds behind you on two wheels. Testers describe it as almost rolling itself. Club Glove's answer is the Train Reaction System, which links multiple Club Glove pieces into a self-balancing train, but that only helps if you own more than one Club Glove bag.

A MyGolfSpy reader with two years on a ClubGlider and prior Club Glove ownership put it plainly: the ClubGlider wins on convenience, the Club Glove wins on the base protection. That matches the test results. Pick based on which of those two problems you actually have.

Who should buy the Pro Traveler, and who should save the money

  • Buy the 2025 Pro Traveler if you fly with clubs several times a year, you carry a cart or stand bag, and you want one bag to hold clubs plus a week of clothes. The padding upgrade makes it a complete soft case now, and the shell durability is the part everyone agrees on.
  • Buy the prior generation if you fly once or twice a year and you're fine adding your own padding, meaning headcovers on, a towel over the heads, and the included Stiff Arm. It's lighter, it's usually cheaper, and it's the version that's actually listed on Amazon right now.
  • Skip both if you're a once-a-year traveler on a budget. A padded cover from CaddyDaddy or similar with a multi-year replacement guarantee does the job for a fraction of the cost, and if it fails, they replace it.
  • Skip both if you fly a lot and your shoulders hurt. Get the ClubGlider.

Where I land on it

The Pro Traveler is the safe, boring, correct answer for anyone who flies with clubs regularly, and the 2025 padding upgrade removed the one real criticism the old bag deserved. What I'd resist is the tour-player halo. Pros travel forty weeks a year with someone else carrying the bag. You travel three weekends a year and carry it yourself. That's a different problem, and for a fair number of golfers the cheaper prior generation or the ClubGlider solves it better. Weigh the packed bag, check the listing for the word "velour," and buy the version that matches how often you actually fly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the Club Glove Pro Traveler come with a Stiff Arm?

Yes. Both the prior-generation (2024) and current (2025) versions include Club Glove's aluminum Stiff Arm support rod to protect club shafts from vertical crushing.

Is the Pro Traveler a hard case or a soft case?

It is a heavy-duty soft-sided travel bag built over a rigid, high-impact wraparound plastic wheelbase.

Will a staff bag fit in the Pro Traveler?

No. The Pro Traveler is designed for stand and cart bags. For full-size staff tour bags, Club Glove manufactures the larger Tour Traveler (formerly Last Bag XL).

Why do some listings say "Titleist by Club Glove"?

Acushnet, the parent company of Titleist and FootJoy, acquired the Club Glove brand from West Coast Trends in February 2023, and co-branded several premium retail models.
