Europe Reclaims the Solheim Cup, and the Statistic That Called It All Week

By Golf Raw Editorial Staff Published 13 Sep 2026 Updated 13 Sep 2026, 17:00 CEST 4 min read

Anna Nordqvist's Europe has won the 2026 Solheim Cup at Bernardus Golf in the Netherlands, passing the 14.5 points needed to take the trophy back from the United States. Lottie Woad's 2&1 win over Auston Kim carried them to 14 and left Carlota Ciganda guaranteed at least a half, which mathematically settled it. Ciganda then beat Jennifer Kupcho 3&2 to make it official, and Europe led 15-12 with the last match still on the course.

The teams started Sunday level at 8-8. There was a number sitting in the record book all week that said this was coming anyway.

Golf Raw was not at Bernardus. Scores and sequence come from live coverage by Golf Channel, Golfmagic, Golf Monthly and Golfweek, plus Sky Sports and Associated Press reporting, all published 12 or 13 September 2026.

How 8-8 became a rout

It took about an hour.

Charley Hull won the first hole of the opening match against Megan Khang and closed her out 3&2. Golf Channel reports that put Europe 9-8 up with leads in seven of the other matches. Golfweek's live blog described a blue wave with every match on the course, in heavy rain and cold.

From there Europe kept converting. Linn Grant beat Angel Yin 4&3. Nanna Koerstz Madsen saw off Allisen Corpuz 2&1. The two rookies delivered, Nastasia Nadaud beating Andrea Lee 3&2 for her first Solheim singles point and Julia López Ramírez winning 3&2 in her first singles match at the event.

Eleven of the twelve matches were settled at the time of writing.

The number that called it

Here's the loop from the opening. Golf Channel and Golfweek both note the score had been tied entering the final session only six times in Solheim Cup history. In those six, the home team's record was 5-0-1.

Now it's 6-0-1.

The American problem underneath that is older and simpler. Golfweek's coverage points out the United States had not won a Sunday singles session since 2015, which is also the last year they won on European soil. Those two facts are the same fact wearing different clothes, and no captain has solved it in eleven years.

Hull and Woad, from nothing to everything

Sky Sports reported on Saturday that Hull and Woad had become the first pair in Solheim Cup history to play all four sessions together. They came out of those four matches with half a point.

On paper they were Europe's strongest pairing. For two days they were the story going the wrong way.

Then Hull led the team out and won. Woad, out seventh, was 4 up before Auston Kim won three holes in a row to cut it to one, per Golfmagic's live blog, and still closed it 2&1 for the point that took Europe to 14.

Woad's reaction, via Golf Channel: “I didn't know it was to win [the Solheim Cup].”

That's the whole tournament in one sentence. The player who delivered the decisive point had no idea she was delivering it.

The American who broke a record and lost anyway

Alison Lee was 4 down to Esther Henseleit. She won five holes on the back nine and took the match 1 up, which Golf Channel and Golfweek both record as the largest singles comeback in Solheim Cup history.

It was worth one point in a session Europe was already running away with.

Worth saying plainly, because the easy version of a result like this is that the visiting team folded. Nelly Korda beat Mimi Rhodes 4&2. Lauren Coughlin handed Leona Maguire her first singles defeat at the event, 2&1. Lindy Duncan beat Maja Stark 3&1, and Stark had been Europe's best player all week, unbeaten in three sessions alongside Grant. Individual Americans played well. The session still went one way.

A sendoff for Nordqvist, and not a penny for anyone

Golf Channel notes Nordqvist announced her professional retirement two weeks ago. She leaves with the trophy she was captaining for.

There's a symmetry on the other side too. Angela Stanford earned the winning point herself the last time the United States won on European soil, in Germany in 2015, per CBS Sports. She came back as captain to end that drought and watched it stretch to eleven years.

One thing neither captain gets, and neither does any of the 24 players: money. There is no purse at the Solheim Cup, no stipend and no payment per point, which puts it in an interesting position next to a Ryder Cup that started paying its American players $500,000 each in 2025.

The Raw Verdict

Europe won this on the first tee on Sunday morning, in the rain, when a player with half a point from four matches went out and won the opening hole. Everything after that was a scoreboard filling in.

The number to keep is the American one. No Sunday singles session won since 2015, no away win since 2015. Until the United States solves the last day, the venue decides the Solheim Cup before anyone tees off, and that's a problem for the event more than it is for either team.
