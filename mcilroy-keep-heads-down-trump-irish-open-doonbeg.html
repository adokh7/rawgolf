Rory McIlroy on Trump's Doonbeg Weekend: 'We'll Keep Our Heads Down and Play'

By Golf Raw Editorial Staff Published 08 Sep 2026 Updated 08 Sep 2026, 23:00 IST 4 min read

Rory McIlroy said on Tuesday that the players at the 2026 Amgen Irish Open will “keep our heads down and play” when U.S. President Donald Trump attends the weekend rounds at Trump International Golf Links Doonbeg, the County Clare resort Trump owns, per the Press Association's account of his BBC Sport Northern Ireland interview. The defending champion said the week would feel different, that the visit brings logistical challenges, and that weekend crowds would be limited. Trump is expected at Doonbeg on Saturday and Sunday, September 12 and 13, according to the Press Association and Irish Golfer Magazine.

Nine years ago the same player was defending a round of golf with the same man to a hostile press. How he got from there to “heads down” is the second section.

Golf Raw did not attend Doonbeg or interview McIlroy. Quotes come from his BBC Sport Northern Ireland interview as reported by the Press Association via Yahoo Sports and by Irish Golfer Magazine. Access rules come from Clare County Council and Garda advice as reported by the Clare Echo, Clare FM and The Irish Times. Historical dates are flagged where older than two years.

What he said, and what he didn't

Three sentences, none of them a position.

The world No. 2 told BBC Sport NI, per the Press Association, that the week would be different with Trump there, that the players are at Doonbeg to play a tournament on a good golf course, and that they'd keep their heads down and play. On the weekend he predicted that “crowds will be limited, obviously there's a lot that goes on.”

Irish Golfer Magazine's account carries the line every headline took: logistically the visit proves some challenges, but if Trump wants to come and watch the Irish Open, “who are we to say 'no?'”

He wasn't asked to endorse or oppose anything, and he didn't. The headlines treating it as either a subtle protest or a surrender have read a mood into a schedule note.

McIlroy is defending the title he won at The K Club in 2025, per BBC coverage. Irish Golfer Magazine notes this is the first major professional tournament Doonbeg has hosted.

From tour crusader to head-down pro

Here's the loop from the top. McIlroy has been asked to be golf's spokesman on this exact subject before, and he stopped volunteering for the job.

In February 2017, per The Guardian's reporting at the time, he played a round with Trump in Florida and spent the following days defending it against public backlash. Flag that year. In November 2023, per the PGA Tour's own announcement, he resigned from the Tour's Policy Board, saying the role was consuming too much of his energy. Flag that too.

Set those next to Tuesday's answer. The 2017 version explained himself. The 2023 version walked out of the boardroom. The 2026 version said the golf course is good and he'd be playing it.

Golf Raw's read is that this isn't detachment for its own sake. It's a player who spent years as an unpaid front man for tour arguments and has decided the only thing he owes anyone at Doonbeg is a scorecard. We'd have said the same in his shoes, and we'd say it's overdue.

Who actually absorbs the circus

Not the players. The person with a €40 ticket and a car.

Clare County Council has confirmed the N67 closes from Doonbeg village to Clonadrum, along with the R484 from Creegh to Behan's Cross, from 6am Friday, September 11 until 11.59pm Monday, September 14, at Garda request for security reasons, per the Clare Echo. Gardaí say access is by Park and Ride or shuttle only, per Clare FM, with a free Park and Ride running from Wednesday at two registered sites and extra shuttles from Kilkee, Kilrush and Miltown Malbay.

Everyone attending faces a security search, with bags limited to roughly under-seat size, per Clare FM. The Irish Times, citing a security source, called it one of the most complex operations ever staged in the State.

Tickets, per the DP World Tour's page as of September 8: general admission from €40, Ticket+ at €155, hospitality on the 18th from €549 before VAT.

What's still open

Where Trump stands, when he arrives, and whether “limited” has a number attached.

None of that was available from any source Golf Raw could open. Neither was a tee sheet from the DP World Tour itself, so check the tour's site for McIlroy's Thursday time rather than any secondhand listing.

FAQ

Will spectators still get in at the weekend?

Nobody official has said otherwise. Access for all ticket holders is by Park and Ride and shuttle, per Gardaí. McIlroy expects crowds to be limited on the days Trump attends, but the DP World Tour has published no reduced capacity. Expect restricted movement on course near the presidential party rather than refused entry.

What's McIlroy's history with Trump?

He played a round with Trump in Florida in February 2017, per The Guardian's reporting at the time, and defended the decision publicly amid criticism. Flag the year. Tuesday's comments at Doonbeg made no reference to it.

How early should I arrive?

Gardaí advise arriving early and carrying as little as possible, per Clare FM. With mandatory shuttles, bag searches and road closures from Friday, Golf Raw would add two hours to any normal tournament-day plan.

The Raw Verdict

McIlroy's answer is the right one and it took him nine years to arrive at it. A player at a golf tournament owes you golf, and the fact that he was ever expected to owe anyone a political statement says more about golf media than about him.

The people who can't keep their heads down are the ones on the bus from a registered car park, in a search line, holding a ticket they bought before any of this was announced. Watch the golf. It'll be good. Just leave two hours earlier than feels sane and don't expect the man in the blazer to thank you for it.
