Bryson DeChambeau and LIV Golf in 2027: What's Actually Been Decided

He hasn't said a word. A Spanish outlet says he's all in on LIV 2.0, and three purse figures are circulating. What's verified, and what's not.

By GOLFRAW Editorial Published 03 Sep 2026 Updated 03 Sep 2026, 17:00 CEST 10 min read

Key takeaways

  • There is no public decision. The headline traces to a Ten Golf post relayed by HITC, with no quote from DeChambeau, his team or LIV.
  • His LIV contract expired after Indianapolis. Yahoo Sports reports the deal ended after the Crushers' season finale in late August.
  • The 2026 season gives him leverage. He won twice on LIV and finished second in the individual race, even as all three early-season major starts became missed cuts.
  • The money reports do not agree. Figures range from $7 million to $9 million per event, around $10 million, or roughly 40% of current purses.
  • The strongest signal is structural. His agency has a BC Partners connection, he has helped shape LIV 2.0 talks and the PGA Tour return window already closed.

He hasn't announced anything. The "decision" in every headline today traces to a single post on X from a Spanish golf outlet, relayed by a British aggregator, relayed by an American one.

Bryson DeChambeau is reported to be "all in" on LIV 2.0 for 2027, according to Ten Golf as relayed by HITC. His LIV contract expired after the season finale in Indianapolis in late August. He has made no public announcement. Cameron Smith and Dustin Johnson are reported to be similarly committed, while Jon Rahm is reported to be out.

What that report can't tell you is why DeChambeau staying would be the least surprising outcome available, and it has to do with who owns a piece of his agency. That's the fourth section.

Built on: HITC's report via Yardbarker citing Ten Golf, Yahoo Sports' coverage of DeChambeau's expired contract, Sports Illustrated's reporting from 2025 and 2026, Heavy's account of ESPN's reporting on LIV player meetings, and CBS Sports' coverage of the PGA Tour's Returning Member Program.

What was reported, and by whom

A social post, then two aggregators. That's the chain.

HITC, syndicated via Yardbarker on September 3, cites a post from Ten Golf on X stating DeChambeau is all in on LIV 2.0, with Smith and Johnson also on board. The same report claims Bubba Watson and David Puig are definitely out, that Rahm is out "one way or another", and that European LIV players remain in a standoff with the DP World Tour over fines and suspensions for conflicting events.

No quote from DeChambeau appears anywhere in it.

I couldn't open the Ten Golf post itself. That leaves a report of a report, with no named source and no on-record confirmation from the player, his team or the league. Treat it as plausible and unconfirmed, because that's exactly what it is.

The contract that already ran out

Sunday night in Indianapolis. That's when it ended.

Yahoo Sports reports DeChambeau's LIV contract expired after the Crushers' season finale, and that he posted a cryptic message afterward reading "It was a good run". He'd told Sports Illustrated at Oakmont in June 2025 that his deal ended after 2026 and he expected to negotiate a new one later that year. Flag that as 2025.

SI's reporting has him signing with LIV in 2022 for more than $100 million.

So as of this week he's a free agent in the strictest sense, with a league that Reuters says may file for bankruptcy protection as soon as the week of September 7, and settlement offers going to players owed money beyond 2026 at a few cents on the dollar.

The difference for DeChambeau is that his deal is done. There's no back-loaded balance to settle. He's negotiating a new contract with a league that's restructuring, which is a stronger position than the one Rahm is in.

The season he's carrying into the talks

Two wins on LIV and second in the season-long race. Also three straight missed cuts at majors.

Yahoo Sports summarises his 2026: two LIV victories, runner-up in the individual standings, missed cuts at the Masters, PGA Championship and U.S. Open, then a T14 at the Open Championship at Royal Birkdale after a penalty in the second round. HITC counts five LIV wins since he joined in June 2022.

The 2024 U.S. Open at Pinehurst remains his second major. Sports Illustrated notes he finished top 10 in three of four majors in 2025 before this year's slide.

Then the number that matters more than any of those. SI reports his YouTube channel has around 2.6 million followers and roughly 22 million views a month.

That audience is why LIV wants him back and why the PGA Tour would too. It's also why he can afford to play wherever suits the channel.

Why staying makes sense, and the connection nobody's flagging

BC Partners holds a stake in his agency. That's the detail that reframes the whole story.

Heavy's report, citing ESPN's Paolo Uggetti, notes that BC Partners, whose credit division is negotiating to finance LIV 2.0, has a stake in DeChambeau's agency GSE. The same reporting says DeChambeau led a players-only meeting at Trump National Bedminster in August and has met investors alongside LIV CEO Scott O'Neil.

He's been in the room building the thing he's now reported to be joining.

O'Neil, per Reuters via Heavy, said he talks to DeChambeau regularly and has a lot of time for him. Flashscore's earlier reporting describes a 25% equity stake in his Crushers team, with an ambition to spread Crushers-branded courses and academies worldwide. Flag that as undated.

A man with team equity, an agency tied to the incoming lender, and a YouTube business that runs on a light schedule was never the likeliest defector. The surprising outcome would have been the opposite.

What LIV 2.0 would pay, and three different answers

Seven to nine million per event, per Ten Golf via HITC. Around ten million, per Front Office Sports. About 40% of current purses, per Golf.com.

Three sources, three figures, no official number. HITC's relay of Ten Golf adds structure: 10 teams of four players, another 50 added through an open format with some automatic qualifiers, 90-man fields, and a cut of the bottom 30 at each event.

That's a very different league from the one DeChambeau joined. Golfmagic reported in February that he was already unhappy with LIV's move to 72 holes, telling Today's Golfer he didn't sign up for that format.

The cut is new too. LIV was built on no-cut guarantees. A 90-man field with 30 going home is a real tournament, for better and worse.

The door he closed in February

He could have gone back. He chose not to, seven months ago.

CBS Sports reported that the PGA Tour's Returning Member Program was created for four players, DeChambeau among them, with a window that opened in late January and closed on February 2. Only Brooks Koepka used it. DeChambeau told LIV's media week he was contracted through 2026 and excited about the season.

Golf Channel carried him saying much the same in January, describing himself as contractually committed.

The Tour's CEO Brian Rolapp said in late August there are no current plans to reopen that pathway. The Athletic reported in April that DeChambeau's representatives were exploring options at the Masters in case he left, per SI. He didn't.

So the honest reading is that every verifiable signal since January points the same way, and today's report just says it louder without adding a quote.

Five things being said about DeChambeau's future that don't hold up

Fact-check

  • "DeChambeau announced he's staying." He hasn't. The claim is a Ten Golf post relayed by HITC. No statement, no quote, no confirmation from LIV.
  • "His contract still has time to run." It expired after the Indianapolis finale, per Yahoo Sports. He told SI in 2025 it ended after 2026.
  • "He can just go back to the PGA Tour." The Returning Member Program closed on February 2 and the Tour's CEO says there are no current plans to reopen it.
  • "LIV 2.0 purses are settled." Three published figures exist, from $7 million to roughly $10 million, and none is official.
  • "He's a declining player." Two LIV wins and second in the 2026 standings. The major record this year is poor, but the reporting on his form has been running both directions.

Verification block

Confirmed or directly reported: the Ten Golf/HITC claim; DeChambeau's contract expiry after Indianapolis; his 2026 LIV wins and second-place standings finish; the BC Partners/GSE connection; O'Neil's regular contact; reported purse ranges; the February Returning Member Program deadline; and the vendor, bankruptcy and OWGR context cited below.

Not confirmed: DeChambeau's 2027 decision, any final contract, investor terms, exact purse, team-equity treatment or a PGA Tour return.

The Raw Verdict

Nothing has been decided that you can quote, and everything that can be verified points to him staying. A player with team equity, an agency linked to the lender, and a YouTube channel that needs him on camera more than it needs him on a leaderboard was always the last man to leave.

Wait for a sentence with his name in front of it before you repeat today's headline. If it comes, it'll confirm what the February decision already told you. If it doesn't, the league he's building may not be there to join.

Frequently Asked Questions

Has Bryson DeChambeau decided to stay on LIV Golf for 2027?

Not officially. HITC, citing a Ten Golf post on X, reports he's "all in" on LIV 2.0 alongside Cameron Smith and Dustin Johnson. DeChambeau has made no public announcement, and neither LIV nor his representatives have confirmed the report.

Is DeChambeau still under contract with LIV?

No. Yahoo Sports reports his contract expired after the season finale in Indianapolis in late August 2026. He'd told Sports Illustrated in June 2025 that his deal ended after 2026 and he expected to negotiate a new one.

Could DeChambeau return to the PGA Tour?

The pathway offered to him closed on February 2, 2026, when the Returning Member Program window shut with only Brooks Koepka using it. PGA Tour CEO Brian Rolapp said in late August there are no current plans to reopen it. DeChambeau said in January he was committed to LIV.

How did DeChambeau play in 2026?

Two wins on LIV and second in the individual standings, per Yahoo Sports, but missed cuts at the Masters, PGA Championship and U.S. Open before a T14 at Royal Birkdale. His YouTube channel draws around 2.6 million followers and roughly 22 million monthly views, per Sports Illustrated.

What would LIV 2.0 look like?

Reports vary. HITC's relay of Ten Golf describes 10 four-player teams plus 50 open-format players in 90-man fields with a cut of the bottom 30, and purses of $7 million to $9 million. Front Office Sports put purses near $10 million. Golf.com's understanding was about 40% of current levels.
