8 Big Names Missing the 2026 Solheim Cup (And the Real Reason Each One Is Out)

From 0.9-point Heartbreaks to pregnancy and broken wrists, here are 8 major stars missing the 2026 Solheim Cup at Bernardus Golf and why.

BY GOLFRAW EDITORIAL PUBLISHED TUE 04 AUG 2026

The 2026 Solheim Cup is nearly here, and while the focus will be on the 24 players teeing it up at Bernardus Golf in the Netherlands from September 11–13, some of the biggest names in women's golf won't be there.

Europe captain Anna Nordqvist confirmed her 12-player squad on August 3, naming four captain's picks to join the eight automatic qualifiers. The American side, led by Angela Stanford, won't be finalized until August 24 — but several high-profile players have already been ruled out or failed to make the cut.

Here's a closer look at eight notable names who won't be competing in this year's Solheim Cup, and the real reasons behind each absence.

1. Chiara Tamburlini — Missed Automatic Qualification by 0.9 Points

Switzerland's Chiara Tamburlini is arguably the player who will feel most aggrieved by her omission. She arrived at the AIG Women's Open holding a slender 1.1-point advantage over Germany's Helen Briem in the race for the second automatic qualifying spot from the LET Solheim Cup points list. But after missing the cut at Royal Lytham & St Annes, her fate was out of her hands.

Esther Henseleit then holed a dramatic 40-foot putt on the 72nd hole to force a playoff, securing the runner-up spot and overtaking Tamburlini by just 0.9 points in the standings. With no captain's pick forthcoming, Tamburlini narrowly misses out on what would have been her Solheim Cup debut.

2. Madelene Sagstrom — Expecting First Child in September

Madelene Sagstrom has been a mainstay of the European team, featuring in 2017, 2021, 2023 and 2024. But the Swede won't be in the Netherlands this year — she's expecting her first child in September, the same month as the Solheim Cup.

Sagstrom continued playing while pregnant, competing at the US Women's Open and the Dow Championship in June, but with the baby due around the same time as the event, she was never going to be available for selection.

3. Georgia Hall — Gave Birth in February

Like Sagstrom, Georgia Hall's absence is due to pregnancy. The English major winner announced the birth of her first child in February and returned to competitive golf in May, but her form has yet to fully return.

Hall missed the cut at both the Women's Scottish Open and the AIG Women's Open, and with five Solheim Cup appearances (2017, 2019, 2021, 2023, 2024) and a 10-9-2 record, her experience will be missed by the European side.

4. Emily Kristine Pedersen — Broken Wrist Derailed Season

Denmark's Emily Kristine Pedersen has been a regular for Europe, playing in four Solheim Cups with a 7-9-1 record. But a broken wrist diagnosed at the beginning of February has severely disrupted her 2026 season.

Since returning to action, Pedersen has made just one cut in six starts, with her only weekend appearance coming at the Kroger Queen City Championship in May. The injury and subsequent loss of form meant she was never in contention for a place on this year's team.

5. Dewi Weber — Not Eligible for European Selection

Dewi Weber's omission is perhaps the most unusual on this list. The Dutch player has produced some excellent results in 2026, including a T3 finish at the KPMG Women's PGA Championship, and is ranked higher than several players who did make the European team.

However, Weber is not a member of the Ladies European Tour, which makes her ineligible for European selection. Despite the Solheim Cup being held in her home country, she'll have to wait for a future opportunity to make her debut.

6. Albane Valenzuela — Poor Form in 2026

Switzerland's Albane Valenzuela made her Solheim Cup debut in 2024, posting a 0-1-1 record in Virginia. But her form has fallen off a cliff in 2026.

The 28-year-old has made just three cuts in nine starts on the LPGA Tour this season, with her best result a T33 at the Blue Bay LPGA. That wasn't enough to earn her a second consecutive appearance.

7. Ingrid Lindblad — Form Slump After Promising Start

Ingrid Lindblad arrived on the LPGA Tour with huge expectations after a stellar amateur career and a victory in her debut professional season at the 2025 JM Eagle LA Championship.

But the Swede has struggled with her driving in 2026, making just eight cuts in 13 starts. While she remains one of the brightest young talents in the game, her inconsistent form meant she was overlooked for a captain's pick. At 25, she has time on her side.

8. Helen Briem — Missed Cut at the Wrong Time

Germany's Helen Briem was another player who saw her automatic qualification hopes dashed at the AIG Women's Open.

Briem had a chance to qualify via the LET points list, but a missed cut ended her chances. The 22-year-old won on the Ladies European Tour in May but has made just five cuts in 11 starts on the LPGA Tour. She only secured her LPGA Tour card at the end of 2025, so this was always going to be a long shot.

The Bigger Picture: What These Absences Mean for Europe

The absence of Sagstrom, Hall and Pedersen removes a significant chunk of Solheim Cup experience from the European side. Between them, they have 13 appearances and a combined 25-25-5 record.

In their place, Nordqvist has opted for youth, with four rookies in her 12-player squad. Lottie Woad qualified automatically, while Julia Lopez Ramirez, Nastasia Nadaud and Mimi Rhodes were all named as captain's picks.

Only time will tell if the gamble pays off. But with the USA defending the trophy and the match being played on home soil, Europe will need all the experience they can get — even if it's from unexpected sources.

What's Next?

The American team will be finalized on August 24, following the conclusion of the CPKC Women's Open. Angela Stanford has three captain's picks to make, and with players like Lexi Thompson, Megan Khang and Rose Zhang all in the mix, there are some tough decisions ahead.

The 2026 Solheim Cup takes place at Bernardus Golf in the Netherlands from September 11–13. It will be the first time the event has been held in the country.
