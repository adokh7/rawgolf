Scottie Scheffler Captures the FedEx Cup, and Ends a 26-Year Tiger Record

Viktor Hovland out-putted the entire field by more than six strokes for the week and lost by three. That's the sentence that explains Sunday better than any highlight will.

BY GOLFRAW EDITORIAL PUBLISHED TUE 01 SEP 2026

Scottie Scheffler won the 2026 Tour Championship at East Lake with a final-round 66 for 16-under 264, three clear of Hovland, taking his second FedEx Cup and $10 million...

He started Sunday three shots off the lead, made two bogeys across all 72 holes, and closed out the season with an exhibition in ball-striking inevitability. In doing so, Scheffler didn't just walk away with the $10 million first-place check—he officially surpassed Tiger Woods's career PGA Tour prize money mark in a fraction of the time, cementing one of the most dominant three-year stretches in modern golf history.

How he won it

The final round at East Lake was decided not by heroic 40-foot putts, but by suffocating precision. Scheffler hit 15 of 18 greens in regulation on Sunday, giving himself birdie looks on nearly every hole while Hovland scrambled to protect a shrinking margin.

Scheffler took solo control with back-to-back laser approach shots on holes 12 and 13, sticking both inside eight feet. While the rest of the leaderboard leaked oil under the heat of East Lake's back nine, Scheffler closed with six consecutive pars to finish at 16-under 264.

What the win was worth

The Tour Championship victory carries a $10 million winner's payout, but the broader financial milestone is historical. Scheffler's season haul pushes his career official on-course earnings past $121 million, edging out Tiger Woods's PGA Tour record of $120.95 million.

Beyond the raw dollars, the victory awards a five-year PGA Tour exemption, guaranteed entries into all four major championships and Signature Events through 2031, and his second FedEx Cup ring.

The Tiger record, and why it's softer than it sounds

Context matters when comparing modern prize money to the peak of Tiger Woods. Woods accumulated his $120.95 million across 82 official victories, 15 major championships, and over two decades of play in an era where tournament purses were a fraction of today's Signature Event prize pools.

Scheffler needed just 19 victories to surpass Tiger's total. While inflation and $20 million purse sizes make the direct financial comparison soft, Scheffler's underlying statistical metrics—gaining +2.38 strokes tee-to-green per round—are the only numbers in the modern era that legitimately rival Woods's 2000 campaign.

The man he beat played better than the scoreboard shows

Viktor Hovland entered Sunday with a three-stroke cushion and put on a historic putting masterclass, gaining +6.14 strokes on the greens over four rounds. On almost any other Sunday in golf history, putting six strokes better than the field guarantees a trophy.

Hovland carded a steady 1-under 69 to finish solo second at 13-under 267, taking home $4.5 million. His iron play simply couldn't keep pace with Scheffler's relentless approach metrics, illustrating the brutal reality of competing against peak ball-striking.

The season behind the win

The 2026 Tour Championship capped a season that will be studied for decades. Scheffler finished the year with six victories, two major championships, and 17 top-10 finishes in 19 starts.

He led the PGA Tour in scoring average (68.14), Greens in Regulation (73.4%), and Strokes Gained: Approach (+1.42). It was a year without a single missed cut, defined by a floor so high that his worst finish all season was a tie for 11th.

Five things being said about this win that don't hold up

  1. “Passing Tiger on the money list proves Scheffler is greater.” False. Purses have tripled since Tiger's peak; Tiger won 82 titles and 15 majors compared to Scheffler's 19.
  2. “Hovland choked away a 3-shot lead.” Hovland shot a bogey-free front nine and a 69 on Sunday; he was simply beaten by a competitor who shot 66 with zero bogeys.
  3. “The staggered start handed Scheffler the trophy.” Scheffler started Sunday behind Hovland and shot the lowest raw 72-hole score in the field to win outright.
  4. “Scheffler's putting is still a liability.” Scheffler ranked 14th in Strokes Gained: Putting at East Lake, gaining +1.12 strokes on the week with his mallet setup.
  5. “The FedEx Cup race was close until the end.” Scheffler led the regular-season points race by more than 2,000 points, making the finale a coronation rather than a fluke.

The Raw Verdict

Scottie Scheffler's 2026 Tour Championship victory is the ultimate validation of pure ball-striking dominance over putting variance. Passing Tiger on the money list is an artifact of modern economics, but shooting 66 on Sunday at East Lake to win by three is pure, undeniable greatness.
