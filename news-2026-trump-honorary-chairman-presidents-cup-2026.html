Trump Named Honorary Chairman of the Presidents Cup Again

He's the seventh U.S. president in the role and the second to do it twice. What the job actually involves, and the dates two outlets can't agree on.

By GOLFRAW Editorial Published 30 Aug 2026 Updated 30 Aug 2026, 18:00 CEST 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Donald Trump has been named honorary chairman of the 2026 Presidents Cup, a role reported as his second turn in the position and the seventh time a sitting U.S. president has held it.
  • The title is ceremonial. The duties are attendance and the trophy presentation — not team selection, captaincy or any sporting decision.
  • Two date ranges are circulating and both are right: 22–27 September counts the practice days, 24–27 September is the competition.
  • The event is at Medinah Country Club, Course No. 3, near Chicago.
  • The Presidents Cup is 30 matches, first to 15.5 points, and has no purse — it is a charity event, not a paid tour stop.
  • The captains are reported as Brandt Snedeker for the United States and Geoff Ogilvy for the Internationals.

The title sounds bigger than the job. Donald Trump has been named honorary chairman of the 2026 Presidents Cup, and the announcement has been covered as though it hands him a say over an American team. It does not. The role is ceremonial, it has been held by six presidents before him, and the loudest disagreement in the coverage is not about power at all — it is about which dates are correct.

Here is what the position actually is, what it is not, and why two reputable listings give two different date ranges for the same event.

The announcement

The PGA Tour named Trump honorary chairman of the 2026 Presidents Cup, and by the reporting around it he accepted the role in a post on Truth Social. That is the sum of the news: an offer and an acceptance of a ceremonial position at a team event that is still weeks away.

It is worth separating the noise from the fact early, because a headline that reads “Trump takes charge of the Presidents Cup” is describing something that is not happening. Nobody is putting a president in charge of a golf team.

What honorary chairman actually means

It is ceremonial. The duties are attendance and the trophy presentation. It carries no authority over the team, the captains, the format or any competitive decision.

The honorary chairman does not pick players. He does not sit in the team room, set a pairing, or influence who plays foursomes on Friday. The captains do all of that, and the players qualify or are picked on golf. The chairman turns up and, if the host side wins, is part of handing over the trophy.

This matters because the role has a long, bipartisan history of being offered to whoever occupies the White House when the event is on American soil. According to the reporting around this announcement, Trump is the seventh U.S. president to serve as honorary chairman and the second to do it more than once. It is a tradition, not an appointment, and it says nothing about the golf.

The date confusion, explained

Both date ranges are correct. They count different things.

You will see the 2026 Presidents Cup listed as 22–27 September in some places and 24–27 September in others, and it is easy to read that as one of them being wrong. Neither is.

The wider window — 22 to 27 September — is the event as a whole, practice days included. Teams arrive, play practice rounds, do the opening ceremony, and all of that is part of the official footprint. The narrower window — 24 to 27 September — is the competition: the four days on which matches are actually played and points are scored. An outlet counting the event counts six days. An outlet counting the golf counts four. Both are right about what they are describing.

How the Presidents Cup works

If the honorary chairman is the ceremony, this is the sport underneath it.

The Presidents Cup is a team match play event between the United States and an International team — everyone outside Europe, which is the Ryder Cup's territory. Over the week the teams play 30 matches: sessions of foursomes (alternate shot) and four-ball (better ball) through the first three days, then twelve singles on the final day. Each match is worth a single point, and the first team to 15.5 of the 30 available points wins. At 15.5 the other side can no longer reach a tie.

The structural oddity, and the thing that separates it from a normal week on tour, is the money: there is none. The Presidents Cup has no purse. It is a charity event, the players are not paid to play, and the funds raised go to causes chosen by the participants. Nobody at Medinah is competing for a cheque.

The captains, and the course

The reported captains are Brandt Snedeker for the United States and Geoff Ogilvy for the Internationals — the two people who will make every decision the honorary chairman will not.

There is a neat subplot in the venue. Ogilvy is a partner in the design firm OCM Golf, which by the reporting was involved in the redesign of Medinah's Course No. 3, the layout hosting the event. An International captain with a professional hand in the golf course is an unusual wrinkle, and a far more interesting one than the ceremonial headline, though how much a redesign helps a visiting team on a course they see for one week is an open question.

The PGA Tour and Trump: a turbulent history

The reason this appointment draws attention is that the relationship behind it has not been smooth, and the record is a matter of public event history rather than interpretation.

In 2016 the Tour moved a World Golf Championship event away from Trump's Doral resort in Florida, relocating it to Mexico — a decision framed at the time around sponsorship and market considerations, and one Trump publicly criticised. In 2021 the PGA of America, a separate body from the PGA Tour, voted to move the following year's PGA Championship away from Trump's Bedminster course in New Jersey. Those are two different organisations and two different events, which is itself a common source of confusion.

Against that backdrop, an honorary chairmanship in 2026 — alongside reporting of a tour event returning to a Trump-associated arrangement under the Cadillac name — reads as a thaw. But a ceremonial title is a low-stakes place to thaw. It commits the Tour to nothing competitive and hands the president nothing operational.

Four things the coverage keeps getting wrong

What to ignore

  • Myth 1 — this is unprecedented. Fact: six U.S. presidents held the honorary chairman role before him, and he has reportedly held it before himself. It is a standing tradition for events on home soil.
  • Myth 2 — the PGA banned Trump, so this is a reversal. Fact: the 2021 decision was by the PGA of America, a different organisation from the PGA Tour that runs the Presidents Cup. Conflating the two produces a reversal that did not happen between the same parties.
  • Myth 3 — the chairman has a say over the team. Fact: none. Selection and captaincy are entirely separate from the ceremonial role.
  • Myth 4 — it is basically the Ryder Cup. Fact: different event, different opponent. The Presidents Cup is USA versus an International team that explicitly excludes Europe, and it has its own history and its own lopsided record.

Verification block

Established and stable: the Presidents Cup format — 30 matches of foursomes, four-ball and singles, first team to 15.5 points, no purse, charity proceeds; and the honorary chairman role being ceremonial, without authority over team or competition.

Reported around this announcement and used here as supplied: Trump as the seventh U.S. president and second two-time honorary chairman; his acceptance via Truth Social; the Medinah Course No. 3 venue; the two circulating date ranges (22–27 and 24–27 September); the captains Brandt Snedeker and Geoff Ogilvy; and Ogilvy's firm OCM Golf being involved in the course redesign.

Public record, presented as event history: the 2016 relocation of a WGC event from Doral to Mexico, and the 2021 PGA of America decision to move the PGA Championship from Bedminster.

Not verified: the exact terms of any 2026 Cadillac-branded arrangement, and the precise nature of OCM Golf's role in the Medinah redesign.

The Raw Verdict

Strip the name off it and this is a routine piece of golf housekeeping: a host nation offers its head of state a ceremonial title for a home event, as it has done for decades, and the head of state says yes.

The story the coverage wants is a president seizing control of a golf team. The story that is actually true is a president agreeing to show up and, if the Americans win at Medinah, help lift a trophy that comes with no prize money attached. Everything that decides the golf — the picks, the pairings, the putts — belongs to Snedeker, Ogilvy and twenty-four players, none of whom answer to an honorary chairman.

The genuinely interesting subplot is an International captain who had a hand in redesigning the host course. That is worth watching. The title is not.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the honorary chairman of the Presidents Cup actually do?

It is a ceremonial role. The reported duties are attending the event and taking part in the trophy presentation. It carries no authority over team selection, captaincy, format or any sporting decision. The captains pick the teams and run the matches.

Is Trump the first U.S. president to hold the role?

No. According to the reporting around the announcement he is the seventh U.S. president to serve as honorary chairman of the Presidents Cup, and the second to hold it more than once. The role has been offered to sitting presidents across multiple editions of the event.

When is the 2026 Presidents Cup?

This is where two date ranges circulate. The event's own listing gives 22 to 27 September, which includes the practice days, while the competition itself runs 24 to 27 September. Both are correct; they are counting different things. The matches are the last four of those days.

Is there prize money at the Presidents Cup?

No. There is no purse. The Presidents Cup is a charity event and the players are not paid to compete. Money raised is distributed to charities chosen by the players and captains, which is one of the structural differences between it and a standard tour event.

How is the Presidents Cup scored?

Thirty matches across the week: sessions of foursomes and four-ball, then singles on the final day. Each match is worth one point, and the first team to 15.5 points wins. With 30 points in play, 15.5 is a majority the opponent can no longer catch.
