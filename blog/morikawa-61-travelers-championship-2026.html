Collin Morikawa Shot 61 at the Travelers and Still Lost by One

Collin Morikawa's 9-under 61 tied his career low and left him one shot out. Hovland beat Scheffler in a Monday playoff. What actually happened, verified.

BY GOLFRAW EDITORIAL PUBLISHED MON 29 JUN 2026 FINAL ROUND RECAP 4 MIN READ
Tournament Recap & Scoring Records: June 29, 2026 | Travelers Championship

Quick answer: Collin Morikawa closed the 2026 Travelers Championship with a 9-under 61 on Sunday, 28 June—posting nine birdies, zero bogeys, and tying his PGA Tour career low. Climbing from eight to nine shots back to post 20-under, it still wasn't enough. Scottie Scheffler and Viktor Hovland tied regulation at 21-under, with Hovland taking the title on a Monday playoff. Morikawa finished solo third, exactly one shot out.

Here is how the round unfolded and why the performance was far more significant than the final leaderboard suggests.

What Did Collin Morikawa Shoot at the Travelers?

A 9-under 61, matching the lowest single round of his PGA Tour career. With nine birdies, zero dropped shots, and the low round of the day at TPC River Highlands, Morikawa delivered a clinic in scoring.

He entered Sunday well behind the pace—trailing the top of the leaderboard by eight or nine shots depending on whether you measure against the field or Viktor Hovland's lead.

The charge started immediately:

  • Back-to-back birdies on holes 1 and 2.
  • An outward nine of 5-under 30.
  • Additional birdies at the 10th, 13th, and 16th to apply pressure on the clubhouse.

How Did He Finish the Round?

In driving rain, straight out of a fairway bunker.

On the par-4 18th, Morikawa found sand off the tee and faced a high-difficulty approach. He was caught between a pitching wedge and a 9-iron, opting against the wedge due to restricted swing mobility with the ball sitting below his feet—a stance he later noted was the most aggravating for his lower back.

Morikawa was competing in his third consecutive tournament week while actively managing a lingering back injury sustained at The Players Championship earlier in the season.

Opting for the 9-iron, he executed cleanly: "I flushed it." The approach stopped inside 10 feet (reported between 8 and 9 feet across official records), and he converted the birdie putt to post 20-under.

Was the Putter Really the Problem?

Public narrative often frames Morikawa as an elite ball-striker held back by an inconsistent flatstick. Sunday’s round completely inverted that script.

The putter carried the round:

  • 1st in the field in Strokes Gained: Putting.
  • ~135 total feet of putts made.
  • Key birdie conversions holed from 30, 31, 17, and 15 feet.
  • Gained over 6.5 strokes on the field on the greens alone.

What Happened After Regulation Play?

Weather delays, an extended wait, and a Monday morning finish.

  1. Weather Suspension: Severe storms suspended play at 5:57 p.m. ET with Scheffler holding a one-shot advantage.
  2. Resumption: Play resumed at 7:20 p.m. ET after 1.12 inches of rainfall, allowing all 72 regulation holes to conclude Sunday evening.
  3. The Playoff: Scheffler and Hovland tied at 21-under, returning at 9:00 a.m. ET on Monday, 29 June for a sudden-death playoff on the 18th hole. Hovland secured victory on the first extra hole, taking home the $3.6 million winner's share from the $20 million purse.

Was the 61 Wasted?

In season context, no.

This marked Morikawa’s sixth top-10 finish of 2026, backing up his earlier victory at the AT&T Pebble Beach Pro-Am. Competing three straight weeks and managing physical limitations, turning a mid-pack standing into a near-playoff appearance at an elite, no-cut signature event demonstrated peak form when it mattered most.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who won the 2026 Travelers Championship?

Viktor Hovland won the tournament, defeating Scottie Scheffler on the first hole of a sudden-death playoff on Monday, 29 June 2026, after both tied regulation at 21-under.

What did Collin Morikawa shoot in the final round?

He shot a 9-under 61 (nine birdies, zero bogeys), tying his career-low round on the PGA Tour and recording the lowest score of the final round.

Did Morikawa make the playoff?

No. Morikawa finished solo third at 20-under par, missing the Hovland-Scheffler playoff by a single stroke.

Why was the playoff played on Monday?

While regulation play finished Sunday evening following an 83-minute weather suspension, daylight and tournament scheduling moved the sudden-death playoff to Monday at 9:00 a.m. ET.

How did Morikawa birdie the 72nd hole?

He hit a 9-iron approach from a fairway bunker in heavy rain to inside 10 feet and made the putt to post 20-under.
