Nine Players Broke Par at Royal Lytham. Yealimi Noh Was One of Them.

BY GOLFRAW EDITORIAL PUBLISHED: SUN 02 AUG 2026
The Quick Verdict

Yealimi Noh takes a three-shot lead into Sunday at the AIG Women's Open. Haeran Ryu, chasing a third straight major, was four ahead of her at one point on Saturday and finished three behind after a 74. Only nine players in the whole field broke par. Noh took 26 putts. Every wire story is calling her unheralded, which is nonsense, and the actual story is more interesting than that anyway.

Somebody was four shots ahead. Somebody is now three shots behind. Same afternoon, same golf course, no weather event, no rules drama, nothing you could point at and call the turning point.

That's links golf and I love it for exactly this reason.

What actually happened on Saturday

Haeran Ryu started the day with the halfway lead and pushed it out to four. She's the world No. 3 and she's won the last two majors, the Women's PGA in June and the Evian in July, so a comfortable Saturday at Royal Lytham looked like the setup for something historic.

Then she made five bogeys in eight holes. Six for the round. She signed for a 74.

Yealimi Noh, meanwhile, opened with two bogeys in her first five and dropped six back. She birdied the par-3 ninth, then the tenth, then the eleventh, added one more at the sixteenth, and walked off with a 69.

Do the arithmetic and it's a seven-shot swing between two players inside one round. Not seven shots against the field, which happens. Seven shots head to head, in an afternoon, on a golf course where nine people out of one hundred and forty-four managed to break par.

Noh had 26 putts.

About that word "unheralded"

Every syndicated recap I read led with some version of it. I get why. She's world No. 69, she missed the cut in three of her last five starts, and she's missed fourteen major cuts since 2022. Those numbers are real and they're not flattering.

But she was one of the most decorated juniors in America. She won three tournaments in consecutive weeks in 2018. She Monday-qualified into a full LPGA event and finished second. She went third at Q-Series, made fifteen cuts in sixteen starts as a rookie, and finished 25th on the money list in her first season. She was a captain's pick for the 2021 Solheim Cup. She won the Founders Cup in February 2025, beating Jin Young Ko by four.

"Unheralded" is a word that means nobody was paying attention. Plenty of people were paying attention. She just took 111 starts to win, and golf has a short memory for anyone whose timeline doesn't cooperate.

If you want the accurate version, it's this: she's a good player who's been quiet lately and had a bad few months right before the best week of her life. Which describes a lot of golfers, at every level, and is a lot more relatable than "nobody saw this coming."

The precedent nobody's mentioning

She's done this exact thing before.

At the Founders Cup last year she held the 54-hole lead, lost it to Ko on the front nine Sunday, and took it straight back on the back nine with birdies at 13 and 14. Closed bogey-free for a 68 and won by four.

That's not a guarantee of anything. A Founders Cup Sunday and a major Sunday at Royal Lytham are different animals and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. But it's the one relevant data point about how she handles a Sunday with something on it, and it says she can lose control of a golf tournament and get it back without falling apart. Given what happened to the person who had the lead on Saturday, that seems worth knowing.

Why three shots isn't much here

Ten players sit within five of her. Henseleit, Li, Thitikul, Kuwaki and Ryu are all bunched at 4-under. Lottie Woad is a shot behind that group and Charley Hull is another shot back, with a home crowd that would very much like a repeat of 2018, when Georgia Hall won here with her dad on the bag.

Normally a three-shot 54-hole lead in a major is comfortable. Here I'm not sure it is, and it's for a slightly counterintuitive reason. When only nine players break par, the field doesn't run away from you but you can't run away from them either. Nobody's shooting 64 to win. What gets you the trophy is not making the mistakes everybody else is going to make, which puts a completely different kind of pressure on the person in front.

Ayaka Furue did shoot 65 on Saturday, so it wasn't impossible. It was just very unlikely, and that's the point.

Sunday's forecast is mild. Partly cloudy, wind at 5 to 10, twenty degrees. Royal Lytham with the wind off is a different proposition to Royal Lytham with the wind up, and if it stays calm, someone from that group of five is going to make a run at her.

What a normal golfer can take from this

I keep coming back to the 26 putts.

Not because putting is the secret, but because of what a low putt count actually means on a day like that. It doesn't mean she holed everything from twenty feet. On a links course in a major with a hard setup, 26 putts usually means she kept the ball below the hole, hit greens in the fat parts, and gave herself a lot of nothing putts to pick up. Boring golf. Boring golf on a hard day is how you end up three clear of five people who are all better ranked than you.

The other thing, and this is the one I'd actually take to my own course. Ryu made five bogeys in eight holes. World No. 3. Back-to-back major winner. Five in eight.

When you go through a stretch like that you assume it's a you problem, some fundamental flaw in your swing or your character that everybody else has solved. It isn't. It happens to the third-best player in the world in the middle of trying to win her third consecutive major. The difference between her and you is that she'll shake it off by Sunday morning, and you'll still be thinking about it at work on Tuesday.

FAQ

Who leads the 2026 AIG Women's Open?
Yealimi Noh, at 7-under 206 through 54 holes, three clear of Esther Henseleit, Lucy Li, Jeeno Thitikul, Shiho Kuwaki and Haeran Ryu at 4-under.

Has Yealimi Noh won a major?
No. She has one LPGA Tour win, the Founders Cup in February 2025, where she beat Jin Young Ko by four. A win at Royal Lytham would be her first major and would make her the first American to have the Women's Open as her maiden major since Mo Martin in 2014.

What happened to Haeran Ryu on Saturday?
She led by four during the round, then made five bogeys in an eight-hole stretch and six in total for a 3-over 74. She's the world No. 3 and had won the previous two majors of 2026.

How hard was Royal Lytham in the third round?
Only nine players broke par. Ayaka Furue's 65 was the best score of the day. For context, Jeeno Thitikul opened the championship with a record 64 on Thursday.

Who won the last Women's Open at Royal Lytham?
Georgia Hall, in 2018, with a closing five-under round to beat Pornanong Phatlum by two. Her father Wayne caddied for her.

GOLFRAW's Take

There's a habit in golf coverage of deciding, retroactively, that whoever wins was always going to. Watch what happens if Noh closes this out. The Solheim Cup pick, the amateur record, the Founders Cup, all of it gets reassembled into a story about a talent finally arriving on schedule. And if she shoots 75 tomorrow, the same facts become a story about a player who couldn't handle the moment. Same player. Same three-shot lead. Same 26 putts on Saturday. The only variable is eighteen holes that haven't been played yet, and the honest thing to say right now is that she played better than anyone else for three days on a golf course that was trying very hard to stop her, and whatever happens Sunday doesn't retroactively change that.
