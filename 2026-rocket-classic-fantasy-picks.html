2026 Rocket Classic fantasy picks: Who fits the new Detroit Golf Club?

BY GOLFRAW Editorial PUBLISHED TUE 28 JUL 2026

The best core for the 2026 Rocket Classic is Si Woo Kim, Cameron Young and Chris Gotterup—but the right captain depends on your fantasy position.

Si Woo offers the strongest combination of current ball-striking and leverage. Young is the safer defensive captain because he is the betting favorite and the most popular selection. Gotterup supplies tournament-winning upside without requiring faith in outdated course history.

That last point matters. Detroit Golf Club is not presenting the same test it did when Aldrich Potgieter won in 2025.

Why old Detroit Golf Club results need a discount

Detroit Golf Club approved a $16 million restoration plan in 2024, led by architect Tyler Rae and intended to recover more of Donald Ross’s original design. Work began after the 2025 tournament and was completed for the 2026 edition, according to the club’s project history.

The changes are substantial:

  • The tournament course has moved from par 72 to par 70.
  • The former par-5 seventh and 17th holes are now par 4s measuring as much as 505 and 537 yards.
  • Only two par 5s remain.
  • The pond in front of the 14th green—the course’s only water hazard—was replaced with native grass.
  • All greens were restored toward their original Donald Ross shapes.
  • The bunker count increased from 84 to 91.
  • Greens and bunkers were elevated or reshaped, while drainage and irrigation were modernized.

The PGA Tour’s official course preview lists the new configuration at 7,328 yards. Rob Bolton’s Power Rankings add that the restored bentgrass greens are expected to run at roughly 12½ feet on the Stimpmeter.

Past results are therefore not useless, but they no longer describe the exact course being played. Familiar sightlines and comfort in Detroit may help, yet previous winning scores and “horses for courses” models deserve less weight than usual.

The statistics that should matter more

For this particular week, prioritize:

  • Current strokes gained on approach and tee to green.
  • Par-4 scoring, especially on long par 4s.
  • Driving that combines usable distance with control.
  • Adaptability on unfamiliar, reshaped greens.
  • Recent form rather than results accumulated on the pre-renovation course.
  • Remaining player starts in season-long PGA Tour Fantasy Golf.

Length still matters on a 7,328-yard par 70. But the restored bunkering should make indiscriminate power less attractive than power combined with competent approach play.

Best 2026 Rocket Classic fantasy picks

1. Si Woo Kim: Best standard captain

Si Woo Kim is the best captain for a balanced lineup or for a manager trying to gain ground.

Kim enters Detroit ranked third on Tour in Strokes Gained: Approach and fifth in Strokes Gained: Total. He has recorded 10 top-10 finishes in 2026—one fewer than Scottie Scheffler—and briefly held the Sunday lead at The Open before finishing tied for sixth.

Those numbers come from the PGA Tour’s current Rocket Classic odds analysis, which listed Kim around +2000.

Why he fits:

  • Elite current approach play
  • Proven scoring across different course types
  • Strong recent form
  • Less dependent on raw distance than several rivals
  • A plausible ownership alternative to Cameron Young

The risk is conversion. Kim has repeatedly contended without winning this season, and his most recent Tour victory remains the 2023 Sony Open. Strong underlying performance establishes opportunity, not certainty.

Verdict: Captain Kim when you want evidence-backed upside without following the most popular play.

2. Cameron Young: Safest defensive anchor

Cameron Young is the strongest choice for managers protecting a lead.

Young has won THE PLAYERS Championship and the Cadillac Championship in 2026. He then closed with a 64 to finish second at The Open. He also ranks fourth in par-4 scoring, a relevant strength now that Detroit has converted two par 5s into demanding par 4s.

Young was listed at +1200 in the PGA Tour’s July 27 market review and shortened to approximately +1000 in a July 28 FanDuel snapshot reported by CBS Sports. That movement illustrates why odds must be dated rather than presented as permanent facts.

Rob Bolton reported that Young appeared on approximately 64% of saved PGA Tour Fantasy rosters. High ownership limits his value as a differentiator, but it makes fading him dangerous for a league leader.

Verdict: Use Young as captain to defend a lead. At minimum, he belongs in the six-player roster if starts remain available.

3. Chris Gotterup: Best ceiling play

Gotterup has won three times in 2026 and arrives fifth in the FedExCup standings. His current PGA Tour profile places him ninth in Strokes Gained: Total and sixth in driving distance.

He has also made the cut in 18 of 19 starts this season. That consistency challenges the idea that he is merely a volatile power player.

The official odds overview listed Gotterup at +1800 and noted that he has made the weekend in all three previous Detroit appearances. His best result was only tied for 26th, but the course has changed enough that the modest record should not override his current form.

Verdict: Gotterup is a strong starter and an aggressive captain alternative for managers chasing points.

4. Russell Henley: Best floor play

Henley brings a different profile: fewer spectacular power numbers, but exceptional control.

He entered the week first on Tour in fairways hit and had recently closed 65-64 for a tied-12th finish at the Travelers Championship before shooting 68-66 on the weekend to finish tied for ninth at The Open.

Henley’s accuracy should help him manage the restored bunker positions and unfamiliar green targets. His main question is whether the course still gives more aggressive drivers enough room to overwhelm precision.

Verdict: A dependable fourth starter who balances a roster built around Young, Kim and Gotterup.

5. Jake Knapp: High-variance tournament play

Knapp’s season was interrupted by a thumb problem and a run of withdrawals and missed cuts, but his tied-13th finish at the 3M Open offered evidence that his ball-striking is returning.

His combination of speed and birdie-making upside suits a tournament that should remain scoreable despite the restoration. The uncertainty surrounding his health and recent consistency makes him better suited to a bench or chasing lineup than a conservative captain role.

Verdict: Use Knapp when ceiling matters more than predictability.

6. Hideki Matsuyama: Strong bench insurance

Matsuyama tied for third at the 3M Open, where he led the field in Strokes Gained: Around the Green and played the par 4s in 12 under.

That short-game strength could become valuable if players repeatedly miss the correct sections of Detroit’s reshaped greens. He offers a stable bench option who can be promoted after the opening round if the renovation proves more demanding than expected.

Verdict: A high-quality sixth selection, especially for managers who plan to manage their starters between rounds.

What should fantasy managers do with Jackson Koivun?

Koivun is the week’s hardest decision.

The 21-year-old won the 3M Open in his third start as a professional, finishing at 25 under and moving to No. 70 in the FedExCup standings. He led that field in Strokes Gained: Approach and proximity while ranking third in both Strokes Gained: Tee to Green and Putting. His victory and updated results are recorded on his official PGA Tour profile.

Bolton reported that Koivun had already reached approximately 37% ownership for Detroit.

The positive case is clear: his game appears complete enough to travel, and every player is effectively learning the restored course together.

The counterargument is sample size. One breakthrough win does not establish a stable Tour baseline, and his rising ownership means managers are paying for last week’s result. Back-to-back contention also creates a physical and emotional test that is difficult to quantify.

Verdict: Koivun is a defensible high-upside selection, not an automatic one. Managers protecting a lead can include him to neutralize ownership. Managers chasing may benefit from using a less popular alternative such as Knapp.

Recommended PGA Tour Fantasy lineup

PGA Tour Fantasy requires six selections: four starters and two bench players. The captain’s fantasy points are doubled. The official 2026 game guide also allows in-tournament roster management, making the bench more than a list of emergency replacements.

Best balanced lineup

Starters:

  • Si Woo Kim — Captain
  • Cameron Young
  • Chris Gotterup
  • Russell Henley

Bench:

  • Jake Knapp
  • Hideki Matsuyama

This assumes the manager has enough starts remaining for Young and Gotterup.

If protecting a league lead:

  • Make Cameron Young captain.
  • Keep Kim and Gotterup active.
  • Consider replacing Knapp with Koivun to neutralize his substantial ownership.
  • Avoid unnecessary exposure to struggling stars simply because of name recognition.

If chasing a league leader:

  • Captain Kim or Gotterup.
  • Retain Knapp’s volatility.
  • Consider omitting Koivun if his ownership remains high.
  • Be willing to promote an aggressive bench player after seeing how the restored course behaves.

Bolton’s published lineup differs: Henley, Kim, Knapp and Matsuyama starting, with Gotterup and Young on the bench. His Fantasy Insider article makes clear that this arrangement was shaped by his remaining-start inventory. It should not be copied without checking your own roster limits.

Players who require caution

Xander Schauffele

Schauffele remains one of the field’s best players, so “caution” does not mean he is likely to fail. The fantasy argument against using him is strategic: recent results have been below his usual standard, and he has historically delivered considerable value during the FedExCup Playoffs.

Managers with limited Schauffele starts may obtain more leverage by saving him.

Patrick Cantlay

Cantlay has slipped to 43rd in the FedExCup and has struggled to convert solid driving into complete results. Like Schauffele, his historical playoff performance creates a legitimate start-management reason to wait.

Aldrich Potgieter

The defending champion was on only about 3.4% of saved rosters and was priced near +10000 in the July 27 market.

That makes him an intriguing contrarian selection, but he is not defending on the same course that he conquered in 2025. His title demonstrates that he can win at this level; it does not prove that the restored layout is an identical fit.

Brooks Koepka

Koepka’s tee-to-green ability provides theoretical upside, but inconsistent putting and limited recent contention make him difficult to trust in a six-player format with several stronger alternatives.

Weather could influence the opening round

The PGA Tour’s July 27 forecast called for hot conditions early Thursday, followed by a front with possible rain or thunderstorms and cooler temperatures. Winds could increase ahead of the front but were not expected to become extreme.

The timing remained uncertain at publication. Fantasy managers should therefore avoid committing to an assumed tee-time advantage several days in advance. Recheck the forecast and any official delays shortly before lineups lock.

Why the FedExCup position matters

The Rocket Classic is the penultimate event of the 2026 regular season. After the Wyndham Championship, only the top 70 players advance to the FedEx St. Jude Championship, followed by fields of 50 at the BMW Championship and 30 at the Tour Championship. The official FedExCup overview confirms that structure.

Koivun entered Detroit directly on the No. 70 boundary. Other players near the cut line also face obvious competitive pressure.

That context matters, but motivation should not be mistaken for predictive evidence. A player needing points does not automatically become more likely to play well.

FAQ

Who is the 2026 Rocket Classic favorite?

Cameron Young was the FanDuel favorite at approximately +1000 on July 28. Odds are time-sensitive and can vary by sportsbook.

Who is the best fantasy captain?

Si Woo Kim is the strongest standard captain based on current approach play, total performance and leverage. Cameron Young is the safer captain for managers protecting a lead.

Does Detroit Golf Club course history still matter?

It matters less than in previous years. The par change, restored greens, altered bunkering and removal of the 14th-hole pond create a materially different test. Current form should receive more weight.

Is Jackson Koivun a must-play?

No. Koivun’s 3M Open victory makes him a legitimate selection, but his professional sample remains small and his fantasy ownership has risen quickly.

Who won the 2025 Rocket Classic?

Aldrich Potgieter defeated Max Greyserman and Chris Kirk in a five-hole playoff after all three finished 22 under.

Is this definitely the final PGA Tour tournament in Detroit?

It is the final Rocket Classic under the current sponsorship agreement. Rocket declined its option for 2027. However, the PGA Tour said it remains interested in Detroit and will explore replacement-sponsor options, so the future of Tour golf in the market is uncertain rather than definitively over. Golf Channel reported the Tour’s statement on June 9.

GOLFRAW's Take

The renovation makes this a current-form tournament, not a course-history contest. Si Woo Kim is the best standard captain. Cameron Young is the safest defensive anchor. Chris Gotterup supplies the most compelling blend of power, form and winning upside. Henley provides stability, while Knapp and Matsuyama give managers two different bench paths. The honest uncertainty is part of the strategy: no analyst has competitive data from this version of Detroit Golf Club. The strongest lineup is therefore one built to adapt after Thursday—not one pretending the renovated course is already fully understood.
