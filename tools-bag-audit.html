02 · How The Audit Works

Fourteen slots, and no sentimentality

Most golfers do not choose fourteen clubs. They accumulate them. A set gets bought, a driver gets upgraded, a hybrid arrives at Christmas, and the club that got replaced never quite leaves the bag. This is the audit nobody performs on themselves.

Dead wood score

Uses per round multiplied by trust out of five. A driver you hit twelve times and rate 4 scores 48. A 3-wood you hit once and rate 2 scores 2. Anything scoring 4 or less is a passenger — and a club you never take out at all is not a club, it is luggage. It is deliberately crude, because the honest version of this question only needs two inputs.

Gapping

Your clubs get sorted longest to shortest and the distance between each pair is measured. Over 20 yards (about 18 metres) is a hole you have no club for. Under 8 yards (about 7 metres) is two clubs doing one job, which means one of your fourteen slots is being wasted on a spare. Between 10 and 15 yards is where you want to live.

Enter carries in yards or metres. The audit converts metres to yards before it measures, so the thresholds mean the same distance either way: 20 yards stays 20 yards, not 20 metres.

Why the gaps matter more than the gear

A 20-yard hole in your set does not show up as a bad shot. It shows up as a decision you cannot make properly — a club you have to hit soft or force, several times a round, every round. That costs more over a season than whichever driver you are being told to buy next. If you are still building the set, the starter bag guide is a useful place to begin. Before replacing an older club, check the club lifespan guide and make sure the problem is wear rather than a gap.

The honest limitation

This audit only knows what you tell it, and golfers are famously generous about their own carry distances. If your numbers come from the one you caught flush downwind, the ladder will look tidier than your bag actually is. Use carry, not total, and use the number you hit on a normal day.

FAQ · Questions Golfers Actually Ask

What is a good distance gap between golf clubs?

Between 10 and 15 yards (about 9 to 14 metres) is the target through the set. Anything over 20 yards (about 18 metres) leaves a distance you have no club for, and anything under 8 yards (about 7 metres) means you are carrying two clubs that do the same job and wasting one of your fourteen slots.

How many clubs are you allowed in a golf bag?

Fourteen, and the putter counts as one of them. Under Rule 4.1b carrying more costs two strokes for each hole where the breach happened, capped at four strokes per round.

Should I replace my 3-wood with a 7-wood?

If you rarely use the 3-wood and do not trust it, yes. A 3-wood needs a tight lie and high clubhead speed to work. Higher-lofted woods and hybrids launch easier from more lies, which is why a club you actually hit is worth more than a club that goes further on paper.

How do I know which golf club to take out of my bag?

Multiply how many times you use a club in a round by how much you trust it out of five. The lowest scores are your passengers. A club you hit once a round and do not trust is occupying a slot that a wedge or a hybrid would earn.
