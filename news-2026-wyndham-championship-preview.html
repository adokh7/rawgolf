Wyndham Championship 2026: Betting Tips, Fantasy Picks and FedExCup Playoff Stakes at Sedgefield

Cameron Young defends at Sedgefield as careers go on the line. Here are the odds, the key stats, and everything you need to know before Thursday.

BY GOLFRAW EDITORIAL PUBLISHED TUE 04 AUG 2026

The PGA Tour's regular season ends this week at the Wyndham Championship, and for dozens of players, it's not just about winning a tournament — it's about keeping their season alive.

Sedgefield Country Club in Greensboro, North Carolina hosts the final event before the FedExCup Playoffs begin. The top 70 in the season-long standings after Sunday will advance to the FedEx St. Jude Championship in Memphis. For everyone else, the offseason starts early.

With five high-profile withdrawals — including Patrick Cantlay, who pulled out on Monday — the field of 147 has been shaken up. But the stakes remain the same: win, and you're guaranteed a playoff spot. Finish on the wrong side of the bubble, and you're watching from home.

Here's everything you need to know about the 2026 Wyndham Championship.

The Stakes: Why This Week Matters More Than Most

The Wyndham Championship is the PGA Tour's last-chance saloon. The top 70 players in FedExCup points advance to the playoffs. The top 50 after Memphis move on to the BMW Championship. The top 30 after that reach the Tour Championship at East Lake.

As of Monday, August 3, Jackson Koivun — the rookie who won the 3M Open just one month after turning professional — sits at No. 70 on the bubble. Every player from Nick Taylor (61st) to Rico Hoey (112th) is committed to playing, meaning the fight for those final spots will be intense.

"It's going to be tense," said one player on the bubble. "You can't fake it this week. You either play well enough or you go home."

Sedgefield Country Club: Course Guide

Sedgefield Country Club is a Donald Ross classic that has hosted the Wyndham Championship since 2008. The course was originally designed in 1926 and restored in 2007, bringing back Ross's signature small, undulating greens and strategic bunkering.

Sedgefield is not a bomber's paradise. At just over 7,100 yards, it's one of the shorter courses on Tour, but the small greens and tricky pin positions reward precision over power. Driving accuracy and iron play are the key stats, with players needing to find the right sections of the fairway to attack pins.

"It's a course where you have to think your way around," said 2024 champion Aaron Rai. "You can't just stand up and blast it. The greens are small, the run-offs are severe, and if you're not in the right spot, you're scrambling for par."

Cameron Young: Defending Champion and Red-Hot Favorite

Cameron Young arrives at Sedgefield as the man to beat — and for good reason.

The 28-year-old American won his first PGA Tour title at this event last year, demolishing the field by six shots with a tournament-record-tying 22-under par. He opened with rounds of 63-62 and never looked back.

But Young's 2026 season has been even better. He won The Players Championship in March — his second career title — and added the Cadillac Championship in May. He reached a career-high world ranking of No. 3 in March and currently sits third in the OWGR.

Young's recent form is scorching: 61 in the second round of the Rocket Classic last week, followed by a runner-up finish at The Open Championship. He's the clear favorite at +820, the shortest price of any player this week.

"He's playing the best golf of his life," said one analyst. "And he's back at the course where he broke through. That's a dangerous combination."

The Contenders: Who Else Can Win at Sedgefield?

While Young is the headline act, several other players have the game to challenge.

Sungjae Im has been tipped by PGA Tour fantasy expert Rob Bolton as a strong play. The South Korean has three top-10 finishes in 2026 and ranks 58th in the FedExCup standings — safely inside the top 70 but looking to improve his position. Im's game suits Sedgefield: he's accurate off the tee, excellent with his irons, and has a strong record on Donald Ross designs.

Tom Kim ended a 1,001-day winless drought at the Genesis Scottish Open in July, his fourth PGA Tour title. The 24-year-old has been inconsistent since — missing the cut at the 3M Open and finishing T41 at the Rocket Classic — but his talent is undeniable. He's a former winner at Sedgefield (2022) and knows how to handle the pressure of the Wyndham.

Jackson Koivun is the story of the season. The 21-year-old rookie won the 3M Open in just his third professional start, holding off world No. 1 Scottie Scheffler with a final-round 66. He's +2200 this week and has the confidence to contend again.

Hideki Matsuyama (+2200) and Ben Griffin (+2500) are also in the mix, with Griffin coming off a strong showing at the Rocket Classic.

Justin Thomas and Jordan Spieth — two players who need a big week to secure their playoff spots — are both in the field and will attract plenty of attention.

FedExCup Playoff Bubble: Who Needs What

The drama at Sedgefield isn't just about the winner. For players outside the top 70, this is their last chance to make the playoffs.

Key players on the bubble:

  • Jackson Koivun (No. 70): The rookie needs a solid week to hold his spot. A top-20 finish should be enough.
  • Nick Taylor (No. 61): Should be safe with a made cut, but nothing is guaranteed.
  • Jordan Spieth (No. 52): Needs a top-20 to feel comfortable.
  • Justin Thomas (No. 48): A top-10 would all but secure his spot.
  • Rico Hoey (No. 112): Needs a win or a top-3 to have any chance.

The PGA Tour has published a full breakdown of what each player needs to qualify. For many, the equation is simple: win, and you're in. For others, a top-10 or top-20 finish could be enough depending on how the players around them perform.

Betting Tips and Fantasy Picks

  • Outright winner: Cameron Young (+820) is the clear favorite and hard to argue against. But for value, Sungjae Im (+1800) offers a better price with genuine winning chances.
  • Each-way value: Ben Griffin (+2500) has been in strong form and has the game for Sedgefield. Hideki Matsuyama (+2200) is always dangerous when he finds his putting stroke.
  • Sleeper pick: Davis Thompson (+4000) has shown flashes of brilliance this season and could be a surprise package.
  • Fantasy must-start: Cameron Young. He's the most in-form player in the field and has proven he can dominate at Sedgefield.
  • Fantasy value play: Sungjae Im. Bolton's analysis suggests he's being undervalued by the market.
  • One and done: If you're playing a one-and-done league, this is the week to use Cameron Young if you still have him available.

Course Stats and Key Metrics

The most important stats at Sedgefield over the past five years:

  • Strokes Gained: Approach — The single biggest predictor of success. Small greens demand precision.
  • Driving Accuracy — Finding fairways is more important than distance.
  • Par 4 Scoring — With only two par 5s, players need to make their birdies on the par 4s.
  • Scrambling — The run-off areas around greens are treacherous. Getting up and down is essential.
  • Putting: Bermuda — The greens are pure Bermuda grass, which can be grainy and tricky to read.

Weather Forecast

The forecast for Greensboro this week calls for typical August conditions: hot and humid with temperatures in the high 80s to low 90s Fahrenheit. There's a chance of afternoon thunderstorms, particularly on Friday and Saturday, which could soften the course and lead to lower scoring.

Wind is expected to be light, which should favor the accurate players rather than the bombers.

The Bottom Line

The Wyndham Championship might not have the prestige of a major, but for pure drama, it's hard to beat. Careers are on the line. Playoff dreams are made and broken. And at Sedgefield, a course that rewards precision and punishes waywardness, anything can happen.

Cameron Young is the man to beat. But with the FedExCup bubble tighter than ever, don't be surprised if a desperate player produces the week of their life.
