Two Generations Just Said the Same Thing About Jackson Koivun

Schauffele called him a world-beater in Detroit on Tuesday. Scheffler had said something almost identical two days earlier. Nobody's put them together.

BY GOLFRAW EDITORIAL PUBLISHED: WED 29 JUL 2026
The Quick Verdict

Speaking to media in Detroit on Tuesday, ahead of this week's Rocket Classic, Xander Schauffele said Jackson Koivun is "obviously a world-beater," that the young players now coming out of college are not afraid at all and look like mini Tour pros, and that when he was in college it did not look like that.

His closing line: "It makes me want to work even harder, so congrats to him."

That's a compliment rather than a confession, whatever the headlines say. The more interesting thing is what Scottie Scheffler had said two days earlier.

The same observation, ten years apart

Here's Schauffele, a 10-time PGA TOUR winner with two majors, who came out of San Diego State in the mid-2010s: the kids arriving now aren't afraid, they look like miniature Tour professionals, and college golf didn't look remotely like that when he was in it.

And here's Scheffler, on Sunday evening at TPC Twin Cities, having just been beaten by three shots: players coming out of college now are really, really prepared — even more prepared than we were coming out.

Two players, from either side of about a decade, asked different questions in different states, making exactly the same point without either knowing what the other said.

Nobody has run those two quotes together, and they're the story. When one Tour veteran says the college game has changed, it's a nice line. When two of them say it independently within 48 hours, one of them the world No. 1 and one of them a two-time major champion, it starts to look like a description of something real.

Worth saying plainly, though: it's still testimony, not measurement. Neither has produced data showing college golfers arrive better prepared than they used to, and I couldn't find anyone who has. It's two very well-placed people telling you what they see.

What Schauffele actually said

He'd met Koivun once before, in passing — Koivun was out playing a casual round with, in Schauffele's phrasing, "JT and a few guys," and Schauffele ran into him. He said Koivun was polite. He said he'd watched him on TV.

Then the substance: that what the win showed was that these players have already won in college, and they arrive without fear. That it's good for the game and good for the Tour to have as many Jackson-like players as it can get.

And the sign-off about working harder.

On the headline framing — you'll see this reported as Schauffele "admitting what he wants to do now." He didn't admit anything. He congratulated a 21-year-old and said the standard rising motivates him. That's a decent line from a man who's won two majors, and it doesn't need selling as a revelation.

The bit that makes it land

Here's some context the reaction pieces have skipped, and it sharpens the quote considerably.

On the same day Schauffele said Koivun makes him want to work harder, the PGA TOUR's own fantasy analyst was advising readers to avoid Schauffele this week — noting he has been misfiring of late, that he sits 15th in the FedExCup, and that he's better saved for the Playoffs.

So the man being gracious about the new arrival is, by the Tour's own assessment, in a form dip himself, in the penultimate week of the regular season. That doesn't make the compliment less genuine. It arguably makes it more so, and it certainly gives "makes me want to work even harder" a slightly different weight.

The claim in the coverage that isn't sourced

Two of the pieces reporting Schauffele's remarks also tell you that Koivun is bound for the US Presidents Cup team — one saying it would be a surprise if Brandt Snedeker didn't pick him in September, another that Koivun has "been urged" to be selected so he can prepare for the Ryder Cup.

Neither names a single person doing the tipping or the urging. No captain, no assistant, no player, no reporter. It's written in the grammar of reporting and it's opinion.

It might well happen. Koivun has won on Tour and sits inside the world's top 75. But nobody with any say in the matter has said anything on the record that I could find, and Snedeker himself hasn't been quoted anywhere.

And one that's simply wrong

One outlet covering this describes Justin Thomas as a "16-time major winner."

No golfer has won 16 majors. The all-time record is 18. The figure almost certainly comes from conflating Thomas's career PGA TOUR victories with his major victories, which is an easy slip and a very visible one.

I'm not going to give you the correct number, because none of the sources in this research state it and I'm not going to assert a figure I haven't checked. But 16 majors isn't it, and if you see it repeated, that's where it came from.

Where Koivun actually stands

After the win he moved to a career-high 73rd in the Official World Golf Ranking and 70th in the FedExCup — which, as things stand, is the last qualifying place for the Playoffs.

He'll defend that position over two weeks. It is the single most volatile number in this article, and it may well have moved by the time you read it.

The details the reaction pieces missed

Buried in the Sunday coverage, and much better than any of the quotes above:

Auburn coach Nick Clinard describes Koivun as the most positive player he has ever coached — a man who "really expects good things to happen to him."

Before the final round, Koivun wrote three words on his glove: confidence, trust, dominate. He also told his father that morning he wished everyone would tamp the hype down a bit.

And after Koivun's tee shot on the drivable par-4 16th, Scheffler's caddie Ted Scott walked over to the 3M Open's executive director and said goodbye. Scheffler was still talking to reporters at the time, waiting to see if the kid would hold on. His caddie had already worked it out.

FAQ

What did Xander Schauffele say about Jackson Koivun?

That he's obviously a world-beater, that the young players coming out now aren't afraid, and that it makes him want to work even harder.

Where and when did he say it?

At a media session in Detroit on Tuesday, ahead of the Rocket Classic.

Did he "admit" something?

Not really. It was a compliment ending in a congratulation. "Admits" is headline packaging.

What did Scottie Scheffler say?

That the win showed both talent and mental strength, and that college players now arrive more prepared than his own generation did.

Is Koivun going to the Presidents Cup?

Unknown. The claims circulating cite nobody, and Brandt Snedeker hasn't been quoted on it anywhere I could find.

How many majors has Schauffele won?

Two, from 10 PGA TOUR titles.

Where does Koivun rank now?

73rd in the world and 70th in the FedExCup — currently the last playoff spot.

Who was Koivun playing with when Schauffele met him?

Justin Thomas, according to AOL — the same piece that gets Thomas's record badly wrong, so treat the detail as reported rather than confirmed.

GOLFRAW's Take

Press-conference reaction is usually the emptiest genre in golf writing. A guy gets asked about another guy, says something nice, twelve websites turn it into a headline with the word "admits" in it. This one's better than that, but only if you read two of them at once. Schauffele saying college didn't look like this in his day is a pleasant line. Scheffler saying the same thing forty-eight hours earlier, unprompted, having just lost to the evidence — that's two of the best players alive independently reporting that the entry level of their sport has moved. And a note on how this stuff travels. The two pieces with the "admits" headline are the same article on two different websites. One of the others contains a claim about Justin Thomas that anyone who watches golf would spot in a second. Both assert Koivun's Presidents Cup selection without a single named source. None of that is malicious — it's just what happens when a warm quote goes through the aggregation machine at speed. The best material from the whole week isn't in any of those pieces. It's a coach saying his player expects good things to happen, three words written on a glove, and a caddie who knew it was over before the man he works for did.
